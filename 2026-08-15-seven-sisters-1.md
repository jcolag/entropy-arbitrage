## What Works Well?

While the diction often feels off, I think that the writing sells a lot more of this than any songs could on their own.  I especially mean *The Angels of Aldebaran*, whose "liner notes" give us over a thousand words filling us in on the situation.

Also, even as "not a fan of the genre," I can appreciate the craft of a lot of the music.  Some of it---and I hope that you'll pardon the "old guy" stereotyping, here, and assume a deliberate choice---sounds to my ears not entirely unlike recordings of a garbage disposal with the occasional piano key struck, **but** it also has bits of complex melodies that help the story along.

And if you've ever thought that Free Culture projects don't have much ambition, we have a three-hour, multi-act, space opera musical about two women from opposite sides of the war falling in love, complete with a full transcript and additional back-story.  Even if it all fell apart---and I don't think that it will---then we'd still need to respect that success.

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

I don't understand the voicing choices.  The protagonists and primary antagonist all seem to (mostly?) speak/sing in the stereotypical heavy metal scream.  That makes it difficult to follow the lyrics---though we have the text versions to help with that---but also makes it difficult to understand whose sentiments we get out of some songs.  That seems like a problem in the prior/introductory story, especially, where you have a song each by both, and they sound near-identical, at least to my ears.  Likewise, in *Prologue*, our protagonists both have clear, separate speaking voices, but they also need to keep reminding us of their names when they sing, because otherwise I don't see how you'd know who did what in their meet-cute-fight-to-the-death.

## Six Years Hence:    Prelude to Whitespace

Again, I don't have too much to say about [*Prelude to Whitespace*]({% post_url 2020-08-15-white %}) six years after talking about it the first time.

Checking in on the [author's blog](https://valianttheywere.blogspot.com) where they posted the story, I see that they posted as recently as last year.  However, they don't have any tagging system on their posts, so I can't tell if the *Whitespace* premise continued.  And other posts don't seem to get regularly released into the public domain, making it less interesting for me to dig through six years of posts to find out.

Actually, maybe of behind-the-scenes interest, I notice that, when I wrote the original post, I still felt the need to...explain the concept of Free Culture, maybe.

> However, the author has placed the story and broader universe into the public domain, so you're also free to do with it as you please.

Looking back, it strikes me as bizarre that I had the foresight to put the *Opportunities* section in the first place---something that only feels like it makes sense in the context of getting involved with the creator's version of the project---but spent quite a while pointing out that you can totally make your own version, something inherent to the licensing, and arguably redundant with the look at adaptable aspects.  If I ever decide to go back to rewrite old posts to match modern standards, I would put fixing that sort of talk near the top of the list.
