## 5:15 PM -- Tue 11 August 2026

[<i class="fab fa-mastodon"></i> Posted to Mastodon](https://mastodon.social/@jcolag/117078943406480096)

> I assume that somebody wants to know that Folkways (the label given to the Smithsonian) streams most of their audio catalog for US/Canada monthly donors. https://folkways.si.edu/friends-of-folkways
>
> Musical about the (ahem) wonders of Reaganomics? Frogs croaking? Stage plays? Protest songs? Experimental music? Songs from pretty much any ethnicity? If I vanish for a few months, you know where I ended up...

The {% wiki Smithsonian Folkways %} story actually has a bunch of weird tendrils.  Folkways itself started as a passion project to record the sounds of the world, which largely worked by providing studio time to anybody who wanted it.  When the owner retired in 1984, he convinced the Smithsonian to take it over, including accepting the condition to keep every recording in print permanently.

Because of that, I grew up aspiring to collect their albums, because they had an absolutely wild collection, ordinary music collections to amateur poetry to field recordings of frogs.  Honestly, it disappoints me that I didn't know about the "Friends" program before now, because I absolutely would've signed up years ago.

## 9:06 -- Wed 12 August 2026

{% cw AI %}
{% embed https://storage.ghost.io/c/0f/76/0f76b548-bc58-4f25-abc3-3f5ebca07da4/content/images/size/w2000/2026/08/SAP_SAPPHIRE25ORL_4229__1_.jpg|People milling around a SAP booth at a convention|false| %}

[<i class="fab fa-mastodon"></i>](https://mastodon.social/@jcolag/117082683815707937){: aria-label="Wednesday morning's post on Mastodon with this article" }
[Software Giant SAP Stops Most Travel and Hiring Because of AI’s Soaring Cost](https://www.404media.co/software-giant-sap-stops-most-travel-and-hiring-because-of-ais-soaring-cost/) from 404 Media @404mediaco@mastodon.social

> The email highlights how companies both big and small are coming to grips with the reality of AI’s cost. Rather than being a massive cost saver, in some cases companies are throttling employees’ AI use as it spirals out of control, and scrambling to find other ways to keep costs down.

Hashtags:  #AI

Over the past couple of years, I have likened LLMs to stimulants, because you see the same sort of behavior of using it for performance, the continuing to use it past the point where performance suffers, due to the unwarranted confidence boost.  Now we see the addiction side, with companies *literally* sacrificing to keep up with the costs for something that *doesn't help them*.

How can I say so confidently that LLMs don't help them?  If it helped, then they wouldn't need to severely cut costs.  They'd already see higher revenue and/or lower costs, pretty much by definition.
