From the Colagioia Homestead, we regret to inform you...

Actually, I think that I need a new logo for this.  Hold on a second.

![A circular logo made up of twelve crescents rotating through the color wheel, with a Kilroy-like face poking through the cavity in the center](/blog/assets/iris-12-color.svg "Sphincter?  I hardly know her...")

Ah, much better.  Much more "with it."  It represents the infinite universe and escalating synergies, with a {% wiki Kilroy_was_here|Kilroy %}-inspired mascot, and...*stop laughing*.  No, that looks nothing like a puckered sphincter with something working its way out.  Get your minds out of the gutter.  How dare you suggest that the mascot might represent what typically comes out of a puckered sphincter.  The logo totally represents humans among the one-ness...not a butt[^gErTnT].

[^gErTnT]:  It definitely doesn't look like a *healthy* butt...not that we've checked a lot of them. {% emoji shifty %}

Ahem.  Now, where did I leave off?

Right.

From the Colagioia Homestead, we regret to inform you that our work this morning *escaped containment*[^urItUd].

[^urItUd]:  Look, some mornings don't go as well as others...

![A glass bottle of milk tipped over, with a puddle formed around it](/blog/assets/234631999_bc02b14977_o.png "No crying, I suppose...")

Even worse, we have evidence that somebody might use this breach in our security protocols to create biological weapons[^G1cyrQ], which we would have no way to stop.

[^G1cyrQ]:  Based on the smell, we might bear some small responsibility for not checking the use-by date before pouring the ~~milk~~ *galactic-scale* model. {% emoji milky way %}

Some might recall that, when we started out, we made the point that we'd have a functioning business as soon as we could get waivers for any relevant laws that our work might violate, our magic beans gave us access to a supply of golden geese, and our product accidentally turned itself into something extremely desirable.  We haven't made much progress on the middle one, hence adding Coalie to our Board of Directors, because...you *mine* for magic beans, right?  We didn't study that in school, for some reason.

![An anthropomorphized lump of coal in more safety gear than actual miners get, looking glassy-eyed at dishonest before- and after-pictures implying that coal mining doesn't affect the environment](/blog/assets/coalie-before-after.png "Pardon my breaking character here, but I don't even understand what this picture thinks that it wants to accomplish...")

However, we have coasted through on getting governments to ignore the law on our behalf.  And spontaneous generation of fast-spreading biological weapons?  That sounds like a viable product emerging before our eyes.

Critics might point out that sound business practice would involve rigorously investigating the breach while taking responsibility for any damage done during the incident, rather than issuing histrionic press releases about the damage that our tools can do, as if marketing a product.

...

Oh, no!  It appears that, while investigating the first incident, we overlooked another breach.  One of our agents *also* escaped containment, totally on its own[^dPPcAG].

[^dPPcAG]:  OK, sure, someone might have left the door open and left a bowl of cat food three feet out the door.  But the ~~adorable kitty~~ *extremely serious agent* worked out how to ~~walk through the door~~ *breach containment* with no direction from the experimenters.

![A kitten poking its head through an old wooden door](/blog/assets/wood-kitten-cat-mammal-black-fauna-861796-pxhere.com.png "The city needs me")

The agent figured this out entirely on ~~her~~ its own, with no instructions from our experimenters.  We will continue investigating to determine how it could have determined that it could escape.

Worse, we have discovered that, while beyond our supervision, our agent indeed caused severe damage to another site in our neighborhood, possibly unfixable damage.

![A jar of pennies spilled over](/blog/assets/jar-money-product-coin-spill-coins-1260577-pxhere.com.png "Oh, the humanity...and definitely NOT a metaphor for how AI companies manage money")

Indeed, this feels exactly like an SCP Foundation dossier, spoiled milk and clever pets going full-on Apollyon Class and breaching containment, becoming an anti-memetic something-or-other.  You couldn't make this stuff up.  Well, I guess the SCP people make this sort of stuff up all the time, hence using them as a frame of reference.

Critics might wonder how we found out about the damage done before the owners of the damaged property even got home to unlock their door, or why the kitchen table where the damage took place currently smells like sardines despite nobody living there eating sardines.  To them, we re---

Oh, no!  Several whistleblowers have gone to the press to report that our work has become so dangerous, so powerful, so *valuable*[^JSpYh2], that we might destroy the world, or at least humanity.  Their doom-saying makes us out as the irresponsible creators of powerful weapons, which we can put into anybody's hands for only twenty bucks per month, a heck of a bargain, provided that you ignore all the costs that we externalize onto the populace, the environment, the economy, and even our users.

[^JSpYh2]:  In fact, as of last month, if you politely ignore all the costs and overhead on our balance sheets, our financial statements show that we finally do nothing but make profit.

We cannot stand for this idea that some people think of us as providing danger at bargain prices.  In response, we have a two-pronged plan to deal with this problem.

First, you can depend on us to ~~destroy the life of the whistleblowers, chilling dissent against our work~~ engage directly with our critics to understand their concerns.  It especially worries us, because we thought that we recruited exclusively from transhumanists who'd happily watch humanity die, as long as we birth something new in the process.  And that doesn't even get to the screening that they go through to make sure that they believe in the Singularity, making it strange that they'd even consider some other outcome.  It seems so out of character for our former ~~cultists~~ employees, and we worry about them.  Without those pillars, you don't have TESCREAL, only ECREAL, which sounds more like a disappointing French pastry or a store-brand fruity shampoo than an ultra-manly futurist philosophy.  Who would want to live in that world?

{% cw "Yeah, sure, we fully expect and demand that kittens and sour milk will compound their accelerated development, becoming the Infinite Libertarian God that humanity needs, and supplant humanity as the dominant intellect on Earth before colonizing the universe.  But---and I hope that Korat's Basilisk has mercy on its infinite simulacra of my personality for saying this---some of us want to see how Phase Twenty-Eight of the FRMU (the Fawcett-Republic Matinée Universe -ed) flicks will pan out before *becoming* Gray Goo or paper clips or whatever in the service of evolution."|info|Statement from Whistleblower Known as "Jay Doe" Regarding Our Suggestions of Their Inadequacies %}

Second, we have already spent the past week ~~colluding with our alleged competition~~ debating the issues at a critical industry summit, coming to the conclusion that we all need to step back.  We need to take a few months or years[^gqGN1l] rolling out updates more slowly, so that we don't accidentally release lactobacillale-weilding death-kittens onto an unsuspecting population.  During this time, we would also encourage legislators to heavily regulate our industry.

[^gqGN1l]:  You can't vacation in the Maldives for longer than that and still call it a vacation, right?  After a while, they start to assume that you live there, probably.

Critics might point out that we have no evidence that any of this could ever happen.  They might also point out that our work seems to have actually plateaued a couple of years ago, so we would need to slow down rolling out updates regardless.  And they might further point out that punitive regulations would actually create a "moat" for us, rather than making our work more difficult, by making sure that well-capitalized companies can afford to comply with whatever governments throw at us, while independent developers and small companies would find themselves locked out of our ~~easiest con~~ *important work*.  Our most extreme critics might point out that, if our things keep escaping containment on our watch, then maybe we haven't learned the responsibility necessary to do any of this work, even calling us negligent, and shouldn't have permission to do that; particularly vile critics might point out that we have plenty of laws that any competent government could use to prevent these incidents from happening so often, so more regulation would only see us flaunting more regulation while agencies and courts shrug their shoulders.  And to that, we say that he who smelled it dealt it, so there.  In your face, critics.

At the Colagioia Homestead, we take these incidents extremely seriously, and believe that you should, too.  In fact, we believe that you should take the threat of the aforementioned well-armed death-kittens so seriously that you do everything that you can to spread panic.  The spoiled milk *and* the cat figured out how to escape, unlike the fancy robot vacuum or the rice cooker.  We know how to solve this problem, unlike the rest of the world:  We need to make sure that the West has the *best* murder-kittens available.  By regulating the industry, we can weed out the amateurs, making sure that they work for us instead of competing with[^ZvDWxf] and undermining us.  By accepting our slow-down as a necessity and not a hasty cover for our lack of progress, it gives us more time to figure out how to ~~unload this submarine with screen doors~~ optimize the product for safety.  And by investing in us, maybe even convincing the government to buy us out ~~so that the taxpayers can hold the bag instead of us when the market crashes~~, it ensures that the work can go on.

[^ZvDWxf]:  Speaking of which, it somehow only recently occurred to us that you can't have a copyright on kittens, meaning that people can copy our business model with little difficulty and no legal risk, so we need to find another space to stomp out competitors.

And remember, the work only needs to go on until the kitten-with-sour-milk accidentally develops a rudimentary grasp of assassination principles.  Once that happens, it should sustain the work on its own and become infinitely powerful within a few hours, maybe an afternoon.

Now, when do we get our venture capital investment?  These murder-kittens seem worth at least a hundred million as a seed round...

## Breaking Character

I won't explain this one in detail, because you can probably work it out, but yes, I needed to blow off some satirical steam, in light of the propaganda that linear algebra---the same matrix math that we teach high school kids who have too much trouble with geometry---"translating" English to Python represents an existential threat to humanity, but the people building, running, and securing (or consistently failing to secure) it, somehow they do *not* constitute a threat to anybody.

Oh, right.  Maybe someone needs to know that {% wiki Korat %} borrows the name of a fancy cat breed.  Someday, I'll get around to a post on (what I call) the Fawcett-Republic Matinée Universe that never quite materialized.  For now, I'll leave everybody to investigate and see how close we nearly got in the 1940s...

And for those of you who don't already have some familiarity with the [SCP Foundation](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/)---think (mostly) the paperwork behind a Free Culture version of **The X-Files**---give it a shot.  The "Apollyon" jargon refers to items/entities too difficult to isolate but presenting an imminent existential threat.
