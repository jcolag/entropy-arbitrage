## Sewing

As something of a headline, my neck pillow from last week now has its pillowcase.

![The terrifying orange pillow from last week, now inside its new beige pillowcase, muting the color](/blog/assets/neck-pillow-with-case.png "I told you that the original color wouldn't matter much.")

An older set of bed linens has become pretty close to garbage---some tears in the fitted sheet and a missing pillowcase---so the undamaged parts will get cycled into the fabric pile.  In this case, I disassembled the surviving pillowcase into two sheets, folded one to fit the pillow with a little extra length for the already-finished end, and stitched the rest.

You can see that the corner didn't quite match up (the little triangle sticking out of the lower-left), possibly for a few reasons, such as the bobbin thread getting jammed up and trying to suck the fabric under the machine.  I'd still call it close enough for only me.

I also printed out the pattern for [Waralee wrap pants](https://freesewing.eu/designs/waralee/) and started cutting the pieces.  If it comes together and fits, they'll probably make decent pajama pants.  In doing so, I noticed a clever aspect of Free Sewing:  When you have a setup like this, where the pattern consists primarily of rectangles (and two huge rectangles with a scoop taken out of each), you can turn off the boring bits, confident that measuring will do everything that a strip of paper would.

## Recipes

This might warrant a (partial) picture, and not only because I have started upgrading my jar collection from old glass peanut butter jars.

![A quart jar filled with an orange-colored paste](/blog/assets/sweet-potato-butter.png "Yeah, more orange.  Blame it on the time of year, I guess.")

I went a bit overboard, because I *also* have two of the sixteen-ounce peanut butter jars full.

{% cook 1|Sweet Potato Butter %}
Bake @sweet potatoes{6}.  Add them to a food processor with @pecans{¼%cup}, @cinnamon{1%Tbsp}, @nutmeg{½%tsp}, @grated ginger{½%tsp}, and @molasses{½%tsp}, puréeing it all into a thick paste.
{% endcook %}

The rice cooker inexplicably also has a slow cooker mode, so I actually "baked" the sweet potatoes overnight that way.  The whole sweet potatoes went into the purée, skins and all.

If you search around, you'll notice that standard recipes seem to want treacle, adding and cooking off apple juice or orange juice, or adding large amounts sugar, I guess not trusting that sweet potatoes already have a sweetness to them.  That doesn't work for me, so mine got a touch of molasses, which I added more for the sharp flavor than the sweetening.

The result worked well in grits and oatmeal.  As the name suggests, it also made a good spread for bread with dinner, and I haven't gotten tired of it, yet.

How does this differ from mashed sweet potatoes and only treating it like a nut butter?  Well, I would never make mashed potatoes by puréeing them, for starters, and certainly wouldn't risk any visitors' allergies by making nuts less visible in something like a side dish.  Otherwise, I couldn't tell you, because this otherwise doesn't look too different from how I have prepared sweet potatoes.  I definitely like it, though.

When I do this again, I won't use more than two sweet potatoes, will reduce the other ingredients accordingly, and might actually want something more sour, like a teaspoon of lemon juice.

Oh, and if I don't get to this large jar within a few days, since I have it in a mason jar with one of those ring-and-insert lids, I may haul out my old stockpot and try canning.  We'll see how the week goes.

## Clearer Licensing

This changes the format a bit, because I largely did the same work across a bunch of repositories.  But I don't want to get ahead of myself, so let me explain.

Last week, David Revoy announced that, at least as a moral stance, he placed a formal [ban on AI derivations of his work](https://www.davidrevoy.com/article1178/license-update-ai-derivation-prohibited-on-all-my-art-lore-stories-and-comics).  He acknowledges that he does that more out of signaling desire than trying to alter the license arrangement, and I see that as a positive step forward.  In a similar move, Christine Lemmer-Webber announced that the [Spritely Institute explicitly does not waive attribution requirements](https://social.coop/@cwebber/117440912762080232) in their licenses.  For example, in the [AGPL v3.0 §7](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html#section7)(b), it says that creators may optionally require (among other things) the following.

> Requiring preservation of specified reasonable legal notices or author attributions in that material or in the Appropriate Legal Notices displayed by works containing it.

Likewise, [Section 4](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html#section4) starts by saying this.

> You may convey verbatim copies of the Program's source code as you receive it, in any medium, provided that you conspicuously and appropriately publish on each copy an appropriate copyright notice; keep intact all notices stating that this License and any non-permissive terms added in accord with section 7 apply to the code; keep intact all notices of the absence of any warranty; and give all recipients a copy of this License along with the Program.

Similarly, Creative Commons licenses bake attribution into the *names* of the licenses, so that people don't forget it.

In this spirit, I have started by (in particular) taking Section 4 of the AGPL at its word, and making sure that files have "an appropriate copyright notice" to propagate with any derivative works.  And I realized that I already *sometimes* use a standard for exactly this, [REUSE](https://reuse.software/).

Therefore, I spent the week making some projects more REUSE-compliant.

- The [blog's code](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/entropy-arbitrage-code)
- The [Ham Newsletter Generator](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/ham-newsletter)
- [Kabang!](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/kabang)
- My [Mastodon Tool Trunk](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/tool-trunk)
- [Notoboto](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/Notoboto)
- [Scrawls](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/scrawls)
- [**Seeking Refuge**](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/silver-bat-01-seeking-refuge)
- My ["Thorough" JSON Resume Theme](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/jsonresume-theme-thorough)

Now, I believe that our actual [path out of AI-enabled abuse involves organizing]({% post_url 2026-09-13-what-next %}), as I started writing about a few weeks ago.  Collective action can force increasingly large organizations to put pressure on their organizations, whereas individual actions won't amount to anything, except among the ultra-wealthy, because libertarian "might makes right" rules apply.  However, until that organizing begins, explicitly asserting attribution rights does at least provide a moral place to stand.
