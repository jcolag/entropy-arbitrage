## Armageddon Game

For our usual context, this episode lands between [*Homeward*]({% post_url 2025-08-21-homeward %}) and [*Sub Rosa*]({% post_url 2025-08-28-sub-rosa %}).  And for those who find that juxtaposition interesting, over at the competition, someone might want to know that the episode also landed between **Babylon 5**'s *Midnight on the Firing Line* and *Soul Hunter*, as they kicked off; as I mentioned in the post for [*The Homecoming*]({% post_url 2026-07-23-homecoming %}), I grabbed the episode information for that franchise from Wikidata alongside the *Star Trek* information.

As for the episode itself, the writers apparently decided that we all wanted to spend more time with the show's most abrasive characters, for some reason...

> **NYDROM**: Coming up to full power.

We won't have any use for him, but if the scientist looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Peter_White_(actor)|Peter White %}, probably best known for his big break in the Broadway and film versions of **The Boys in the Band**.

> **O'BRIEN**: Doctor, start the sequence, or we will be here a month.

Thank goodness we brought Miles along to swill coffee---and yes, I realize that this will become a key plot point---and snipe at people.  Wouldn't an engineer have *something* to contribute, here, other than complaints?

> **SHARAT**: But eliminating the harvesters is only half the task. We must make certain all scientific data concerned with them is wiped out as well.

Not really relevant to our project, here, but this policy *never* works.  If someone developed something once, others can develop it again.  And "all scientific data" necessarily includes what they did here to neutralize it, leaving them vulnerable.

Oh, and if Thing #1 looks familiar to you, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Larry Cedar %}, now known mostly for his stint on **Deadwood** as Leon, and we'll see him again on **Voyager** and not see him on **Enterprise**, not to mention a **Stargate SG-1** and (original) **Battlestar Galactica** appearance each, but *I* recognize him as one of the main cast from **Square One Television**, an attempt to create something like **The Electric Company**, but for math.  (I direct younger readers to find the nearest adult in their fifties to explain those references...)

> **BASHIR**: Yes, it's set for tonight, but the Chief is quite anxious to return back to the station.
>
> **O'BRIEN**: It's been a long week.
>
> **SISKO**: Still, I think we can spare you for another day.

Read as "by all means, keep your whinging a few million miles away from my office for a while longer"?

> **O'BRIEN**: I'm programming the system to overload thirty seconds after we transport. Let's go.

And if the system logs activity elsewhere, or if they can read through in less than thirty seconds, this helps how...?

> **E'TYSHRA**: ...Along with some of our greatest scientists.

And if Thing #2 looks familiar to you, then you might recognize her as {% wiki Darlene Carr %}, not a massive star, but definitely had a solid career before she retired, and her sisters voiced Wonder Woman for most of the run of **Super Friends** and played the eldest daughter (Liesl) in **The Sound of Music**.

> **O'BRIEN**: They could be booby-trapped. The Cardassians used to rig the supplies they left behind with pressure grenades. I've seen more than one soldier permanently lose his appetite that way.

That sounds a bit made-up.  With all the racism and propaganda that we've seen for almost seven years---at that time, four and a half years, blog-time---directed at "official enemies," if one of them *actually* did something like this, I feel like news would spread fast enough that Bashir would have heard about it by now, and probably us, too.

> **O'BRIEN**: Federation rations. I've had enough T'Lani food this past week to last me a lifetime.

Oh, O'Brien doesn't like foreign food?  Color me shocked...

> **O'BRIEN**: Wait. Listen to me, Julian. The Kelleruns are going to expect us to be on the move. If we start running, their sensors'll pick us up before we've gone a kilometer. If we stay still maybe, just maybe, it'll take them longer to find us. The way I see it, we have only one chance to survive this thing that's to stay alive long enough for Commander Sisko to find us.

Let the record show that O'Brien tried to invoke **Jurassic Park** (out more than six months when this aired) Tyrannosaurus rex rules as military protocol...

> **BASHIR**: I thought maybe I could help. I took the engineering extension courses at Starfleet Medical.

This strikes me as odd, because the term "extension course" has (at least in my experience) tended to refer to continuing education classes taken by especially non-traditional students.  That sloppy class that you took on LinkedIn because you found out that your public library subscribes to the service feels like the canonical example today, or your town's parks and recreation office offering knitting and defensive driving, but most colleges also have some program for people in the community to get started on some new skill.

By contrast, medical students already have a credit structure to work inside.  Medical schools also have electives, and while they tend to involve medical fields---because nobody goes to medical school for fun---you can almost certainly convince an advisor to allow a course or two from another department, and anybody can ask the instructor to audit a course.

In other words, I don't see Bashir doing that.

> **O'BRIEN**: Then let me do my job.

How can he do his job without somebody interrupting him?  Oh, he probably means the repairs and not the whinging.

> **KIRA**: I'm surprised the Chief didn't detect it.
>
> **DAX**: It must've been buried very deep in the system.

They seem to have a much higher opinion of O'Brien than I've developed.

That said, I do need to point out how much more grief they show in this scene than the *Enterprise*-D crew showed for the deaths of any number of people other than their bosses.

> **BASHIR**: You'll make it work, Chief. It's the kind of challenge you live for. You know, I was looking forward to the celebrations on T'Lani Prime. I don't know if you'd noticed, but T'Lani women are quite attractive.
>
> ...
>
> **O'BRIEN**: Just because you're married doesn't mean you stop looking at women.

They could take any number of opportunities to not act creepy, and yet...

> **BASHIR**: Just as long as you don't let your wife see you look. For me, tonight's celebrations would have been an adventure. The most you could have hoped for was a good meal.

Well, they both got their adventure...

> **BASHIR**: Fair to them. I mean, look at us. Our lives are constantly in danger. There's enough to worry about without worrying about the wife and kids at home worrying about us. I'm sorry, Chief. I just feel that way. A lot of career officers feel that way.

Does this explain why everybody in this version of the franchise looks at members of the opposite sex like prey?

> **BASHIR**: Nothing. Absolutely nothing. It's no secret that your assignment to this station hasn't been conducive to your marriage.

The emotional abuse probably doesn't help either, but nobody seems to ever notice that.

> **KEIKO**: Commander Sisko.

A tiny note, but I can't help noticing that Keiko seems happy for the visit.  Maybe they did it for the emotional shift, but it also does mark one of the few times that we see someone acting genuinely social.

> **KEIKO**: That's right. Late afternoon. Miles never drank coffee late in the afternoon. It keeps him up all night.

We don't really care, but since I hinted at this earlier, people who watched the teaser closely already know the episode's punchline, because we actually saw him ostentatiously drink that coffee, meaning that we already know that they didn't alter *that* part of the footage.

> **BASHIR**: What happened is I graduated. Her father was the top administrator at a medical complex in Paris. He offered me a job, promised I'd be Chief of Surgery within five years
>
> **O'BRIEN**: But you would have to give up your Starfleet career.
>
> **BASHIR**: I can't tell you how close I came. But, here I am.

Regardless of whether they care about money, we see again that they *absolutely* care about the status and power that comes with that kind of job.  Also, though, this seems like the first time that we've had any hints of civilian medical practice.

> **O'BRIEN**: Listen to me, Julian. You're always talking about adventure. Ha, adventure! Marriage is the greatest adventure of them all. It's filled with pitfalls and setbacks and mistakes, but it's a journey worth taking because you take it together. I know Keiko's been unhappy about us coming to the station. We still argue about it. But that's all right, because at the end of the day, we both know we love each other.

For the record, he decided that nobody should mind his wife's unhappiness, because it hasn't caused her to stop loving him.

## Conclusions

We mostly see the Federation through the lens of O'Brien and Bashir, but still...

### The Good

We actually see grief, and for people who don't hold institutional power.

### The Bad

O'Brien continues to do a substandard job, distinctly preferring to complain than cooperate with anybody, and leaving plenty of room for pursuers to catch him.  He seems mostly disinterested in both the engineering and military aspects of their situation, even before he gets sick.  Somehow, nobody seems to notice this.

We also (via O'Brien) see the usual sorts of racism, the disgust at foreign food and propaganda to make foreigners seem more dangerous.  Likewise, they take an entire scene to exercise their sexism, especially making it clear that they see women as homemakers while men go to work.  They also give a strong impression that said homemakers don't really get a say in how the family lives, as long as they stay in the relationship.

And we still see the obsession with acquiring status, such as important jobs.

### The Weird

Apparently, active students take continuing education classes, even though their school seems to offer them.
