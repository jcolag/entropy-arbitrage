## Sanctuary

For our usual context, this episode lands after [*Parallels*]({% post_url 2025-08-07-parallels %}), with no new **Next Generation** episodes for a couple of weeks.

And I'll warn you now, this episode might get overwhelmed by pointing out the guest stars...

> **SISKO**: I can understand that, and I sympathize. As far as I'm concerned you can shout at the provisional government all you want to, as long as it doesn't interfere with your duties here on the station.

This line almost feels shocking to me.  Over the course of my career, I've had exactly *one* manager who had an explicit "do whatever you like, as long as the work gets done" policy.  Most offices have had an implicit policy along those lines, but implicit policies get applied unevenly, making them dangerous.

> **VARANI**: Having my work appreciated again, I've almost forgotten how it feels.

If our musician friend with significant familiarity with the **DS9** theme looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki William Schallert %}, who---among *many* other appearances---we've seen (and will see again) as Nilz Baris in [*The Trouble with Tribbles*]({% post_url 2020-11-19-trouble %}).  He also led the Screen Actors Guild for a couple of years.

> **SISKO**: I'm Commander Sisko of the United Federation of Planets. You've just been beamed onto a Bajoran space station. We don't mean you any harm. We're going to bring your ship in. Once it's docked, we'll diagnose its problems and hopefully fix them. After that, you can be on your way. I can offer you medical assistance for that wound

I may have mentioned it before, but I feel like front-loading your greetings with a pile of proper nouns won't win many friends *or* influence people.  "Me Zarble Bloop of Gribble-Mibble-Doos.  You zittled to a Looquomoss thingy.  We probably won't hurt you."  Even if the translators worked well, most of what he said would convince them that it didn't...

> **TUMAK**: Oo-mata! Oo-mata! Dongo patar meeoto fenta. Fenta!

If the kid seems familiar to you, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Andrew Koenig %}---yes, Walter "Chekov" Koenig son---known largely for his role as "Boner" on **Growing Pains** (which I believe had recently ended), inspiring Harlan Ellison's *Jeffty Is Five*, his activism, and his untimely death.

> **HANEEK**: Naga dentana. Dentana. Oobay a-tal? Oobay a-tal. Keetama sootay. Shalanta Skrreea. Skrreea.

If the woman strikes you as familiar, then you might recognize her as {% wiki Deborah May %}, regular guest performer---who we'll later see on **Voyager**---but probably best known as Miss Indiana in 1970.  That said, she did have a spot on **Cobra Kai** a few years back, so she still makes the rounds.

> **SISKO**: Something must be wrong with the Universal Translator. Chief?
>
> **O'BRIEN**: It's working, Commander, but for some reason it's having a hard analyzing their language patterns. Their syntax and their grammatical structure must be completely unlike anything in our database.

As a technical person, I understand what O'Brien means, but I also assure you that, when a mechanism doesn't serve the purpose that people use it for, it absolutely does not qualify as "working."  It reminds me of the time, many years ago, when I took a long hike through a park with a group.  Dutifully following the trail markers, we reached a shockingly deep drop to a river.  The path back looked far less clear than it did in the other direction, so we ended up exiting the park and asking the rangers to pick us up.  After, the rangers explained that they blazed the trail for the crew expected in a few months to build a bridge across that gap.  {% emoji person facepalming %}  The markers "worked," but "for some reason" provided me with no useful information, or in other words *didn't* work.

> **GAI**: Kootana.

If our mystery man looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Leland Orser %}, who mostly seems to have a more "serious" career than most people wandering through here (if not always successful at the box office), but we'll see him again as another character later in the series, then on **Voyager**, plus (not in these posts) on **Enterprise**.

> **DAX**: Are all your leaders women?
>
> **HANEEK**: Yes.
>
> **BASHIR**: All of them?
>
> **DAX**: You heard her, Julian.

We all know the annoying patriarchal thing where a woman at a meeting suggests an idea that gets ignored, then a man suggests the same idea and gets credit for it.  In the future, men will apparently do that by asking the same questions, too...

> **O'BRIEN**: It seems the men are a bit too emotional to become leaders.

Notice who finds the idea the most objectionable, the only one in the room who committed violence against a civilian---during [*In the Hands of the Prophets*]({% post_url 2026-07-09-in-hands-prophets %})---because he felt frustrated, not to mention all his racist comments.  No, he has excellent control over his emotions... {% emoji eyeroll %}

> **HANEEK**: I don't know their name. I only know they were members of something called the Dominion.

We learned about the Dominion in [*Rules of Acquisition*]({% post_url 2026-09-03-rules-acquisition %}), though this doesn't tell us whether that information trickled from the Ferengi to Starfleet.

> **ROZAHN**: First of all, I want you all to know that the provisional government has taken the Skrreeans' request for immigration very seriously. The debate in the Chamber of Ministers became quite heated at times. Though I suppose you could say that about all of our debates.

If the minister looks familiar, then you might recognize her as {% wiki Kitty Swink %}, who has made many appearances over the years, including a later appearance on this show as another character and over on **Babylon 5**.  She also went fairly public with her fight against pancreatic cancer, and also married  Armin "Quark" Shimerman.

> **SORAD**: Bajor simply cannot absorb three million refugees at this time.

If the Vedek looks familiar to you, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Robert Curtis Brown %}, another performer with a long career.

Also, not really our problem, since Bajoran politics has nothing to do with these posts, but notice the smoothness of, oh, we have too many problems of our own, so we certainly can't accept refugees, certainly not working-class people who might need help in surviving.  We might need to take from one of our own people, who we consider distinct and more important.  Gosh, where have we heard *that* one before?

Sure, the episode stacks the deck in Bajor's favor, because resettling three million people at once, to a disjointed bureaucracy, would feel daunting, but to quote the twenty-first century's greatest philosophical text[^2vrNvD], "What kind of a messed up place would turn away refugees?"

[^2vrNvD]:  **The Good Place**, for those of you who haven't had the pleasure.

Maybe of interest, for any historical precedent that I can think of the writers having in mind, here, this doesn't end well for the Skrreeans.  For maybe the most prominent incident in history to that point, the {% wiki MS St. Louis %} carried nearly a thousand Jewish refugees out of Germany in 1939.  After crossing the Atlantic, Cuba, the United States, and Canada all turned the ship away.  They returned to Europe, where countries proudly accepted as few of the refugees that they could, the majority accepted by---you guessed it---countries about to get invaded by the Nazis that they fled.  You'll see the trip referred to in (casual) history books as the *Voyage of the Damned* (also the name of many fictional depictions, including a blockbuster film in the 1970s) and the *Ship of Fate*.

While Captain Gustav Schröder rightly gets a lot of credit for dealing with this while treating his passengers as well as possible given the circumstances, it does the incident a disservice when you notice that historians guess at the fates of those passengers, using statistics about the Holocaust to guess at how many survived.

I don't want to say that the Skrreeans *definitely* ended up in the path of a genocidal invasion, but I do want to draw attention to how abruptly the episode ends.  Once they leave the station, nobody cares.

> **HAZAR**: What is this about, Commander?

If the General looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Michael Durrell %}, who has appeared in a lot of weird spots starting in daytime dramas, but also films, sitcoms, science fiction, and more.

## Conclusions

As mentioned, most of this post goes to guests.  And behind the scenes, I also need to spend a lot of time consoling my spell-checker with all the alien lingo...

### The Good

We see some excellent management.

### The Bad

Even when the possibility exists that the translators won't work, and even when they know that the translators won't work, they still seem to expect everybody to speak idiomatic English.

Likewise, technology "works" when it runs, not when it accomplishes its goals.

We see some sexism, specifically in the grumbles of "reverse sexism."  They seem mystified by the idea of a matriarchal culture in general, and O'Brien gets histrionically offended that a woman would consider men emotional.
