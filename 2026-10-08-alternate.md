## The Alternate

For our usual context, this episode has no **TNG** predecessor, but [*The Pegasus*]({% post_url 2025-08-14-pegasus %}) follows it, another episode involving officers lashing out because of their father figures.

> **QUARK**: I demand an investigation.

I think that I do, too.  Police procedurals have always seemed played out, but **CSI:  Bajor** feels like it should have at least some potential.  Pair it with Odo's hard-boiled narration from [*Necessary Evil*]({% post_url 2026-09-10-necessary-evil %}) last month, and we might have a winner.  More seriously, we seem to have laughed off a corpse not accounted for elsewhere, which sounds murder-y...

> **MORA**: Odo!

If Mora looks familiar to you, then you may recognize him as {% wiki James Sloyan %}.  Actually, you *probably* recognize him, because we talked about him in [*The Defector*]({% post_url 2023-07-06-defector %}) and [*Firstborn*]({% post_url 2025-10-16-firstborn %}).  We'll see him again later in the series as Mora, then as a different character in **Voyager**.

> **ODO**: I integrate as much as I want to.
>
> **MORA**: Define *as much as I want to*.

Again, a missed opportunity to not spin Odo off into a show where he works out the antiderivatives of functions, and probably getting into fights with anybody (probably O'Brien) who dismissively calls the result "the area under the curve."

> **SISKO**: You've studied that whole Klingon opera for the music test on Friday?

Does Keiko go out of her way to ignore the local culture, or does some Federation high school curriculum demand learning specific foreign operas for music classes?  At Jake's age, I don't think that I took any music classes[^mb4F91], but I don't recall classes before that getting much more intense than learning to play some minimal song on a cheap plastic recorder.  Regardless of that, though, you'd think that after [*In the Hands of the Prophets*]({% post_url 2026-07-09-in-hands-prophets %}), Keiko might at least have locals present Bajoran art to the students.

[^mb4F91]:  This recollection doesn't make sense to me, as I think about it.  Cutting funding to the arts had already begun, but surely New York State required at least one art or music class to get a diploma, and I definitely graduated, still a bit traumatized from the heat during the outdoor ceremony under dark gowns...

Although I probably should give them credit for exposing students to culture from "overseas."  If a government didn't collapse well over a thousand years ago, I probably got all my information on foreign culture from children's television, not formal education.  Well, except that my high school did inexplicably once bring in {% wiki Gorky_Park_(band)|Gorky Park %}---the Soviet Union had at least started collapsing, so we had a lot of hasty attempts to fix Cold War distrust of the Russians, hence the different approach to the Klingons in this era---to play a set for us.  I remember that not going over well, because it felt more like a captive audience for an unremarkable rock band than having any political or educational value.

Anyway, all that to say that I find it both commendable and laughable that they expose teenagers to long-form foreign art.  Trying to think of a comparable political-and-media situation in our current environment, imagine pulling **Romeo and Juliet** out of the curriculum---which should happen anyway, because Romeo does every possible terrible thing, and we somehow hold him up as romantic---and replacing it with a {% wiki Ta'ziyeh %} performance, reading {% wiki Romance of the Three Kingdoms %}, or watching footage of some year's {% wiki Arirang Mass Games %}.  How many fourteen-year-olds passively accept this as an assignment to complete, versus those who get deeply into it or reject the idea as too alienating?  How many take anything from the experience?

> **SISKO**: This isn't about tests, Jake. This is about learning. You can't learn to appreciate Klingon opera by cramming for the exam the night before.
>
> **JAKE**: What am I ever going to use Klingon opera for?
>
> **SISKO**: Well, first of all, you don't know what you're going to be when you grow up. You may discover along the way that you want to be a musician, or you may find yourself among some Klingons...in a job...somewhere.

I love how he peters out, here, but I also find it funny that he completely lost the plot as he went through it.  He wants his son to "learn to appreciate Klingon opera."  That has *nothing to do* with "using" it, whatever it might mean to use opera.  You learn to appreciate things, because you might find out that you love them despite superficial qualities that turned you off, once you understand the mechanisms at play and (probably more importantly) deliberately sit through paying attention.

> **JAKE**: Just because you suffered through all that doesn't mean I have to.
>
> **SISKO**: Yes, it does...

It actually does amaze me that this conversation manages to avoid talk about both the curriculum requirements ("you need to learn it because otherwise you don't graduate") and---other than Sisko's abandoned first attempt---the idea that Jake can't find out that he likes the shows if he doesn't sit with them.

The conversation also raises an interesting point, though.  If presumably everybody in the Federation needs to watch some number of hours of Klingon opera at an impressionable age, then why do we only ever run across Klingons who enjoy it?  As a loose parallel, consider ancient mythologies.  Most students, at least in the United States, spend maybe ten to twenty hours learning about the Olympian gods.  Most of us, I think, retain a lot of it, but many students also dive deeper and start looking at other Indo-European myths.

> **MORA**: It was remarkable. Every morning I would come into the lab, and every morning Odo would be there resting in his liquid form in a Krokan Petri beaker. One morning I walked in and Odo was gone. The beaker was empty, but next to it was an identical beaker that had not been there before. A perfect replica in every way. It was amazing. It hasn't been the same since you've gone, Odo. Working with you has been the most rewarding part of my career.

It seems *really* weird to me that Bajoran scientists use the term {% wiki Petri_dish|Petri %} for their cell-culture containers, considering that we named the dishes after a nineteenth century German scientist.  Maybe they meant to hyphenate the name, suggesting that a presumably non-human Krokan invented something similar around the same time or refined it later?

> **WELD**: Initiating scans.

If the irrelevant sidekick looks familiar to you, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Matt McKenzie %}, known now mostly for his voice work in video games.  We'll see him again during **Voyager**'s run.

> **DAX**: Why don't we take it with us. Our computers back on the station might be able to decipher the inscriptions.

Yeah, this ancient archaeological site probably doesn't have any value to anybody, certainly not any of the locals whose cultures might relate to the people from here, right?  Might as well take whatever you want... {% emoji eyeroll %}

> **SISKO**: When my father became ill I can remember how small and weak he looked lying there on the bed. He'd been so strong, so independent. It always seemed to me there was nothing that he couldn't do. But in the end, I realized that there was nothing that he could do, and nothing I could do to help him.

I can't help noticing that, the first time that we hear about flaws in the medical establishment, of someone not getting help that they need, we hear it from a Black man, which at least resonates with {% wiki medical racism %}.  Every other time that someone has talked about aging parents---[*Where No One Has Gone Before*]({% post_url 2022-06-23-gone %}), for example, or [*Dax*]({% post_url 2026-04-16-dax %})---we seem to get the impression that they putter along fine until they...vanish, maybe?  They don't really seem to grapple with the end, but regardless, I don't recall anybody since McCoy in [**The Final Frontier**]({% post_url 2022-04-14-tff %}) (taking place something like eighty years prior) suggesting frailty or pain in old age.

Also, fans of the series know that the elder Sisko apparently didn't need anybody to "do something," since he'll become one of the more fun recurring characters soon enough.

> **DAX**: Doctor Bashir wouldn't listen to me and hid my clothes so I wouldn't leave. I had to sneak out to my quarters in a hospital gown that wouldn't close in the back. What did this?

First, still creepy.

Second, four hundred years, and they still haven't fixed *hospital gowns*?  If you'll pardon a couple of leaps of logic, but this seems to remind us that the Federation uses something like money.  Hospital gowns fail to cover everything, because minimal coverage for a "medium-sized" person (*man*) saves a ton of money.

For example, *we could* issue patients something like a poncho.  Cut a sheet of the same gown material to the right size, either pop their heads through the middle or wrap it around their shoulders, and you have full coverage on any body shape, with no worries about the garment constraining the patient.  We don't do that, because it would involve labor, generate unusable scraps to discard, and facilities would need to figure out how to store bolts of material in a way that keeps it clean.  In other words, we keep the substandard gowns, because the alternatives cost more.  If they could replicate clothing without cost, then they would probably do the same, or something more sophisticated with embedded instrumentation.

> **DAX**: Feel like getting a raktajino before we call it a night?
>
> **BASHIR**: My replicator or yours?
>
> ...
>
> **BASHIR**: She enjoys it. She actually gets some kind of perverse pleasure out of it. One of these days I'm going to stop chasing her, and then we'll see.

Or---and I only share this option because I care---respect the woman's damned boundaries and stop trying to figure out how to manipulate her into bed, especially not more than a couple of hours after hiding her clothes.

The day when he stops chasing her can't come soon enough, in other words.

> **MORA**: Constable?
>
> **ODO**: It's a nickname that I barely tolerate.

I've pointed this out before, but we wouldn't need to go through this, if he had a job with an honorific title like everyone else.

> **ODO**: It doesn't! I do not commit criminal acts. It is not in my nature.

Not really relevant to our project, but I can't pass up at least *mentioning* that it makes no sense to have a nature that precludes committing crimes, because what constitutes a crime changes over time and as you travel.  For a probably apocryphal example, would Odo [reflexively start smiling](https://skeptics.stackexchange.com/questions/27959/are-the-citizens-of-milan-legally-obliged-to-smile) if he pursued a suspect to Milan, Italy, suddenly incapable of frowning?

> **MORA**: They won't know what to do with you. They'll put you in a high security prison, or quarantine you on a deserted asteroid in the Gamma Quadrant. Odo, they'll put you in a zoo.

I mean...if the *Enterprise*-D crew's treatment of Data provides us with any clues, they'd presumably either kill Odo or send him off for experimentation with someone like Mora in hopes of manufacturing the ability to change shapes, maybe both...

> **SISKO**: Just get him to the force-field. We'll do the rest. Pass the word that phasers are to be set on maximum stun. The moment Doctor Mora appears to be in jeopardy, I'll open fire. Their orders are to follow my lead. If maximum stun doesn't bring him down immediately, we set phasers to kill.

That escalated even faster than I expected.  After all, they could probably beam Mora out if he gets into trouble, but maybe Starfleet regulations require ratcheting up the dramatic tension by having the crew endanger the life of a valued colleague?

## Conclusions

We don't get much out of this episode, though more than I expected.

### The Good

Federation schools at least attempt to expose students to foreign works of art.

### The Bad

Schooling seems utterly bizarre, with high school opera appreciation classes that nobody seems to understand the purpose of.

We continue to see the idea that ancient cultural sites exist for wealthy white people to pillage them.

The healthcare system has apparently serious limits, possibly racialized.  And on the subject of race, we also see that people of minority ethnicities receive an extrajudicial execution if they ever step out of line.

And Bashir continues to delight in violating Dax's boundaries, trying to coerce her into a relationship.

### The Weird

We see more confirmation of economics influencing decisions.
