## Blood Oath

To those celebrating or have celebrated, and (as always) acknowledging the [holiday's problematic history](https://daily.jstor.org/thanksgiving-has-been-reinvented-many-times/), I'd like to wish you all a happy {% emoji fowl %} Thanksgiving.  I give thanks that we seem to have broken the accidental tradition of some serious turkeys of episodes falling on the day for something more fun.

For our usual context, this episode lands between [*Journey's End*]({% post_url 2025-10-09-journey-s-end %}) and, after a spring hiatus, [*Firstborn*]({% post_url 2025-10-16-firstborn %}).  Over in the **Babylon 5** world, they hadn't had an episode for a couple of weeks, but *Deathwalker* followed this episode with a similar hiatus.

Anyway, a lot like last week, we won't spend much time in areas relevant to us, so we can probably kick back and enjoy the guests.

> **ODO**: The Battle of Klach D'kel Brakt was a legendary Klingon victory over the Romulans almost a century ago.

Not that it matters much, but this timing---this episode presumably coming about six years after our fixed date of 2364 in [*The Neutral Zone*]({% post_url 2022-11-10-neutral-zone %}) at the end of **TNG**'s first season---seems to put the battle between the end of the original series and [**The Motion Picture**]({% post_url 2022-03-10-tmp %}).

> **KOR**: [A bunch of Klingon words that I won't torture my spell-checker with]

You no doubt remember {% wiki John Colicos %} from [*Errand of Mercy*]({% post_url 2020-07-09-mercy %}).  He'll return a couple of times through the series, and the character---though with James Doohan's voice---also appeared in [*The Time Trap*]({% post_url 2021-11-04-time-trap %}).

> **KOLOTH**: I am Koloth.

And you presumably also recognize {% wiki William_Campbell_(actor)|William Campbell %} from [*The Trouble with Tribbles*]({% post_url 2020-11-19-trouble %}), not to mention the prototype-Q Trelane in [*The Squire of Gothos*]({% post_url 2020-05-07-squire %}).  Let's not ask why they didn't track down Charlie Brill to reprise the Klingon disguised as a human...at least, not for a couple of years, our time.

> **KOR**: Curzon, my beloved old friend!
>
> **DAX**: I'm Jadzia now.
>
> **KOR**: Well, Jadzia, my beloved old friend.

They *had* to have written this to criticize the Federation, right?  By this point, the franchise has spent about seven years trying to sell us on the idea that we hallucinated the (unfortunate) Mongol crypto-Russian Klingons from the original series and substitute them with the "motorcycle gangs mired in inflexible tradition" idea, with a love of toxic masculinity.  And yet, despite setting up fairly conservative expectations bolstered by introducing the character trying to relive past glories, he accepts Dax's new identity with no trouble.

Meanwhile, you'll recall Crusher's allegorical transphobia at the end of [*The Host*]({% post_url 2024-04-11-host %}) not too long before this, and even Sisko's confession that he'd need time to make sense of the revised relationship in [*A Man Alone*]({% post_url 2026-03-19-man-alone %}).  A well-past-his-prime Klingon---and a Ferengi, in *A Man Alone*---have shown a more inclusive attitude than any Federation citizen has so far.

> **DAX**: You never had much use for the Federation, did you, Koloth. I used to call you D'akturak, Ice man, because no proposal, no matter how reasonable, no matter how much it favored the Klingon Empire would satisfy you. You were the toughest man I ever faced across the negotiations table.

I don't want to nitpick---not really, anyway---but since we don't have much else to do this week, this seems weird, right?  *The Trouble with Tribbles* had nothing to do with negotiations, as opposed to Kor in *Errand of Mercy* who at least had a partly administrative position.

> **KANG**: Yes. That is exactly what it means. I have found the Albino.

And you presumably recognize {% wiki Michael Ansara %} from [*The Day of the Dove*]({% post_url 2021-04-01-dove %}).  He'll come back for a **Voyager** episode, as well as an appearance on **Babylon 5**, but people mostly know him for marrying Barbara Eden after dating her as a literal publicity stunt.  You might also know him from voice work in **Reading Rainbow** and **Batman:  The Animated Series** and its spinoffs.

> **DAX**: Don't mistake a new face for a new soul, Kang. I still feel at one with your family just as I did eight decades ago. I hope one day you will embrace me as a brother again.
>
> **KOLOTH**: Brother! Ha!
>
> **KOR**: Brother, sister, what's the difference. We're here now. Speak to us, Kang. Where is he? Where did you find the Albino?

OK, not all the Klingons have progressive views on gender.  Good for Kor, then.

> **KANG**: The old Klingon ways are passing. There was a time, when I was a young man, the mere mention of the Klingon Empire made worlds tremble. Now, our warriors are opening restaurants and serving racht to the grandchildren of men I slaughtered in battle. Things are not what they used to be, not even a blood oath.

Judging from what we saw in [*Melora*]({% post_url 2026-08-27-melora %}) and [*Playing God*]({% post_url 2026-11-12-playing-god %}), they at least open *nice* restaurants.

> **KIRA**: Why are we talking about this?
>
> **DAX**: If it bothers you, we can stop.

It really does seem awful to push someone in Kira's position like that.  Dax treats it like she casually made a decision to turn vigilante, rather than growing up traumatized with a need to overthrow an occupational government.

As I recall, by the way, many police departments at this point had begun mandating post-traumatic stress counseling for officers who discharge a firearm on duty, regardless of the reason or result.  To echo Kira's final response to the conversation, nobody walks away intact from trying to kill someone.

> **KIRA**: Look, you've said yourself every new life for a Trill has to be a new life. If not, you'd wind up paying off old debts forever. These Klingons can't possibly expect you to keep this oath.

Wait, what?  How much debt---financial or otherwise---do Trill rack up in their lives that they'd expect to need to pay it back for hundreds of years?  Related, if the Trill have already solved this, then that seems like something that they should've brought up in [*Dax*]({% post_url 2026-04-16-dax %}), when we spent all that time watching the entire cast try to find an answer to exactly this question.

> **DAX**: I knew I had to get you very, very angry or my mission wouldn't succeed. Getting you angry at me was the only way to begin to create a bond between us.

Once again, Dax announces that she sees herself as a manipulator using people as instruments to achieve goals.

> **SISKO**: Jadzia Dax took an oath too, when she joined Starfleet. You're subject to orders, Lieutenant.

You'd think that this oath would come up more often, and maybe we'd get some sense of what it entails, but...nah.

> **SISKO**: Let's say you are. Let's say you even survive this insanity. You expect to just come back here and resume your duties as though nothing's happened?

And she'll do exactly that, with apparently no repercussions other than a quick ableist slur.  I feel like Worf got to do that at some point, too, making me think that Starfleet routinely treats bloodthirsty quests for vengeance like ordinary personal days.

> **DAX**: If Curzon were here, you'd be out of luck, but Jadzia Dax is a science officer.

I notice that she calls herself a "science officer," not a scientist.  That seems odd, considering that one term has at least a vague meaning, and the other...doesn't.  I suppose that it probably doesn't matter much, since neither term implies that she could engineer something to disable energy weapons.

> **ALBINO**: Report.

If not-important-enough-to-get-a-name looks familiar to you, then you may recognize {% wiki Bill Bolender %} playing him, from his (usually minor) appearances throughout film and television.

> **GUARD**: The armory's been sabotaged. I've diverted the forward patrols to that area.

And if the trusty sidekick looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Christopher Collins %}, the screeching voice of Cobra Commander and Starscream in the 1980s.  But we have also seen him in [*A Matter of Honor*]({% post_url 2023-01-12-matter-honor %}) (as a different Klingon), [*Samaritan Snare*]({% post_url 2023-03-16-samaritan-snare %}), and [*The Passenger*]({% post_url 2026-04-23-passenger %}).  Not to bring the mood down, but he'd come to a highly untimely end a couple of months after this aired from a bout of encephalitis.

## Conclusions

As expected, we don't get much beyond a bit of history.

### The Bad

We see faster acceptance of Trill identity-changing from elderly Klingons than we have seen from anybody with Federation citizenship.

People continue to indulge Dax's complete lack of empathy towards her colleagues, in this case prying into Kira's worst moments, and describes herself as manipulative.  And maybe related, Dax seems to believe that the Trill as separating their hosts more to ignore social debts that they have incurred, rather than because of some inherent change in character.

Despite each officer taking an oath (of some sort), Starfleet doesn't seem to have any issues with said officers abruptly leaving the service to violate that oath and break laws, since they don't see any consequences for their actions beyond the social.

We do, however, still see casual bigotry against psychiatric illness.
