## Paradise

For our usual context, this episode lands between [*Lower Decks*]({% post_url 2025-09-04-lower-decks %}) and [*Thine Own Self*]({% post_url 2025-09-11-thine-own-self %}).  At the competition, this sits between **Babylon 5**'s *Born to the Purple* and *Infection*.

Oh, and while I can't imagine that anybody comes here for franchise-related news, I also feel like it would probably strike people as callous[^tO5u9w] to not mention that we lost Walter Koenig on Sunday and Patrick Stewart yesterday.  Best wishes to the people who they leave behind.

[^tO5u9w]:  I didn't note the passing of Nichelle Nichols at the end of July 2022, but I also didn't spend any time on guest stars at that point in the run.

> Station log, stardate 47573.1. To respond to questions about setting up colonies near the wormhole, Chief O'Brien and I have set out to survey nearby star systems.

Notice that we've gone back to needing colonies, despite "near the wormhole" feeling a lot like Bajoran space.

> **SISKO**: I don't want you to make it easy for him, Chief. He has to know what it takes to make it in Starfleet.

I realize that some of the upcoming arc centers on this, but it still bears mentioning that Sisko assumes that his child will automatically gravitate toward military service, or that they don't see any other careers worth considering.

> **O'BRIEN**: I'll have him up to his elbows in thorium grease, sir.

That sounds more like a threat than fairness.  As we ended up needing to discuss in [*The Siege*]({% post_url 2026-08-06-siege %}), you generally won't see much risk in casual exposure to thorium, but if you get any internally---such as the droplets and debris inevitably kicked up trudging through grease about a foot away from your face---or experience long-term exposure, that can get pretty nasty.

> **O'BRIEN**: It was a matter of figuring out how to get a field transporter operational in ten minutes or wind up a Cardassian prisoner of war. Now, I didn't know a transporter from a turbo-lift in those days but somehow, in nine minutes, fifty-three seconds, I got that thing to work. I got thirteen men safely off the surface of Setlik Three. Next thing I know I'm the tactical officer on the *Rutledge*. That's how I got the gold suit.

That story sounds *incredibly* fake, but it also shows exactly Starfleet's (and O'Brien's) entire deal with "engineering."  He "somehow" fixed it, with know prior knowledge of how any of the gadgets work.  We know that he has no issues with spewing out technobabble to make himself look smart, so the fact that he doesn't describe the problem means that it either required embarrassingly little knowledge or work ("I plugged it in") or he doesn't actually know what happened, but took credit for it.

Also, though, how does he square his low mechanical aptitude with the loud announcement that he built bottled ships in [*Booby Trap*]({% post_url 2023-06-08-booby-trap %}), and the mention in (the still-upcoming, no less) [*All Good Things...*]({% post_url 2025-11-13-all-good-things-1 %}) that he built model starship engines?  I suppose that he could've done a terrible job with those hobbies, but O'Brien doesn't really strike me as the sort of person who sticks with personal projects where he doesn't see immediate success.  I mean, we've seen that he doesn't do any emotional regulation, and even his personal life largely involves him making everybody else do the relationship work while he "participates" by resenting that people ask him to show up.

> **SISKO**: Well, Jake's ready for a new suit, too. The boy seems to grow a centimeter each week. You know, he struck me out with a curveball the other day. First time.

A---as in "one"---new suit?  Does Jake wear suits?  I feel like we see him in different *clothes* all the time, so Sisko probably doesn't mean that if he only needs one, but I admittedly also haven't paid much attention since they made it clear that the crew had started selecting from Bajoran and other non-Federation styles.

> **VINOD**: Don't move! And put your hands up. Turn around. Slowly.

If the not-at-all merry man looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Michael B. Silver %}, probably most prominent in a recurring **NYPD Blue** role.

> **JOSEPH**: They're from Starfleet.

And if Joseph looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Steve Vinovich %}, who has appeared seemingly everywhere, but would probably get recognized more as a stage performer, such as his run as Joe in Broadway's **Waitress**.

> **SISKO**: *Erewhon*-class personnel transport.
>
> **JOSEPH**: Commissioned the *Santa Maria*. Now, we just call it the Cabin.

The ship class refers to **{% wiki Erewhon %}, or Over the Range** by Samuel Butler, an 1872 novel about a country that satirizes English culture, but also tries to treat machine intelligence seriously.  And the ship's name presumably refers to the {% wiki Santa_María_(ship)|Santa Maria %}, the biggest of the ships given to Christopher Columbus so that he could cosplay explorer finding his way to India.  I don't know about any of you, but that already sounds like a disaster waiting to happen...

> **ALIXUS**: After all this time, visitors!

If the leader looks familiar, then you might recognize her as {% wiki Gail Strickland %}, who made a *lot* of appearances over the years, starting on **Dark Shadows**---the daytime soap opera that pulled the majority of its plot points from horror and Gothic fiction---and until her retirement a while back.

> **VINOD**: Who won the soccer matches this year? Is Golanga still playing?
>
> **SISKO**: No, he hurt his knee four years ago. They replaced it with a bio-implant, but he wasn't the same after that. Who was it that won last year anyway? I never can remember.

People apparently still play soccer and call it that rather than association football---sorry everyplace not in the United States---in enough numbers that you can expect random people to know recent wins and player drama.  Oh, and the surname Golanga seems to have the most representation around Tanzania.

> **CASSANDRA**: What are women's fashions like back home now?

Last guest that I know of:  If our fashion plate looks familiar, then you probably recognize her as {% wiki Julia Nickson %}, who among many other appearances, we previously saw in [*The Arsenal of Freedom*]({% post_url 2022-10-06-arsenal-freedom %}), and would soon---as in, a week and a half after this aired---get her introduction as Catherine Sakai on **Babylon 5**.

> **KIRA**: Do you know an Admiral Mitsuya?
>
> **DAX**: Best poker player in the fleet, next to me.

I don't know if I find it more annoying that we get yet more evidence that nobody in the fleet does anything but play poker, or that they still need to play these insipid dominance games.  And if you don't believe that poker occupies a lot of their attention based (largely) on what we saw on **The Next Generation**, consider that Dax doesn't explain the reference at all (and go on to explain defects in Sisko's ability) to a colleague who barely grew up around her *own* culture, let alone Earth's, presumably meaning that either somebody insisted on trying to get Kira to learn the rules and play---and why didn't we get *that* episode, given how amazing a mismatch that seems?---or the crew talks about it enough in front of her that it doesn't sound to her like the fizzbin scene from [*A Piece of the Action*]({% post_url 2020-12-03-action %}).

> **SISKO**: Not very good. She says we've become fat and lazy and dull.
>
> **O'BRIEN**: My wife told me something along those lines just last week.

C'mon, you can't step on *my* jokes like that...

> **SISKO**: "The common conceit that the human species has evolved over the last several centuries is ludicrous. What gains we have made have come at the cost of our own core identities. Man has lost touch with his true power."

The evolution bit tracks.  Genetically, a species doesn't generally drift that much genetically without some crisis that eliminates parts of the population with or without some trait; you might recall this described in a biology class as {% wiki punctuated equilibrium %}.  But we have also spent seven years with Picard's crew having them lecture us about once per year on how you can only really look to Shakespeare's plays, developed almost eight hundred years prior to their setting, to understand the human condition, suggesting that even *they* don't believe that much evolution has happened.

The rest of the quote, though, feels like every right-wing philosophy, insisting that humans have some inborn nature---usually something that loves strict hierarchies and has no empathy---suppressed by social progress.

> **O'BRIEN**: There's nothing to find. Waveguides, conduits, baffle plates. You name it, it's gone. The ship looks like it's been through a Ferengi junkyard.

Why a "*Ferengi* junkyard"?  I have only known one person in the business over the years, but I would think that anybody running a junkyard wants to off-load as much of the junk as possible as quickly as possible.  I don't even think that a Hallmark Christmas movie could pull off "we can only sell this catalytic converter to the right customer"...

> **JOSEPH**: Well, we all had to abandon our dependence on technology. Removing every trace of it, well, that made the transition easier.
>
> **O'BRIEN**: As an engineer, you must've had some problems with that.

How the heck does *that* follow?  If technology no longer works, I think that most of us would prefer to *recycle* the parts instead of discarding everything, but if internal combustion engines somehow stopped working, you wouldn't expect every automotive engineer to keep their useless gasoline-powered vehicles because they spent time learning about and working on them.  You can use that space and material for anything else.

> **JOSEPH**: I was the last convert. But as time passed, I realized Alixus was right. She was helping us discover a new philosophy of life together, because we needed one to survive. And I'll tell you, every one of us is better for it. We're more committed to each other. We are truly a part of each other's lives. We've renewed a sense of community that man left behind centuries ago.

You might recall this loss-of-community bit from [*Time Squared*]({% post_url 2023-02-16-time-squared %}), Pulaski recycling the evergreen conservative moaning about the impending collapse of civilization due to people eating alone.  As I mentioned in that post, this philosophy usually derives from some combination of anti-labor sentiment suggesting that workers shouldn't have the money to eat separately and anti-feminism mourning the days when housewives might spend days preparing dinner parties for the bread-winner's colleagues.

> **DAX**: The details are coming in from Starfleet. The Romulan vessel *Gasko* reported seeing an abandoned runabout. Markings NCC seven-two-four-five-two.

While none of the characters see fit to comment on it, I can't help noticing how they casually mention the Romulans helping, rather than trying to characterize them as vile, conniving enemies.  You could almost think of them like people...

> **SISKO**: Well, my father was a chef. He grew all his own vegetables. My brothers and I were sent out to the gardens every day.

In [*The Alternate*]({% post_url 2026-10-08-alternate %}), Sisko similarly spoke about his father in a distant past tense, still a bit odd, considering that we'll spend quite a bit of time with him and check out the restaurant starting in...probably about a year.

> **VINOD**: Most of the others, they'd only eaten replicated food before they got here. The flavor of the foods we prepare, quite a surprise to them.

We have another comment to add to the list of people finding replicated food substandard.

> **O'BRIEN**: What did he do to deserve this?
>
> **JOSEPH**: He stole a candle.

And we don't get a *single* {% wiki Jean Valjean|24,601 %} reference.

Anyway, did you notice that the tight-knit community where everybody relies on everybody else for their survival has private property to steal?  And I feel like we really need some back-story, here, because surely you either steal a candle due to an emergency or would have plenty of time to ask around.

> **SISKO**: Discipline is one thing. Torture is another.

Specifically, we (with no flair for the dramatic) call this method the {% wiki Box_(torture)|box %}, sometimes hot box or sweatbox.  It became most widely used in the Southern United States by slave camps and prisons, and its use has diminished significantly but not ended by jurisdictions with hot weather at times of terrible leadership.  And yes, I mean that the United States keeps coming up on the list with a certain faction in charge, in between fascist Portugal and the usual suspects in East Asia.  Given the sugarcane-like appearance of their crop, though, it seems *really* hard to shake the impression of slavery.

> **ALIXUS**: Stephan will never steal another candle, and neither will anyone else who saw him here today. In time, you'll understand that this is a simple and effective way to maintain law and order in our community.

It probably doesn't matter for our purposes, but it feels worth noting that [law and order](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/law_and_order) rarely means the [rule of law](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/rule_of_law), but rather that the law exists to enforce a social order.

> **SISKO**: Did she send you here? Did she send you here to make love to me?
>
> ...
>
> **SISKO**: Core behavior. Does that chapter come before or after sexual procurement?

This episode has plenty of problems, but I think that it might have at least flowed better if we didn't have this angry objection to sex work.  It doesn't do anything for the episode---and the anger probably flew beneath most of the audience's notice at the time, since many people saw sex work as some inherently awful crime, rather than today where we see multiple movements to decriminalize (though, for complicated reasons, rarely legalize) the work---and it makes Sisko look like a Puritanical weirdo.  That could draw some analogy with Alixus's Puritanical crackpot views, but nothing like that ever comes up.

> **DAX**: A very talented Hopi I knew did things with a rope you wouldn't believe. It's an old West American art-form.

Apparently, the Hopi still live on Earth, despite at least strong hints to the contrary in [*Journey's End*]({% post_url 2025-10-09-journey-s-end %}), which would've aired about six weeks after this.

> **DAX**: Remind me to send a thank you note to the assembly teams.

Do you know what would make me believe that she might do this?  If, rather than the quip, she looked up and named at least one of those workers, instead of treating them like a label.

> **ALIXUS**: No, Ben, I'm not. You are his commanding officer. I hold you responsible for his actions. I'm putting you in there.

Hey, remember when I said that this discipline/torture has its strongest association with slavery?  Yeah, somebody on the writing staff probably should've given the optics of this scene some thought.  Or maybe they could've done something with the idea of the leader seeming to salivate over the idea of punishing him.

> **O'BRIEN**: I can do it so it won't hurt at all.

Well, *that* sounds unnecessarily sinister.

> **ALIXUS**: I helped invent it. You'd be surprised how many scientists are sympathetic to my philosophies.

We've seen evidence throughout the franchise that quite a few people want to abandon space travel and technology.  Now, it sounds like they have started organizing.

> **O'BRIEN**: We have room for all of you on the runabout. If you have anything you want to bring with you, you ought to get it now.

Really?  They always make those things seemed cramped for three or four people.

> **JOSEPH**: Miles, this is our home. Whatever Alixus may be guilty of, she did give us our community. I'm not sure if we'll leave the device on or off now that we know it's there. And we'll have to decide if we want to establish contact with the outside world. But Alixus is right. We have found something here that none of us is willing to give up.

They...leave this guy in charge?  I mean, he seemed sympathetic for most of the episode, but then he stepped on the couple of people *planning to leave* to assert that nobody wanted to leave.  That doesn't sound voluntary, but I guess that we stopped caring.

## Conclusions

Soccer has retained significant popularity.

### The Good

We see the Romulans treated as entirely normal people, rather than official enemies.

### The Bad

We see again that parents see Starfleet as, in effect, the only serious career option for children.

Starfleet, at least, seems to have terrible health and safety regulations.

O'Brien continues to make his incompetence clear.  He tells a story about fixing something on a deadline with no clue what he did.  And he seems to believe that engineers should have a strong emotional attachment to the physical technological artifacts, even when they don't work.

They still obsess over poker, even in the highest social classes.

We also continue to see that people treat relationships as fights to win.  And maybe related, we also see casual racism against the Ferengi.

The episode centers on a radical anti-technology sentiment that has significant support throughout the Federation.  They also seem to drench it in reactionary thought, bemoaning social progress, using slavery-adjacent punishments, and so forth.  But they also talk a lot about the inferiority of replicated food.  This movement may have started organizing and planning.

Sisko shows an almost violent rejection of sex work.  And he also leaves the colony in the hands of someone who seems to have only slightly more interest in civil liberties than his predecessor.

### The Weird

The Federation needs colonies again.
