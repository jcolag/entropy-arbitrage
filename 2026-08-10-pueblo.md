## Entropy Arbitrage, the Posts

{% codeberg jcolag/entropy-arbitrage-posts %}

As expected (and hinted in the previous section), I converted every(?) link to a Wikipedia page---such as the link to the Pueblo Revolt's entry at the top of this post---to use my new `wiki` plugin instead of using a raw URL.  For those interested in this sort of thing, I took care of *most* of the conversion by running this on the command-line.

```console
sed --in-place 's/\[\([^]]*\)\](https:\/\/\([a-z]*\).wikipedia.org\/wiki\/\([^)]*\))/{ % wiki \3|\1|\2 %}/g' *.md
```

To prevent Jekyll from expanding the plugin into a Wikipedia URL, I added a space between the open-brace and percent sign that Liquid[^JpBLDZ] uses to identify the plugins.

[^JpBLDZ]:  As if I don't have enough projects using technology from terrible people, Shopify maintains Liquid, and their CEO recently suggested that wealthy Canadians should get extra votes in their elections, following up almost a decade of helping hate groups fund themselves by literally selling hate speech.

Aside from that, the "stream editor" goes into each file to directly modify them---the `--in-place` bit; you'll often see people abbreviate it to `-i`---to pull out the key information from the Markdown link, such as the displayed text in brackets (the name of or less direct reference to the page), the language[^Gb-aMN], and the "slug" that identifies the page.  The regular expression then turns that into a use of the plugin that looks something like `slug|name|language`.  That command took care of well over a thousand posts at once with no problems, leaving me with about a hundred fifty pages with newly broken links.

[^Gb-aMN]:  In case anybody wanted to know, pulling out every Wikipedia link across the blog and taking out the duplicates, over five thousand links go to English-language Wikipedia.  Twelve go to German, nine to Simple English, and one each to Japanese, Malaysian, Portuguese, and Romanian.

You see, Media Wiki's URL format doesn't really play well with Markdown.  A link in Markdown has display text in square brackets `[like this]`, followed immediately by the URL in parentheses `(https://like.this)`.  However, when a page title on a Media Wiki page could refer to multiple things, it disambiguates by specifying what it means in parentheses, so you get names such as `Orion_(constellation)`.  Normally, this doesn't matter, but when you combine the two, you get something like `[Orion](Orion_(constellation))`, ending by closing off two parenthetical bits, which Jekyll handles well enough, but makes it difficult to extract with a clean regular expression.  And after a couple of years into the blog, I realized that, for my own sanity, I should replace parentheses in URLs with their URL encodings, `%28` and `%29`.

However, I didn't go *back* to fix those older URLs, and my `sed` command doesn't even bother to try to figure it out, leaving me with some files that'd result in a broken Wikipedia URL followed by some remnants of the real URL.  To find those candidates, I ran the following to search, except (again) for that first space in the search string to get Jekyll to render it properly.

```console
grep --only-matching '{ % wiki [^}]*}[^["|!?,.;:`*/ -]' *.md
```

That looks for the `wiki` plugin with all text not a closing brace (`[^}]*`), closes off the brace, and has a character other than an open bracket `[`, quotation mark `"`, pipe `|`, exclamation point `!`, question mark `?`, comma `,`, period `.`, semicolon `;`, colon `:`, back-quote (which I can't figure out how to show without fouling up the rendering), asterisk `*`, slash `/`, or hyphen `-`, any of which could normally follow a URL.  Unsurprisingly, the `--only-matching` argument (also `-o`) only shows the text that matches that expression instead of the entire line, as you might guess from the full term.

From there, I opened those files as a group unless I could already see something that made sense.  Then, searching for the `wiki` plugin and visually inspecting for anything that would produce a broken URL, I could fix it manually.

It took a while, but as mentioned, this allows me to change the URL format in the plugin and redirect *every Wikipedia link* to a clone or an alternate front-end, but not a "Wikipedia alternative" that has lower standards of reliable sources.  And as I've mentioned a couple of times now including earlier in this post, I did this in case [Wiki Workers United](https://wikiworkersunited.org/) calls for a strike.  Specifically, I wanted a structure where, in that I-hope-unlikely[^R54AW_] event, I can switch to a mirror to avoid crossing their picket line, then switch back at the end of the strike so that I can support a project that I still value.

[^R54AW_]:  Given that members of the Wikimedia Board have delivered statements in inconsistent places that contradict each other and public facts, it seems like the Foundation either has no coherent plan or wants people to see them as not having a plan.  Combine that with their registering Littler Mendelson---maybe the most infamous union-busting law firm, with recent clients that include Volkswagen, Starbucks, and Amazon---as their representation with the (US) National Labor Relations Board, while holding captive-audience meetings to pressure employees against joining the union, I increasingly suspect that my hopes for avoiding a strike won't come to fruition.

While I didn't bother to revert anything, this would also allow me to stop worrying about the parentheses completely, which does slow down writing posts a bit.

Oh, and if you happen to check the log, you'll notice that I staged the commits for this over a few days. ## Entropy Arbitrage, the Code

{% codeberg jcolag/entropy-arbitrage-code %}

The aforementioned wiki plugin now does some substantial cleaning of the inputs.

Most important, it calls `.strip` on each argument, to make sure that no random spaces show up to wreck the URL.  It also calls `CGI.escape` on the target page's "slug," so that I no longer need to worry about parentheses or apostrophes messing with the HTML code.  Actually, that bends the truth a bit.  I actually call `CGI.unescape`, *then* call `CGI.escape`, because of all the times that I manually escaped the "slug."  If I don't do both, then all the old posts either need editing, or an open-parenthesis (to pick an example) turns from `%28` to `%2528` when it escapes the percent sign...

It also defaults to English, since I rarely[^oOUpnG] link to pages on other versions of Wikipedia, so I can skip specifying the language most of the time.  And, especially nice for me, if the page's slug matches the text that I want to display---plus or minus the spaces and underscores---then I can supply the display text alone, and let the plugin deal with it all for me.

[^oOUpnG]:  You should have an earlier footnote putting numbers to that assertion, if you scroll up a bit...

For example, I could point to the English {% wiki Where's the beef? %} entry by only providing `Where's the beef?`, and it should URL-encode the apostrophe and question mark in the URL, then replacing spaces with underscores---resulting in `Where%27s_the_beef%3F`---to make browsers and servers happy.  Alternatively, I could specify `Where's_the_beef?|an outdated fast food slogan|ms` to get to the {% wiki Where's_the_beef?|Malaysian Wikipedia entry|ms %}.

And other than the parsing, we have one key line, which I have described, but probably haven't shown.

```ruby
"<a href='https://#{@lang}.wikipedia.org/wiki/#{@page}'>#{@text}</a>"
```

If I change that URL template to point to some mirror, clone, or similar alternate route[^qEXZJZ] to the same information, then that changes every Wikipedia URL across the blog to point to that site on the next rebuild.  For example, I have seen alternate front-ends to Wikipedia over the years with URLs like `https://some.example/wiki/#{@page}?lang=#{@lang}`, so plugging that in would point everything to them.  Then, when I no longer feel the need to point people elsewhere---assuming that the Foundation recognizes the union, I mean---then I can revert the URL template, and everything swings back to Wikipedia.
