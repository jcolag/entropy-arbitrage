## Rules of Acquisition

For our usual context, this episode came immediately before or after [*Attached*]({% post_url 2025-07-17-attached %}), maybe depending on the market, with no intervening **Next Generation** episode before the next **Deep Space Nine** episode.  Meanwhile, this episode has more to do with moving the overall plot along---and investigating another culture---than the Federation, but we'll do what we can.

> **PEL**: My apologies, Quark, but I really think you should try this.

If Pel looks familiar, then you might recognize "him" as {% wiki Helene Udy %}, who has actually had much more of a film career (in projects that I've never heard of) than anything else.

> **ZEK**: Why do you look so surprised? I told you I'd be back.

We last saw the Nagus (and eventually his valet or whatever) in...well, [*The Nagus*]({% post_url 2026-05-07-nagus %}), as it turns out.

> **KIRA**: Which is why, if you're smart, you don't do business with the Ferengi.
>
> **SISKO**: I think you've made your point, Major.

Apparently, you can't spread racist stereotypes about the Ferengi...to the faces of their more powerful representatives.  They seem fine with it in every other situation.

> **SISKO**: On whether you want to conduct business here or not.

It seems notable how quickly Sisko...doesn't really abuse his authority, because he mostly only holds the Nagus to the offered terms, but he does so smoothly to help Bajor, with seemingly no expectation that anybody but the Ferengi government will get the credit.

> **INGLATU**: Don't worry, just a minor disagreement.

If Inglatu looks familiar to you, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Brian_Thompson_(actor)|Brian Thompson %}, mostly known for action roles, especially under prosthetics.  We've seen him in [*A Matter of Honor*]({% post_url 2023-01-12-matter-honor %}) and (still about a year in the episode's future) [**Generations**]({% post_url 2025-12-04-star-trek-generations-1 %}) before this, and he'd go on to recur as a Romulan on **Enterprise**, after another guest spot on this show in a couple of seasons.

> **KIRA**: You sound like you admire them for it.
>
> **DAX**: I suppose in a way I do.

As I have probably mentioned, I don't particularly care for the Dax character.  Much of that centers on this "not like other girls" cliché, and the suggestion that she has become a better person by learning to accept and appreciate the sexism, instead of "letting it get to her."  That *has* to have messed up a lot of viewers.

And it almost certainly comes from privilege.  She presents as a conventionally attractive young woman, but will have an even younger identity eventually.  She has power and authority in Starfleet, plus an extensive education.  The competitiveness of the Trill joining process, as we learned about it in [*Invasive Procedures*]({% post_url 2026-08-13-invasive-procedures %}), strongly implies that she comes from a background granting her advantages over the rest of the population.  And probably more aspects show that the harm probably lands more on her colleagues than it does on her.

In fact, it seems really disappointing that the episode doesn't even try to connect Dax's "really admiring the way a culture treats its women live livestock" attitude with Pel's "cost of doing business" expression as "he" needed to listen to the rest of the bar staff giggle about how much they want submissive women.

> **DAX**: That's because you don't socialize with them the way I do. Looking back over seven lifetimes, I can't think of a single race I've enjoyed more.

First, no, you do not need to get to know the misogynists better, ever.

Second, pardon me, but who the Hell "enjoys" a *race*?  She specifically doesn't refer to any of the individuals, but the race as a monolithic thing that exists for her entertainment.  I don't even feel comfortable referring to the times that people say anything like that in our world.

> **KIRA**: They're greedy, misogynistic, untrustworthy little trolls and I wouldn't turn my back on one of them for a second.
>
> **DAX**: Neither would I. But once you accept that, you'll find they can be a lot of fun.

We went from enjoying a race as a faceless mob to literally using them for entertainment once you ignore all the standard ingredients for fascism.

> **DAX**: You can't buy that kind of loyalty.

Short-term?  Sure you can.  **The Next Generation** showed us that constantly...

> **DAX**: I didn't know the Replimat had a Ferengi menu.
>
> **PEL**: It doesn't. I like to try new things.
>
> **DAX**: That's unusual for a Ferengi.

How does anybody even consider saying something like this about a space-faring culture?  This reminds me substantially of [*Suspicions*]({% post_url 2025-04-17-suspicions %}), where they wanted us to marvel at the existence of a Ferengi scientist, as if all their technology appeared randomly.

> **DAX**: You know he once convinced me to go up to a holosuite with him. Turns out he recreated the bedroom I slept in as a child. He overheard me describing it to Kira. Of course, most of the details were wrong, but it was a very sweet gesture, up until he tried to kiss me.

One time, off-screen where you couldn't see it, Dax passed the {% wiki Bechdel–Wallace test %}.  Well, maybe.  She could've described the bedroom to Kira in the context of a man, which actually sounds a lot more like how they write this character.

> **QUARK**: The Dominion? What's that?
>
> **ZYREE**: Let's just say if you want to do business in the Gamma Quadrant, you have to do business with the Dominion.

Well, *that* doesn't sound like it'll ever matter, right?  I wouldn't even bother noting the name...

## Conclusions

Does "ugh" work as a conclusion?  I didn't even bother with Quark's big {% wiki Gay_and_trans_panic_defense|Gay Panic %} moment, since that had nothing to do with the Federation.

### The Good

Sisko goes out of his way to get a benefit to Bajor, almost certainly with no credit going to him or the Federation.

### The Bad

While we've seen that people have no problems saying insulting things about the Ferengi, they draw the line about saying it to heads of state.  And Dax gives us a strong impression that many see foreign cultures as a source of amusement, rather than groups of peers.  She also returns to the prejudice that Ferengi don't have curious members of society.

Dax also really doubles down on the idea, that we've seen before, that the Federation expects women to comply with and even applaud systemic misogyny.
