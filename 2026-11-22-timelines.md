As a quick note, this fairly short---and late, due to technical difficulties---post started life as a shorter piece for the April 2024 newsletter.  Consider it an open question, especially if anybody knows of useful markup languages and the like of relevance.

![People looking at a large timeline taped on a wall](/blog/assets/16117622897_5e87d3f350_o.png "The writing on the wall...or something")

Every once in a while, I sift through my old notes.  Usually, I hope that something will stand out as worth writing about now, for the blog, or that I can work on with the available technology and attention.  Other times, I mostly sweep through to see if any patterns manifest that I hadn't noticed before.  Recently, I found either an unnoticed pattern, or one that I knew about and subsequently forgot about.

## Please Elaborate

Some readers may know that I did a lot of my early public writing on mailing lists focused on certain table-top superhero role-playing games[^17uVr].  With (at my best) the same sort of attention that I give to [*Star Trek*'s society](/blog/tag/star-trek) on the Thursday posts, I spent a lot of time poring over old comic books, novels, and other media trying to determine the exact limits of things or sequences of events.

[^17uVr]:  Obscure programming languages also featured heavily in those days, but that has nothing to do with this post, or at least nothing that I can think of.

In the latter case, I'd compile the history of a character, a location, or something broader, as a text file with entries structured along the lines of the following hypothetical timeline, a year followed by one or more events.

```text
2019
John starts **Entropy Arbitrage**.

2024
John published the original version of this post in the forty-sixth issue of the newsletter.

2026
John publishes this post.
```

For most of them, I then often did some work fleshing out the past of the world when it has unique aspects, and then trying to extrapolate events into today.  Some, I built from some oddball premise.

For an example of the latter, several pseudo-historical theories[^4RhYUs] propose that the ancient world didn't actually exist, but rather powerful people fabricated the archaeological record to justify their influence, most of it copied from the Middle Ages.  I picked the specific idea of not having two halves of the Roman Empire, and realized that if one did want to treat the year 476 as secretly 1453, and you take it far too seriously by picking a reference year of 2000 or so, then a bunch of fun ideas line up, such as a time-compressed version of Kepler's **Somnium** (about a dream where a boy travels to the Moon) happens to coincide with the Apollo program.  That delighted me enough that I built out a kind of cheap superhero universe with the concept that they needed to defend their timeline from some time-eating villain.

[^4RhYUs]:  Most commonly, you'll see it referenced in the form of "New Chronology," because it got published in popular-science works relatively recently, but variations on the premise date to the eighteenth century.

We can all call it silly, sure, but I enjoyed the idea.

## Why, Though

In many cases, I planned to use those worlds as story or game settings, or something along those lines.  These range from the banal, to the unusable for copyright reasons, to the unusably detailed without some clever redactions and replacements, to the potentially interesting.  The worlds include novels, comic book publishers, and more.  I had the thought to write this post, because I have *dozens* of them.

And it all raises the question:  What the heck do I do with these timelines?  What *can* I do with them?

## Clearest Options

Having sat on some of these for decades, I have given occasional thought to what purpose these things can actually serve beyond my running across them and getting a quick giggle.

For a variety of reasons ranging from inclination to social circles, it doesn't feel like I'd run a role-playing game anytime soon.  That pretty much rules that possibility out.

I could plausibly try to build books---novels or setting references---around the premises, to see how that goes, replacing the dry timelines with a more compelling historical text.  It sounds like fun.  However, it would also take a lot of effort, including formatting things usefully and developing the storytelling aspects.

Without a lot of context, not to mention finding some clever formatting, the timelines would make little to no sense as blog posts.  I have considered it a couple of times, though, especially when some aspect of the described world fit with current events, such as the release of some major film or the passing of a creator whose works inspired some of the material.

Finally, it seems pointless to "dump" them somewhere and hope that someone else makes sense out of them.  I could potentially combine this idea with the book idea, collecting the timelines into a single text with some background, but that seems like it combines all the potential problems of most of these possibilities.

In other words, all the ideas seem...mediocre at best.

## To the End of Time

Regardless of that, this situation struck me as odd enough and interesting enough that I wanted to open the question of potential uses of timelines like these, to a wider audience.  If anybody has similarly given this sort of thing thought, I'd love to hear opinions.

For now, though, they sit in wait, for the next time that I sift through my notes...

Does anybody know of "prior art" to point to in publishing this sort of material usefully?  Do any chronology-specific markup languages exist to make reuse more natural?  Do other options for this material exist?  If not, maybe I'll see what this can look like as a book, despite the effort involved.
