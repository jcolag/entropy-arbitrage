## Necessary Evil

For our usual context, this episode lands between [*Attached*]({% post_url 2025-07-17-attached %}) (maybe, as discussed last week) and [*Force of Nature*]({% post_url 2025-07-24-force-nature %}).  And like a few other episodes recently, we probably won't have much to do, this week.

> **VAATRIK**: I didn't kill him, you know. A lot of people believed it was me. That shape-shifter thought so. But he was wrong.

I don't know why she doesn't have a Wikipedia page, because she had an extensive (but maybe not reported on?) career until around 2000, but if Vaatrik looks familiar, then you might recognize her as Katherine Moffat.  We last saw her as the enemy captain who conned Riker into spreading the gospel of ~~Claude~~ the addictive application with the Frisbee-eating pipes in [*The Game*]({% post_url 2024-06-20-game %}).  She'd also have a relatively small part (unnamed supervisor) in a **Babylon 5** episode near the end of its third season.

> Commence Station Security log. Stardate 47282.5. At the request of Commander Sisko, I will hereafter be recording a daily log of law enforcement affairs. The reason for this exercise is beyond my comprehension, except perhaps that humans have a compulsion to keep records and lists and files, so many, in fact, that they have to invent new ways to store them microscopically, otherwise their records would overrun all known civilization. My own very adequate memory not being good enough for Starfleet, I am pleased to put my voice to this official record of this day. Everything's under control. End log.

I recuse myself from this discussion, as someone who has (as of a few posts ago) cleared three million words on the blog, to say nothing of about thirty years of notes and other projects...

> **QUARK**: Magnesite drops? What are magnesite drops?
>
> **ROM**: A compound that will eat through duranium one on each corner and the panel will fall off.

Yeah, somehow, I don't think that {% wiki magnesite %} does that...

> **QUARK**: I have no idea. Eight names. All of them Bajoran. I want to get a picture of this list before I reseal the box. Go, get me an imager. Go.

For the young'uns reading, back in the '90s, you needed to *go get* cameras, instead of holding up whatever you want and finding someone in technical support to get you a copy of the pictures taken from your neighborhood license plate readers, home security devices, gaming consoles, smart TVs (designed for video calls), computers, creepy sunglasses, toaster ovens...

> **SISKO**: Rom, as a friend, if you know anything that might be helpful, I think you should tell us.

I guess that we should note the "good cop, bad cop" routine.  Even if we accept policing as a necessary role, Sisko's military/political position brings in conflicts that (now that I think about it) parallel Dukat prying into the investigation looking to cart someone off for execution.

> **DUKAT**: Yes, yes, please come in. I'm Gul Dukat. We've met before.

And speaking of him, we last saw Dukat in [*The Homecoming*]({% post_url 2026-07-23-homecoming %}).

> **ROM**: C! It's a C.

The Bajoran culture, whose civilization they told us (as I recall) in [*Emissary*]({% post_url 2026-02-26-emissary-1 %}), predates any human civilization, so it defies reason to suggest that the Bajoran writing that we saw on screen happens to precisely map to the Latin alphabet.  I could believe that the Federation has arbitrarily assigned the Bajoran characters the English names of Latin letters, given how they've treated the local culture so far, but we have no evidence of that.  And sure, they probably did it for *our* benefit, like we can assume that Odo and Rom probably don't speak English when alone, but it seems like a really weird time to employ that trick when inventing letter names seems like low-stakes, given the strong context.

> Security Log, supplemental. The Ferengi holds on to life like it's gold-pressed latinum. Maybe he just doesn't want his brother to get the bar, or maybe he knows he's the only real witness I have.

With so little for me to do in the episode, I end up thinking about the writing.  And could you imagine how much better this episode would've landed if they had consistently used Odo's log like a hard-boiled detective's narration, commenting incessantly on trivialities through tortured metaphors?

> **ODO**: Selling out their own world for a profit. Collaborators. Not even a Ferengi would do that. It explains a lot of things.

...?

Sorry, that vitriol took me by surprise, because what the Hell does this story tell us about Odo *other* than that he served as a collaborator?  Does the word *not* mean "person who worked with the occupying government," the Cardassians in this case?  And sure, the same question arose during [*A Man Alone*]({% post_url 2026-03-19-man-alone %}), so it shouldn't surprise me, but...they keep avoiding an answer, and now wants to indicate that Odo hates collaborators.  And it *especially* feels strange, given what we'll learn around the new year.

## Conclusions

We mostly get back-story for the show, and little else, unless you count the side possibility that the Federation has imposed human descriptions on even the Bajoran language.

### The Bad

Humans have a reputation for obsessive record-keeping that threatens other storage.

Sisko seems to directly participate in law enforcement actions.

Once again, Odo doesn't qualify as a Cardassian collaborator, despite literally collaborating with the Cardassians.
