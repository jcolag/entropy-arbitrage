## 9:02 -- Mon 29 June 2026

{% embed https://images.theconversation.com/files/739570/original/file-20260602-57-4s3f1l.jpg|Alan Montag, a third-generation Iowa farmer, and his son Aden load soybean seed into a planter on May 6, 2026|false| %}

[<i class="fab fa-mastodon"></i>](https://mastodon.social/@jcolag/116833526687351316){: aria-label="Monday morning's post on Mastodon with this article" }
[How everyone pays the cost for patents on seeds, and private companies get rich from keeping them secret](https://theconversation.com/how-everyone-pays-the-cost-for-patents-on-seeds-and-private-companies-get-rich-from-keeping-them-secret-284144) from The Conversation @TheConversationUS@newsie.social

> Even as recently as the 1970s, most plant breeding was carried out by public researchers at government stations and universities, while private companies focused on producing and selling those varieties at scale.

Hashtags:  #Agriculture #IntellectualProperty

Funny thing, I remember about twenty years ago when these patented seeds started hitting the market, whenever I raised the potential problems, people assured me that farmers welcomed this.  They *hated* harvesting seeds from their crops for replanting and not knowing exactly what will grow.  It sounded shady, but it ended the conversation, because people with a firm understanding of patents and enough Internet time to have heated conversations about intellectual property, back then, tended not to have the ability to quickly check the opinions of actual farmers.

And as predicted, that turned out less than truthful, covering for more market consolidation, raising costs for farmers, and stifling investigation into claims made by the seed producers.

I don't get involved in many intellectual property conversations, these days, but patents definitely went wrong, somewhere along the line, probably when software got to have both patents and copyrights, somehow both purely functional and purely artistic expression, and definitely not a series of numbers selected because no other sequence does the job...
