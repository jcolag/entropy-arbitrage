## What Works Well?

The game not only tells a story, but---even though science fiction has often returned to the topic---manages to feel absurdly timely, despite a release about a year before ChatGPT's release.  The concept also does a nice job of justifying the (complete) lack of impact that the player can have on the story.  If you take it as more of a short story with some homework for the player to do, it'll probably go down better than treating it like a game.

People with different tastes might feel different, but I also appreciated the "crossovers," suggesting what the player's situation might look like as an antagonist in those worlds.  In that sense, you can probably link it to a story like [*Collectivity*]({% post_url 2022-04-16-collectivity %}).

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

Maybe I overlooked something, or maybe this comes down to taste, but I found the crafting almost shockingly frustrating for such a small game.  It requires familiarity with five (actually six) shapes, three of which seem too similar to guess which one to use.  Each instance of them gets one of two treatments, which renders them in a *slightly* different color.  Oh, and sometimes the game has the recipe wrong.  And you can't reverse decisions, with limited raw material at the start.  As a result, it takes far too much digging through the interface---the least-clear signalling for any action, you can click on the recipe elements to see the recipes for how to make those---experimentation, and even guesswork to get these right.  And in the early cases, that experiment will require a restart when it fails.

Maybe related, the game has no serious decisions to make, as mentioned.  Even failure only means restarting the scene.  And no matter how cleverly that idea gets woven into the narrative, the lack of decision means that the tedious and flawed building process only serves to slow down getting to each scene, in a way that almost makes me want to put the character on autopilot, so that I can watch the story unfold instead of fussing with their designs.
