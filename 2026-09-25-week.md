## Told You So

This time, I have one reminder of an older post and one post by someone else that didn't really fit anywhere else.

### Recovering from Media

With *Harry Potter* productions back in the discourse for whatever reason---I assume that they released a new thing, but haven't investigated---it seems worth mentioning that I wrote a post where I tried to work out some [guidelines for ethical media consumption]({% post_url 2020-08-02-ethmedia %}#my-state-of-the-art), especially when grappling with creators revealing their terrible ideologies.  To summarize, I came up with the following.

- We can't change the past, so we can still return physical media that we already own.
- Separating art from artist can *only* work if we have the authority (through public licenses or lapses in copyright) to modify the work.
- Acknowledge and try to balance any known harm(s) done; if we can afford to subscribe to a big service, then we can afford to donate (at least) that much money every month to a relevant charity or to buy from independent artists in the harmed demographic.
- Treat a single accusation as grounds for suspicion, not abandonment, but each subsequent accusation counts against us as much as it does the accused, if we ignore them.
- Judge work by who its community rushes to protect.
- Don't make any media (that we didn't create) part of our personalities.

Admittedly, it bothers me that I haven't seen anything better than that.  I can't find it---annoying, because I could've sworn that it came up in that post---but somewhere, I mentioned that the impression that a work might have made on you *does* represent something that you needed from it, but you fulfill your "obligations" to the work by taking those beneficial ideas forward, not by trying to excuse bad behavior.

### Web Browsers

As I said, I didn't write this, but some of you might note that I have followed the [Base Browser Project](https://codeberg.org/basebrowserproject/organization)---a fork of Firefox that deletes all the problematic features---so I enjoyed seeing the founder's musings on [The Future of Web Browsers](https://sarahjamielewis.com/log/2026/future-of-web-browsers.html).  I don't necessarily agree with everything, but I still find it useful to see people trying to work through what these spaces *could* look like, instead of insisting that we've ever gotten it right.
