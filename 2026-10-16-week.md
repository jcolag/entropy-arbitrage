## Told You So

As mentioned, I occasionally want to take the time to mention that I have already written about some current topics.  Three times over six weeks makes the Friday posts the idea's home, I guess.

With election season on us in the United States, I should point to my posts on [voting]({% post_url 2020-10-11-vote %}) and why [political parties don't reflect you]({% post_url 2020-08-30-parties %}).  To let you in on the secret, they don't reflect your beliefs because you don't get involved to *contribute* your beliefs.

Since the new *Harry Potter* thing has persisted in the news, along with people (rightly) pointing out that supporting it supports a deliberate and active anti-transgender agenda, some of you might want to dust off my musings on [ethical media consumption]({% post_url 2020-08-02-ethmedia %}).  I have almost no exposure to *Potter* itself, so I didn't have that in mind, but I have enjoyed plenty of media that turned out to raise the profile and improve the careers of some horrible artist.  As such, I set the following rules for myself as a start.

- If I already own it on physical media, it doesn't count, because I have already done the damage and the artists receive no further benefit.
- Work without a copyright or available under the terms of a Free Culture license also doesn't count, because it doesn't really belong to the artist anymore.
- Acknowledge and try to balance any harm done by spending at least as much money and attention on independent artists as harmful corporate infrastructure.
- A single accusation could come from an honest misunderstanding, but it usually doesn't, and the chances decrease dramatically with every further accusation.
- Judge works by which parties the publishers and community protect, whether they choose the powerful, the victims, or some anonymous third party.
- Don't make your media part of your identity.

Especially with the Warner Brothers/Paramount merger under right-wing control, on the heels of the Disney/Fox merger (spinning off a new Fox, remember), it makes a lot of sense to drop the streaming services.  If you can't, though, you can feel free to start with my list.

Oh, and as Big AI starts their campaign agitating for AI rights (but not human rights), some might want to know that I wrote a post this spring trying to gesture at this eventuality (without saying so) by pointing out that [corporate personhood *should* mean abolishing corporate ownership]({% post_url 2026-05-31-free-corporation %}).  The same applies now:  If Anthropic believes that Claude has developed consciousness and feelings, then we can't allow them to *own and sell access to* Claude, because that exploitation would---by their own admission, even if we don't believe it---violate some fairly serious laws.

And speaking of Big AI, several people suggested that I exaggerated when I presented [Linus Torvalds defending AI usage]({% post_url 2026-07-12-free-culture-creators-1 %}) over the summer, because he *only* uses it for debugging, never programming.  Well, [Torvalds has started submitting vibe-coded kernel code](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Linus-Torvalds-Claude-Code).  Oh, and they want to [remove LLM attributions](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Linux-Dropping-Assisted-By-Req) from commits, because they allegedly don't see any value to that information.  Those stories accelerate my plans, which I note progress on in the [developer diary posts](/blog/tag/dev-journal), to move off Linux before this careless behavior starts causing problems.
