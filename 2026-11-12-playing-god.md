## Playing God

For our usual context, this episode lands between [*Eye of the Beholder*]({% post_url 2025-09-25-eye-beholder %}) and (after almost a month) [*Genesis*]({% post_url 2025-10-02-genesis %}).  Over in the **Babylon 5** world, it sits between *The Parliament of Dreams* and *Mind War*, plus *The War Prayer* and *And the Sky Full of Stars* during *Star Trek*'s little spring hiatus.

> **ARJIN**: Oh, you've already been helpful. Just hearing about Dax makes me feel a little less nervous about the whole thing.

If our candidate looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Geoffrey_Blake_(actor)|Geoffrey Blake %}, known primarily for gigs in Tom Hanks films, but maybe most recognizable as Jodie Foster's father in **Contact**.

> **QUARK**: You can't quit now. You just won. ...Female!

We don't see Dax's reaction, but it does seem interesting that he hurls her gender at her as an insult.  I've mentioned before that a lot of my distaste for Dax comes from the "not like other girls" concept, which carries a certain amount (a lot, in fact) of misogyny by suggesting that women have some inherent problem that she has overcome.

The first scene after the credits does this even more, too.  She wrestles before work, with a suggestion of---*gasp*---extramarital sex.  And she doesn't need people to respect her rank or legitimacy, and so forth.

> **SISKO**: Phasers on stun, Mister O'Brien. I want those voles taken alive.

The writers wouldn't have likely known this, but as it turns out, studies have since shown that (our world's) voles comfort individuals in pain, suggesting a level of empathy and intelligence.  I realize that they meant the line to get a laugh, and it does especially with Sisko's military intonation, but yeah, in situations where not killing them works, maybe don't kill them.

That said---I needed to look a lot up when a couple got into my house and caused problems---they also tend to not survive long after releasing them back to the outdoors.  Combined with their breeding, destruction, and possible disease vectors, that tends to rule out live-traps to release outside once they get indoors, leaving you with as humane a killing as you can manage or ceding the building to them, unless you have the capacity to safely cage and care for them.

> **DAX**: Really? I've never seen a Cardassian vole.

I mean...*{% wiki vole %}*, as in the field mouse.  The word sounds weird, but we have all seen plenty of the little guys.  Presumably this sort looks similar, otherwise why call it that?  Maybe it has some exoskeleton-like aspects.  We see it later, and...yeah.  Imagine going to someone's house and asking them to show you the squirrels in the area.  Sure, you have squirrels (to pick a fairly common animal) everywhere back home, but you've never seen squirrels *there*.

And I call this out beyond the oddity, because the sequence taps into that same misogyny of trying to distinguish her from "normal" women by her I-guess-uncanny ability to accept the existence of vermin without panicking.

Not for our purposes, but this raises an odd question:  Do Cardassian voles have some familial relationship with Earth voles?  [*The Chase*]({% post_url 2025-04-03-chase %}) informed us that most of the cultures that we know got seeded around the galaxy from some original stock.  Did they also have a thing for mice?  Do rodents have a **The Great Mouse Detective**-style parallel version of **TNG** where their crew met a tiny Salome Jens with big ears talking about the ancient plan to hide (maze-like) navigation information in DNA to draw all the surviving cultures together?

And yes, we have seen the "[Alien Planet] [Earth animal]" construction before, mostly in this series and usually involving Odo.  Double-checking episodes that we have already covered, [**The Wrath of Khan**]({% post_url 2022-03-24-twok %}) and [**The Undiscovered Country**]({% post_url 2022-04-21-tuc %}) both refer to an extraterrestrial bat.  [*The Schizoid Man*]({% post_url 2022-12-29-schizoid-man %}) referred to an extraterrestrial ox.  [*Past Prologue*]({% post_url 2026-03-12-past-prologue %}) has the mention of the extraterrestrial ostrich that drowns itself.  [*If Wishes Were Horses*]({% post_url 2026-06-11-if-wishes-were-horses %}) had two imaginary extraterrestrial jackdaws, while Quark refers to Odo as an extraterrestrial hawk.  Maybe this goes beyond voles, then, or maybe they only use the words as analogies, but I'd totally watch a *Star Trek* series with mice in little spandex uniforms.

> **DAX**: A self-exiled Romulan. I collect forgotten composers. Feel like taking the controls?

How did music not from 1930s Earth slip through production?  Or maybe that rule only applied to the *Enterprise*-D...

> **CHEF**: Ak'la bella doo. Bella ak'la doo. La suhm, La suhm, L'kahtra la suhm.

You no doubt remember the unnamed chef and {% wiki Ron_Taylor_(actor)|Ron Taylor %} from his other appearance in [*Melora*]({% post_url 2026-08-27-melora %}).  Also, I can't say that I ever considered the concertina a likely Klingon musical instrument.

> **DAX**: Got me. It's from Julian. He called it the solution you've been looking for.
>
> **O'BRIEN**: *It worked in Hamlin*. Very funny.

C'mon, show, don't make me start siding with Bashir...

> **DAX**: Yes, but I'm not Curzon Dax. And I won't do to him what Curzon did to me.

Didn't she already do that by pushing him through the montage of wacky adventures?  She didn't do it harshly, but that seemed *entirely* designed to scare him off, or at least haze/bully him.

> **DAX**: I understand why you feel that way. But this isn't about me. This is about the standards for Trill hosts. The opportunity is too rare and too important to waste on the wrong candidate.

I have mentioned a couple of times, most recently last week in [*Shadowplay*]({% post_url 2026-11-05-shadowplay %}) that Dax seems to have a tendency to not see others as fully formed *people*, treating everyone as some idle amusement.  This elitism seems to bolster that.

> **ODO**: I don't step on ants, Major. Just because we don't understand a lifeform doesn't mean we can destroy it.

Of all the people in the room, *Odo* needs to say this?  Don't the rest of them have ethical obligations that they'd need to bring up, if not personal objections?

> Personal log, supplemental. One hour. One hour to make a decision that could mean the life or death of a civilization or the end to our own. My mind keeps going back to the Borg. How I despised their indifference as they tried to exterminate us, and I have to ask myself, would I be any different if I destroyed another universe to preserve my own?

How much do the rank-and-file know about the Borg?  I suppose that we have the benefit of [**First Contact**]({% post_url 2025-12-25-first-contact-2 %})---still a while in this episode's future---where Picard made it fairly clear that the Borg attacked because they did seem to care once they understood that the ships carried intelligent creatures, but a combination of desperation to get to Earth and Picard's aggression or sabotage forced them to blow through the defensive lines.  But even by this point, with all relevant **TNG** episodes behind them, Starfleet should no longer see them as the indifferent ravagers that seemed to present in [*Q Who?*]({% post_url 2023-03-09-q-who %}).  Have the top brass hidden that information so that officers won't hesitate to shoot on sight?

And this sounds like a vague thought experiment, but since we'll see in the new year that this show will spend a lot of time with a military force considered by *both sides* of the conflict to only have the capacity for killing, it seems like a worthwhile question to consider how contrary information spreads in their society.

> **SISKO**: You're in love with a dabo girl?
>
> ...
>
> **JAKE**: You mean you would have understood?
>
> **SISKO**: Of course not! It's Mardah, isn't it? The one you've been tutoring in entomology.

First, I love how this scene plays out.

However, does Sisko only really object to her *job*?  I feel like any other parent would start with the age gap, or the fact that a kid that age might not know how to distinguish between an attraction and persistent romantic feelings.  Especially if she studies entomology, it seems inappropriate to put much focus on her current job at all.

I tried to think of our world's equivalent to "dabo girl" outside of casinos, and could only come up with "spokesperson for a gambling app," which seems so much more destructive than board game cheerleader.

> **DAX**: Take us to fifty kph.
>
> **ARJIN**: Confirmed. Seventeen seconds to the wormhole.

Wait.  I realize that this has nothing to do with working out the Federation's culture, even less than the vole thing, but assuming that "kph" in this context means kilometers per hour as it would for us, and not some novel interstellar unit, fifty thousand means moving means a little over eight hundred kilometers per minute, or shy of fourteen kilometers per second.  If it takes seventeen seconds to cover the distance, that gives us two hundred thirty-six kilometers, about twice the depth of Earth's atmosphere or almost nine times around the Large Hadron Collider.  That...sounds unsafe to me, but I suppose that you eventually need to place a bet on the potential cone of damage if something dangerous spills out of the wormhole or how to defend if somebody attacks the area.

> **DAX**: This ship is only fourteen meters wide. That gives you over two meters to work with. Verteron levels are red-lining. Five seconds to Gamma.

I guess that I might as well do the math on this, too, since I brought up [*Paradise*]({% post_url 2026-10-29-paradise %}) making a point that they could carry everyone in the colony back to the station.

It looks to me like they have a frame roughly twice as long as wide, and the nacelles and struts take up about half the width, meaning that we have an approximate rectangle for the main body, seven by twenty-eight meters, maybe a bit shorter, because they have that deep "dashboard" in the cockpits.  Looking up occupancy load, different kinds of space (unsurprisingly) have different loads.

- Warehouse space would have a maximum occupancy of only six people for the two thousand (or so) square feet; I don't know why, since warehouses seem designed for flexible use of space.
- Residential spaces fit ten.
- Office or industrial space can accommodate thirteen people.
- Examination rooms in a medical setting can fit about twenty people.
- Retail space bumps that to thirty-three.
- Classrooms can manage a hundred.
- Restaurant seating and assembly seating jumps to around one hundred thirty-three.
- Standing-only assembly space jumps again to about four hundred.

Presumably, the runabouts would actually fit less, because the bulkheads and equipment take up space, and vehicles necessarily have spaces where you shouldn't sit/stand for safety reasons.  But yes, I suppose that they could take even hundreds of passengers, though at some point, each only has about five square feet to move around in, so they probably won't enjoy the trip, and disembarking sounds like a nightmare.

> **DAX**: I'm not Curzon.

Really, though?  As I see it, she deliberately made him feel awkward, then made him feel miserable, and pointed out that he'd never make it through in his current condition, then reluctantly accepted him.  Maybe Curzon did worse, but despite the claimed trauma, Dax's story only actually *tells* us that she felt bad because he tried to shock her out of her bookworm mentality, and the evidence of his passing the symbiote along suggests that he did so because he knew that she wouldn't get any further without that push...like Dax did in this episode.

## Conclusions

I feel like this episode weirdly became more about measurements and voles than anything else, which I feel improves it from focusing on Dax's self-esteem, but still...

### The Good

They at least try to avoid exterminating rodent infestations.

### The Bad

The episode gives us a lot of misogyny in its attempts to highlight Dax as above the criticisms.  Quark tries to play on it.  Dax repeats the argument multiple times.

While the crew would apparently prefer not to destroy an entire universe, they leave it to the non-Federation citizens to debate the morality of doing so.  And maybe similarly, word hasn't seemed to have spread in Starfleet that the Borg don't have the disinterested stance originally assumed.

Sisko's reaction to Jake's first romance at least implies a lot of classism.
