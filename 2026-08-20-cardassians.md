## Cardassians

For our usual context, this episode lands between [*Phantasms*]({% post_url 2025-07-03-phantasms %}) and [*Dark Page*]({% post_url 2025-07-10-dark-page %}).  And everybody rejoice, because this episode features the return of...

> **GARAK**: Tarkalean tea again, Doctor?

We met Garak in [*Past Prologue*]({% post_url 2026-03-12-past-prologue %}), and then weirdly ignored him for a year.

> **GARAK**: I hope I'm not intruding, but I couldn't help noticing what a handsome young man you have here. Argh!

Yeah...don't say things like this, ever?  Having someone bite your hand seems like a pretty restrained reaction, honestly.

> **DAX**: Another unsatisfied customer?
>
> **O'BRIEN**: He always cuts the pants too long.

Figures that the one time that O'Brien makes me giggle, he does so in service of blowing off a foreign national getting attacked only *two weeks* (our time, at least) after a racist uprising put all their lives in danger.

> **SISKO**: I'm surprised how quickly the news reached you, Dukat. I only heard about it myself a minute ago.

Do we want to explore this?  Soon enough, we'll find out that Garak wouldn't report this to anybody.  Does Dukat have spies on the station?  Surveillance?  Oh, we've already moved on?  Never mind, then...

> **SISKO**: The Cardassians are suggesting that the Bajorans are raising these orphans to hate their own people.

This seems like a shocking thing for Sisko to present to the father as an accusation, no?  Said father gets a nice little speech out of the deal explaining that, you know, the Cardassians didn't exactly treat Bajor kindly, and you can't really {% wiki 1776_Commission|whitewash ethnic cleansing and other oppression %} and still call it history...

> **PROKA**: It is not true. It is not true. Rugal will tell you. We have not mistreated him. Is this some Cardassian...?

This seems to have gotten worse, because now we have Sisko accusing him of child abuse, on the guesswork from a representative from an adversarial foreign government.

Oh, also, if Proka---who has shown up before and had lines, but none of use---looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Terrence Evans %}, who we last saw as one of the farmhands in [*Progress*]({% post_url 2026-06-04-progress %}).

> **SISKO**: There'll be ample opportunity to go over the matter in detail, but during the investigation, we'd like for Rugal to stay with our schoolteacher, Mrs O'Brien. She's expecting him now.

Wait.  To make sure that the kid gets a balanced education, they want him to learn from Keiko, who spent [*In the Hands of the Prophets*]({% post_url 2026-07-09-in-hands-prophets %}) making sure that everybody knew that she had absolutely no interest in respecting anybody else's culture...?

> **SISKO**: Then I'll have to call Security and have them remove him from your quarters. I don't think either of us wants that.

Did I miss a scene where this all became justified?  Zolan---played by {% wiki Dion Anderson %}, who passed earlier this year---seemed to describe bullying, not domestic abuse.  This feels utterly unhinged, like the episode wants to rush the family separation and not put much thought into it.

> **O'BRIEN**: Gentle was bred out of these Cardassians a long time ago.
>
> **KEIKO**: You know, that was a very ugly thing you just said.

Well, *that* saved me some trouble.  Thanks, Keiko...

> **KEIKO**: Rugal, dinner's ready. I hope you like it.

Sadly, after calling out Miles, they *don't* have anything say anything about Keiko's horror at someone praying at her table.  And look, *I* tend to feel awkward around things like that, but I don't stop everything and gape.

> **O'BRIEN**: Keiko, darling, what is this?

O'Brien, as I recall, had pretty much the same reaction to Japanese food in [*The Wounded*]({% post_url 2024-01-25-wounded %})...

> **KEIKO**: I found some Cardassian recipes in the memory bank of our food replicator. It's a stew, made with Cardassian zabu meat. I thought Rugal might enjoy a dish that's popular on his own world.

It *doesn't* come from his world, though, does it?  The Cardassians occupied Bajor for something like fifty years, and they've given no indication that the kid had any life prior to Bajor.  This seems to fit with the O'Briens' backgrounds, though, where they've made it abundantly clear in episodes such as [*Data's Day*]({% post_url 2024-01-18-data-s-day %}) that Keiko and Miles grew up in Japan and Ireland, respectively, with few if any people outside their ethnicity, whereas if you handed me Italian food as something "from my homeland," I'd laugh you out of the room, because I come from Long Island, that big fish-looking sandbar that sticks out of New York City.

> **O'BRIEN**: He'll understand that. You're a Cardassian. They should've taken you home when they left.

Again, they don't get the idea that he absolutely didn't grow up there.

> **O'BRIEN**: That's not what I meant. There's nothing wrong with being a Cardassian.

He *totally* meant that.

> **O'BRIEN**: Come on, even I got my bottom whacked by my Dad once or twice.

In defense of the elder O'Brien, I think that we've all wanted to deck him by now...

> **O'BRIEN**: Well, you can't judge a whole race of people. You can't hate all Cardassians or all Klingons or all humans. I've met some Cardassians I didn't like, and I've met some I did. Like you.

Seriously?  *O'Brien* lectures us on pluralism, in the same breath that he *literally judged a whole race of people* and insisted that the kid couldn't possibly want to live in the home where he has grown up?  Also, not to belabor the point, but he also only decided to like the kid five seconds ago or so, maybe at dinner when they had a meet-cute over both hating foreign food.  And I assure you that bonding over bigotry doesn't actually mean that you overcame your bigotry.

> **DEELA**: Yes, the Bajorans opened their hearts to many abandoned children.

If Deela looks familiar, then you might recognize her as {% wiki Karen Hensel %}, not a big name, but we last saw her as the admiral in [*Unification*]({% post_url 2024-06-27-unification-part-1 %}).

> **ASHA**: Excuse me, sir. Have you come to take us back to Cardassia?

Let me get this straight:  When Dukat vaguely insinuates child abuse, they willfully misinterpret some pilot's comment about "them" to find a way to deport one child back to Cardassia.  But when a crowd of children in a home ask for help repatriating, after seeing Bajoran kids run from a Cardassian and one of the volunteers strongly imply her own hatred, this doesn't sound relevant to them?  Bashir will mention their plight in the next scene, but only abstractly and in passing.

> **GARAK**: I believe in coincidences. Coincidences happen every day. But I don't *trust* coincidences.

Oh, this has nothing to do with the project, but I can't *not* include such a fantastic line.

> **PA'DAR**: I am Kotan Pa'Dar.

If Pa'Dar seems familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Robert Mandan %}, almost certainly best known as part of the main cast on **Soap**, though he had much more of a career than that.

> **O'BRIEN**: We'd do almost anything to protect our families. I have a daughter myself. She's four, the same age your son was when you lost him.

They do anything, except contact them.  O'Brien mentioned a father in this episode and two brothers last week in [*Invasive Procedures*]({% post_url 2026-08-13-invasive-procedures %}), but apparently doesn't talk to them...

> **PA'DAR**: No, Commander. You are a father too. You would be acceptable to me.

It seems worth mentioning that, for all the noise that they make about the adversarial Cardassians, when we actually spend time with them in episodes like this one and *The Wounded*, they come off as more normal and even-tempered than most of the Federation folks, other than the military leaders.

> **BASHIR**: Isn't there an inquiry about to begin concerning the Cardassians who were involved in the attempted military coup on Bajor?

Well, at least some part of this episode still remembers that *The Siege* only aired a couple of weeks ago.  If only they had remembered the racism...

> Station log, stardate 47178.3. After long and difficult deliberations, I have decided to allow Pa'Dar to take his son back to Cardassia. Although I am convinced his Bajoran foster parents treated him with love, Rugal has been the clear victim in this conspiracy. I believe it's time for his healing to begin.

That seems like the worst decision for everybody, no?  Despite assurances that Dukat will keep the incident quiet, Pa'Dar will still need to explain how his son came back from the dead *and* he only has a few months before trying to care for a teenager who literally hates him and everything he stands for, even before getting to the resentment.  Rugal gets torn from the only home and family that he can remember, people and places who he cares about, to live in an entirely alien culture that he knows primarily from the genocidal occupation that endangered everybody who he loves.  Proka loses his child to a war criminal for no clear reason.  It almost certainly undermines Sisko's relationship with Bajor to suggest that their people can't reasonably protect foreign children and integrate them into society.  We'll find out in a moment that it probably won't help the other abandoned kids.  It foils Dukat's plan to coerce one potential investigator of his record, but with no indication of how many other versions of this plan he has rolling or whether he might take a scorched-Earth approach when pressed.

I could imagine some shared custody arrangement, since (accidentally) separated families do deserve the chance to reconnect, absent any evidence or memories of abuse, even if Rugal opposes it at the moment.  But this seems wildly out of step unless we believe in strict racial segregation.  I don't see any benefit without presuming deep racism.

> **SISKO**: Perhaps you'll use your influence to help other Cardassian orphans on Bajor.

Oh, yeah, the people who actually *wanted* to leave.  I almost forgot about them, because they somehow had nothing to do with the plot.

## Conclusions

The show wandered pretty far off the path, which usually pans out well for us...

### The Bad

Almost everybody actually makes time for racism.  They dismiss violence, because it happens to a foreigner.  They treat vague insinuations like well-founded accusations.  We see the idea that people should have some affinity for and even a positive view of the government most closely associated with their ethnicity.  O'Brien openly spouts racist remarks, even as he claims that people can't judge an entire culture.  The see foreign foods as an imposition.  And they completely ignore a crowd of Cardassian war orphans in apparent distress.  The episode even makes prior rhetoric about Cardassians seem far more deliberately racist by showing quite a few Cardassians not engaging in contrived plots.  However, they seem to have forgotten about the ethnic purity movement that caused them trouble for multiple episodes, or no longer see it as worrisome.

Similarly, we still see a discomfort around or distaste for Bajoran religious practices.

Everybody also seems completely incurious about how a foreign military commander seems to get word about local incidents at around the same time as it takes to ride the elevator.

Children still sometimes face corporal punishment as a routine sort of discipline.

### The Weird

They have revived the idea of humans claiming tight relationships with their family despite not seeming to have any interest in family members.
