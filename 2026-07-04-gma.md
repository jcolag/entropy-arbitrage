## Galactic Mutant Animals

The creator describes the as follows.

> Galactic Mutant Animals is a comic I drew when I was twelve (~1993). Probably not the best comic you'll read today, but if somewhere a kid sees it & wants to create its own comic I'll be happy. Sorry you have to speak French to understand all the hilarious jokes.

The creator also provides this biography.

> I draw on my spare time, and love sharing my art under 'libre'/free license ; all my works use CC-BY-SA.
>
> I love monsters.

Occasionally, I see the creator posting new art on Mastodon, often the monsters, which led me here.

As a programming note, during the week, I decided to break the comic into *four* posts instead of the previously announced two.  Why?  It occurred to me as I started reading that not only have I let my French get rusty over the decades, but the comic has no text transcript, leaving me to decipher the scanned handwriting in a project where the creator definitely didn't prioritize the lettering.  And it looks like the comic has an ambitious plot, too, slowing reading down even further.  Add in the heat wave coming through here slowing *me* down, and it made more sense to slow this one down, even if it means having less to say for almost a month.

## What Works Well?

While I couldn't always follow the action without carefully translating the dialogue, the visual action looks nice.

And I have to admit that the comic's enthusiasm feels infectious.  The world has a bunch of goofy ideas and tries to carry them through in interesting ways.

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

We spend a *lot* of time on dry exposition, detailing plans and weapons and characters.  And then, in case we didn't catch it the first time, the comic has little "intermissions" (my term, not theirs) with pages of reference material between stories.  Not explained, though?  We don't find out why evolving animals has resulted in what sounds like a state of total war, with all of each society dedicated to wiping out the other.

And maybe related, as mentioned in the previous section, it doesn't always seem at all clear what actually *happens* in these stories.  It especially seems tough to follow with phrases that recur to a degree where I'd want to read a joke into it, but can't find anything funny about the different electrical energy/electromagnetic energy/other electrical-type energies come up.

And while I can't really blame a young person in 1993 for not coming to the right conclusion for 2026, I do want to mention that the comic does have exactly the accessibility problems that an amateur paper comic would.  You read the handwritten text visually or not at all, with no descriptions of the comic.  Someone could fix that, and I might even release my (almost certainly error-riddled) transcription of the text for anybody who needs it (though I don't know what use that serves if not associated with the comic itself), but if you like it enough to recommend it, make sure that you don't recommend it to someone with vision or language problems who won't get anything out of it.

## Opportunities

I don't see any openings to contribute to or work with the creator, especially not on a comic from over thirty years ago.

## What's Adaptable?

We get the broad setting in 2894 that includes all(?) animals evolved into humanoid form, and---like I mentioned---each of the two stories in this section has a set of encyclopedia-style pages following them, to describe the characters and their ships and weapons.

## Six Years Hence:   Forgotten

Continuing our look at projects that we discussed early in the book club, we come to [*Forgotten*]({% post_url 2020-07-04-forgotten %}).  And in this case, I don't know that I have much to say about it.

If I remember those days well enough---admittedly, I don't specifically recall---I tried out their other MIT-licensed project, [**Sonic OC 7**](https://effigysoftworks.itch.io/sonic-oc-7), which has a similar angle of a short vignette with a forgotten creature roaming a vintage computer.  And it didn't change my opinion of **Forgotten** as competent and interesting, but maybe too sparse for its story.

Since then, or actually since years before the post, the small Aether Interactive studio has disbanded, but the (lead?) developer released a visual novel in January, though I haven't investigated it at all.
