## Framboise ou Chocolat

The creators describe the film as follows.

> Produced by Ethic Cinema. This short film was made using only free license software. To know more visit: [https://ethiccinema.org](https://ethiccinema.org "https://ethiccinema.org")
>
> "Framboise ou Chocolat" (raspberry or chocolate) offers a glimpse into the life of Salomé (Fannie Lineros). This young woman finds herself at a loss when she has to pick an ice cream flavor. Behind the raspberry and the chocolate, two memories of two different men are revealed, which one should Salomé choose?
>
> Actors:  
> Salomé: Fanny Lineros  
> Ice cream seller: Fabienne Welter  
> Romuald (raspberry): Adrien Guitton  
> Edouard (chocolate): Mathieu Carle
>
> This short film was based a scenario by Anne-Flore Barletta, was delicately crafted by the expert eye of director François Téchené, the skillful hands of director of photography Thierry Cazorla and by the attentive ear of the sound engineer Etienne Racary.
>
> Make-up, sound recording and much more: Ysaline Farraj  
> Hairdresser: Julien Humeau  
> Assistant director: Valentin Téchené  
> Production Assistant: Audrey Lefevre  
> Music: "Memory Blank" et "System Shutdown" par Airglow
>
> To produce this short film the team of Ethic Cinema has used:  
> Gimp, Kdenlive, Blender, Ardour, Subtitles Workshop, Visualsubsync
>
> Format: Short film, 4:15 minutes.

They translated that one, so you don't even need to trust my decaying grasp of French, this time around...

However, I'd actually like to talk more about the fact that the Ethic Cinema project has three rules.

> - **Create**:  A film labeled "ETHICAL FILM MAKING" is mainly made with **{ wiki Free_software#Definition_and_the_Four_Freedoms|Free/Libre }** technologies.
> - **Contribute**:  In order to maintain the collaborative harmony, a production labeled "ETHICAL FILM MAKING" technically and/or financially **supports** the creation or development of one or more **Free/Libre technologies**.
> - **Share**:  Any creation and development within an "ETHICAL FILM MAKING" project is released under a **Free/Libre license** that is approved as **{% wiki Definition_of_Free_Cultural_Works|Free Cultural Works }**

It probably won't come as much of a surprise that I, as someone who thinks maybe-too-much about what Free Culture "means," find this an important part of their undertaking.

## What Works Well?

A silly thing to focus on, but I did grin at using the Ethic Cinema logo as the logo for the ice cream stand.

Especially for such a short story, it impresses me how small a language barrier we actually have, here.  The second flashback comes off as less clear than the others, and you might not catch the *specific* jokes in the first one illustrating the protagonist's terrible ex, but you get a good sense of the relationships through their tone and body language.  And then actually paying attention to the dialogue or subtitles, and catching that the ex plans to spend the summer building an arcade cabinet and wonders where she'll go on her vacation, that only improves on an already excellent scene.

Finally, this also seems like a point barely worth mentioning, but since so many other projects seem to skip this step, it feels worth it.

![The final screen in the closing credits, showing the license, the logos of the technologies involves, and their own logo flanked by that of Creative Commons and the old Free Cultural Works seal](/blog/assets/framboise-chocolate-license.png "It doesn't take more than this, and you can even get away with less.")

You see it, right?  For the first time in a while, we have a film that actually identifies its license *inside* the film, instead of making us track down metadata stored somewhere else.  Calling out the Free Software tools used, as big studio productions credit the technologies that they use, also makes this feel more appropriate.

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

The sales clerk strikes me as unnecessarily abrasive, drawing attention to her role as a plot device pulling the protagonist along, rather than as a character.  She keeps trying to rush our main character, but I can't see any reason to do so, with no line behind her and sufficient evidence that the stand doesn't move throughout the day.
