## 9:05 -- Mon 26 October 2026

{% embed https://images.theconversation.com/files/766770/original/file-20261016-50-nyv1w2.jpg|A page of a standardized test with numbers and bubbles next to it, with letters inside the circles|true| %}

[<i class="fab fa-mastodon"></i>](https://mastodon.social/@jcolag/117507352221681241){: aria-label="Monday morning's post on Mastodon with this article" }
[Students take up to 88 standardized tests before high school, but more testing doesn’t improve learning](https://theconversation.com/students-take-up-to-88-standardized-tests-before-high-school-but-more-testing-doesnt-improve-learning-294123) from The Conversation @TheConversationUS@newsie.social

> Teachers estimated spending an average of 14 out of 180 school days preparing students for these exams, whether it be reviewing the question formats students see on tests or teaching test-taking strategies.

Hashtags:  #Education

I can remember teachers weighing the lack of effort in grading against the lack of information that the grade provides.  And the fact that "test-taking strategies" can exist at all (beyond "learn the material") tells you everything that you need to know about the educational value of the tests, doesn't it?

It actually reminds me of kindergarten.  Someone got it into their heads to give us all IQ tests for some reason.  The teacher somehow got us all to sit and pay attention long enough to explain (not in these words) that we shouldn't take any result seriously, because of the class-based assumptions in especially the verbal questions.  They gave the specific example of a question *wanting* everybody to associate the word "cup" most closely with "saucer," even though tea sets with cups and saucers don't actually fit into the normal experience of middle-class children.

In that vein (and almost certainly what the teacher had on their mind), if you have a history of good test grades and want to get a sense of what it feels like to sit on the other side of the cultural divide, I strongly recommend digging up a copy of the {% wiki Chitling Test %} and taking it as seriously as you would cups and saucers.
