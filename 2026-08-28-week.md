## 6:10 PM -- Tue 25 August 2026

[<i class="fab fa-mastodon"></i> Posted to Mastodon](https://mastodon.social/@jcolag/117158432383695526)

> Way back when news slipped out that #DollyParton helped fund at least one of the COVID-19 vaccines, I joked that the Internet wasted decades on "Chuck Norris Facts" trying to make him into some sort of folk hero, when nobody needs to exaggerate Parton.
>
> Both died this year, and my opinion hasn't changed. Only one of the two has gotten immediately mourned beyond "celebrity"...

I don't remember *where* I posted the original quip, but I still sometimes giggle about it.  If someone makes some wild claim about Parton's abilities or resilience, it comes off as funny in its near-believability, unlike with Norris where it often felt like a non sequitur or at least not recognizing the difference between characters and performers.

Some of you might know that I often find myself grumping about how we handle celebrity deaths, which I think relates to this.  When people like Harry Belafonte and Sidney Poitier died, it got a mention in the news crawls and some vague mention of their non-acting work, and people moved on.  By contrast, Betty White got seemingly endless eulogies.  People took time out to talk about Chuck Norris, of all people.  At least Dolly Parton seems to get some of the attention that she deserves.

Although even here, it (almost) shocks me to hear the difference in emotion in people's voices.  Parton (academic voice) wrote a significant portion of popular music across genres, acted in beloved films, produced powerful television, funded medical research, fought illiteracy by sending books to children, and much more.  But Tim Curry (choking up) starred in high-profile movies.  And don't get me wrong, here.  Curry's presence in a film's cast often predisposes me to checking it out, *but* the idea that **Clue** or **Muppet Treasure Island** had so much more of an impact on people than (even footing) **9 to 5** and **Steel Magnolias**, let alone all her other work, seems wild, unless we frame it through systemic misogyny or "thing that I saw once as a child" tops everything else.
