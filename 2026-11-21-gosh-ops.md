## GOSH Ops

The creator describes the book as follows...plus or minus English dialects.

> You, the top spy for the Global Organization for Secret Hi-jinks, have been sent to infiltrate the enemy's base. GOSH’s success depends on yours. Go to it, Agent!
>
> This game is for anyone who finds themselves in a shopping mall or a shopping center and wants to entertain themselves. While you're there, play this little game!
>
> First, determine exactly what GOSH have sent you to do. Are you there to steal data or items, install bugs, or sabotage infrastructure? You’ll have multiple small targets rather than one big one.
>
> In this game, you will try to achieve your goal by secretly interacting with various targets. In your fiction, targets are related to your goal and may be corporate servers, archives, security panels, etc. In real life, targets are represented by shops or other named public places in this shopping mall. You’ll need to infiltrate a number of these targets to complete your mission!

If Langridge's name sounds familiar, then you might recognize him from (usually Linux-centric) podcasts, web standards work, former association with Canonical, a couple of books, and so forth.  I have loosely followed his work for years, and discovered this particular project when he posted about it on Mastodon.

## What Works Well?

Other people might disagree, but I admittedly got a quick laugh when I realized that, if you ignore spacing, punctuation, and capitalization, then you can read the title in two ways, highly appropriate the premise of the game.

I also find the concept of the game, at least, highly amusing.  We don't get many automated scavenger hunts out in the ordinary world.  I don't know if I imagine myself heading out to *play*, but I suppose that if I ever find myself stuck, I'll consider it.

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

The letter-indexing could probably use more clarity.  I understand the intent[^JScgvT], but I needed to read through the example to get the point, because two pages each refer to the required rule as already known, despite it never getting described.

[^JScgvT]:  For anybody who gets lost reading through, you take the fourth letter of the first text.  Then, convert that letter to its numerical position in the alphabet (A = 1, B = 2, and so on to Z = 26, for English), calling that the *index*.  Finally, from the second text, take the index-th letter of the second text.  That letter both represents success and provides a clue to the next mission.

And as I hinted in the previous section, I do somewhat question the premise of the game.  Maybe I have lived a unique life, but my mall trips have either already had a mission---buy specific things and leave, maybe with a walk afterwards---or I had company that probably wouldn't appreciate my dipping into random shops...

## Six Years Hence:  The Journey to Thelio

Since the series only existed to advertise the System 76's then-new Thelio line of computers, the story definitely hasn't continued.

Poking around, I only see one new development, and I wouldn't call it *good*.  Six years ago, the agency that developed the campaign had a page for the source code and assets marked *Coming Soon*.  These days, the page shows the episodes of the campaign, but has no mention at all of providing the assets.
