This week, our [Free Culture Book Club]({% post_url 2020-05-02-freeculture %}) reads the second quarter of **Galactic Mutant Animals**, a few more encyclopedic pages and the *Ninja-World* story.

To give this series some sense of organization, check out some basic facts without much in the way of context.

 * Full Title:  **Galactic Mutant Animals**
 * Location:  <https://www.deviantart.com/guiea/gallery/85120580/gma>
 * Released:  1993
 * License:  CC BY-SA
 * Creator:  [Guie A](https://www.deviantart.com/guiea)
 * Medium:  Paper comic, scanned
 * Length:  Approximately seventy pages
 * Content Advisories:  More large-scale violence

This should go without saying---even though I plan to repeat it with every Book Club installment---but *Content Advisories* do not suggest any sort of judgment on my part, only topics that come up in the work that I noticed and might benefit from a particular mood or head space for certain audiences.  I provide it to help you make a decision, rather than a decision in and of itself.

## Galactic Mutant Animals

The creator describes the as follows.

> Galactic Mutant Animals is a comic I drew when I was twelve (~1993). Probably not the best comic you'll read today, but if somewhere a kid sees it & wants to create its own comic I'll be happy. Sorry you have to speak French to understand all the hilarious jokes.

The creator also provides this biography.

> I draw on my spare time, and love sharing my art under 'libre'/free license ; all my works use CC-BY-SA.
>
> I love monsters.

Occasionally, I see the creator posting new art on Mastodon, which led me here.

## What Works Well?

This story does have some clever pieces, most prominently the X-nimals, which I find far too amusing for my own good.

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

These pages feel maybe overly ambitious, packing too much onto the story page.  This sometimes condenses the text into a tiny space, which compounds the problems with hastily handwritten lettering.

Because this mostly focuses on fighting, the story hinges on the visual storytelling, helping the reader keep track of everything going on.  And as you might guess based on how many experienced professionals do this badly, I'd argue that it fails.

## Opportunities

I don't see any openings to contribute to or work with the creator, especially not on a comic from over thirty years ago.

## What's Adaptable?

Mc Beurk---not that I get the joke beyond riffing on McDonald's---exists "in space" on the way to the Cornitia star system with a couple of named planets.  We also get some song lyrics so impressive that I need to include them.

## Six Years Hence:    Manuel, the Magnificent Mechanical Man

Continuing our look at projects that we discussed early in the book club, we come to [*Manuel, the Magnificent Mechanical Man**]({% post_url 2020-07-11-manuel %}).

I don't know that I have anything useful to say about the book six years on, though.  I did submit some small corrections (accepted), which sparked a quick but fun conversations about the book with the author.  And I believe that the project still stands as one of the few (I think the only one that I've seen) that published its finances, for anybody interested in trying to duplicate and improve on the process of selling a book.

We have three more weeks to look back, though, so maybe something will arise...
