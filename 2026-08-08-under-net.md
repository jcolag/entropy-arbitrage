## Under the Net

The Codeberg page describes the as follows.

> This is an open source novel. Based on a true story in China. And it is also a letter for help for the people who live in 1984. You can contribute your own plot in this novel.

Beyond that, I only know what I see in the repository, that it has two chapters in Chinese so far, and what looks like the start of the first chapter translated into English.

The [license](https://codeberg.org/jiiku/Under_the_net/src/branch/main/LISENCE.md) causes some mild concerns.  The text copies the so-called [Deed](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) for CC BY-SA 4.0, suggesting an intent, but without the full text of the license or a statement of intent, the slim possibility also exists that the author didn't want a full license for some reason.

Oh, and as usual, my knowledge of Chinese tops out somewhere in the neighborhood if *nihao*, so I'll read this in translation.  That comes with the usual caveats that I may miss some critical nuance, especially important when the book claims to have a basis in fact.

## What Works Well?

When we actually have a story (see below), it seems written fairly well, even in shoddy machine translation.  Nothing in the story-so-far feels like it *accidentally* got in there, and I'd actually call this fairly good writing.

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

Primarily---and I realize that this could change quickly, given how little of the story we have---it seems like trouble that we only really have one character.  We have a secondary protagonist off in the background who plays (distant) second banana, barely even getting a hint of an inner life.  But the other characters feel entirely instrumental.  And that especially feels like a shame, since the writing shows that it could probably do a good job of giving us some time with more characters.

This probably comes down to taste, because I can think of two successful authors who do this sort of thing, but *wow* does this book ever like to deliver dry technical information to didactically teach the topic.  And with so few words, it seems bizarre to have a section on {% wiki Distributed_hash_table|distributed hash tables|en %} or the like.  But like I said, I've seen this among more famous authors.  I vaguely remember my introduction to Cory Doctorow's writing involving a short story that mostly wrapped a conference talk on mesh networking.

You can add to this the rants about school feeling useless/boring and the rampant product placement.  The spotlight on GitHub seems especially silly, since it came out *and* takes place after projects and developers started fleeing the site over their AI obsession, their ICE contracts, the increasingly broken user interface, and Microsoft dissolving the organizational walls isolating the platform from the rest of the company.  And while I realize that the story focuses on China's record on civil rights, Microsoft's record seems highly relevant in all that.

## Opportunities

I'll repeat what the description said, because I don't see much else.

> You can contribute your own plot in this novel.

Quickly investigating, the Codeberg account behind the repository hasn't done anything other than uploading the text four months ago.  They *might* integrate contributions, but they may also have abandoned the project.

## What's Adaptable?

Note that, because of the "based on a true story" premise and the language gap, I can't say for sure what the writing creates versus real-world references.  For example, we have the book's signature project Unity Whale/团结鲸鱼, a derivative of {% wiki Tails_%28operating_system%29|Tails|en %}.  And I can't find any distinct references to that anywhere, but...maybe it exists, somewhere?

## Six Years Hence:    Morevna, Episode 3

Continuing to look at what the Book Club talked about in its early days, **Morevna** has continued with [Episode 4](https://morevnaproject.org/anime/episode-4/), released a couple of years ago. Shading those accomplishments, the organizers of the project have made public statements in support of the Russian military's invasion of Ukraine, which puts something of a damper on the excitement, people have started feeling uncomfortable sharing their work.
