## Second Sight

For our usual context, this episode lands between [*Force of Nature*]({% post_url 2025-07-24-force-nature %}) and [*Inheritance*]({% post_url 2025-07-31-inheritance %}).

> Personal log, stardate 47329.4. I finally realize why I've had trouble sleeping the last few nights. Yesterday was the fourth anniversary of the massacre at Wolf three-five-nine. The fourth anniversary of Jennifer's death. I'm not sure what bothers me more. The date itself, or the fact that it almost passed unnoticed.

For our purposes, Sisko refers to [*The Best of Both Worlds*]({% post_url 2023-11-09-best-both-worlds-part-2 %}) and/or the prologue to [*Emissary*]({% post_url 2026-02-26-emissary-1 %}) showing the overlapping events.  Sadly, they have this narration with him staring at one of their fancy chess sets, rather than as he throws darts at a picture of Picard...

Also, the entire subsequent scene with Jake feels like one of the rare times that we've seen anything resembling supportive family interactions.

> **SISKO**: Get some hot chocolate and tell me about it.

In the twenty-fourth century, they will have returned to the hilarious idea that, if you can't sleep, then you should caffeinate yourself.  I never understood those people...

> **JAKE**: I have a calculus test in the morning.
>
> **SISKO**: Then you'd better get some sleep.

The franchise doesn't go in for productive self-care often, but "get a good night's sleep if you need to get things done" shows that they certainly *can*, despite the "have some stimulants to help you get back to bed" thing.

> **FENNA**: Beautiful, aren't they? I don't think I've seen the stars shine so brightly.

If "Fenna" seems familiar to you, then you almost certainly recognize (now) {% wiki Salli Richardson-Whitfield %}, who has shown up in a lot of genre fiction, especially when the production has an inclusive vibe.  Actually, she made history *this week* at the Emmy Awards as the first Black woman nominated for directing on a drama series, but we have her pretty close to the start of her career, here.

> **SISKO**: The Bajorans call that constellation the Runners. I can never figure out if they're running toward something or away from something.

We finally see some evidence that the Starfleet crew has started acknowledging the local culture...other than O'Brien threatening food vendors, I mean.

> **FENNA**: Commander of the station. That must be very exciting. All those ships coming and going. Every day must bring something new.

On **TNG**, I made fun of comments like that, characters telling us that we should find the writing clever.  But here, this *would* probably make a pretty great TV show, you know...?

> **SISKO**: Is this maintenance or repair?
>
> **O'BRIEN**: Right now it's maintenance, but five'll get you ten it'll be a repair by the time I'm through.
>
> **SISKO**: Admit it, Chief. If you were on a station where everything worked, you'd be miserable.

I swear that they keep quietly setting up a story where they reveal that O'Brien sabotages everything on the station for some nefarious reason...

> **KIRA**: Of course. You can drink whatever you like.
>
> **SISKO**: Thank you, Major. I appreciate your support.

I've mentioned it before, probably haven't shut up about it, but I love this friendship.

Actually, total aside, but does the quality of the dialogue seem significantly better than the franchise's norm?  It doesn't really have big quotable lines that some episodes do, but every conversation feels both grounded and snappy.

> **DAX**: Benjamin, have you ever met a terraformer?

We did, in [*Home Soil*]({% post_url 2022-09-15-home-soil %}), one of many episodes where the *Enterprise*-D crew thinks that the genocide side of the argument might make some solid points.

> **SEYETIK**: There you go, Lieutenant. That should increase your generator output by at least five percent. You must be Commander Sisko. Gideon Seyetik. Great pleasure to meet you, sir.

If Seyetik looks (or more likely sounds) familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Richard Kiley %}, best known (by a long way, I think) as the original Broadway Don Quixote in **The Man of La Mancha**, but around this time also lent his voice to **Jurassic Park** as the recorded tour guide, in addition to a lot of other work.

> **SISKO**: You've been talking to Nog about women again?

Better he talk to Nog than Bashir, O'Brien, or Dax...

> **SISKO**: Let's see. I'd say she's about one point six meters tall. Brown skin, dark hair. And the last time I saw her, she was wearing...she was wearing red.

He seems awfully excited about someone wearing red for someone *wearing red*.  Also, though, I note the fairly matter-of-fact note about skin color.  As I recall, at around this time, a lot of media had tried to lean into "not seeing race" when talking about individuals.  And while identifying somebody by skin tone sounds as awkward as "we'll have another person arriving later; she has brown eyes, black hair in tight curls" discussions at a restaurant, without known her culture or ethnicity, it makes sense in context.  And you might note that, due to the widespread acceptance of emoji, had they filmed this scene today, they might have referred to a {% wiki Fitzpatrick scale %} number with some qualification to indicate that they don't try to apply it to people with skin tones that don't resemble humans.

> **DAX**: No, I understand. It's hard to talk man to man with a woman.

Following up on my comment about Jake getting information on women---while I don't think that we've seen it yet except in her "you need to ignore the misogyny and lies" defense of the Ferengi in [*Rules of Acquisition*]({% post_url 2026-09-03-rules-acquisition %})---as the series progresses, Dax's personality will crystallize around wanting people to see her as shockingly transgressive.  People like *that*, you can't really have a serious relationship conversation with, at least in my experience, because they won't have conversations about topics other than their shocking transgressions...

> **SISKO**: I don't know about that. Van Gogh, Beckett, Y'Raka. I wouldn't exactly call them optimists.

He calls out painter {% wiki Vincent van Gogh %}, writer {% wiki Samuel Beckett %}, and random extraterrestrial filler to make it sound like the Federation has more culture than relatively modern Europe.  Van Gogh's depression seems to have driven him to an early grave.

Beckett seems "miscast," though.  His death a few years prior to the episode probably made his name more prominent at the time, but while he wrote a lot of black comedy, I can't find any information suggesting that it derived from some dark, haunted personality.  Also, it seems almost funny to classify him as a writer, since he joined the French Resistance during World War II, and befriended James Joyce and drove young André the Giant to school.  He wrote exceptionally well, but the man had a lot on his plate that didn't involve a typewriter at all...

By the way, I didn't find a line worth quoting, but it seems shockingly on-the-nose, though probably in Seyetik's character, to choose a ship named after {% wiki Prometheus %} for this mission.

> **SEYETIK**: Nine volumes and counting. You know, I always said I wanted to write as many books as I've had marriages. Which reminds me, wait till you taste the food my wife has created for you. None of that replicated nonsense you're used to. No, every dish was prepared entirely by Nidell's own sweet hands.

We still see some tension with replicated food.

> **BASHIR**: I, for one, find him remarkable entertaining.

This fits with Bashir seeing a religious leader wanting to travel through the wormhole in [*Battle Lines*]({% post_url 2026-05-21-battle-lines %}) as a road-trip to enjoy.  For all his many faults, the man does know how to not let things get to him.

> **NIDELL**: Commander, can I help you with something?

Right?  If you need an excuse to linger for conversation, you could at least help clear the damned dishes.  Did he grow up in a barn...?

> **DAX**: That would have never stopped Curzon.

Yes, we had an entire episode, [*Dax*]({% post_url 2026-04-16-dax %}), making that abundantly clear.  And look, I get that marriages fall apart, and some people have open relationships, so don't take my groan at the line as some Puritanical idea of unbreakable vows.  However, maybe start with a vague presumption that somebody married means to stay in the relationship until told otherwise, before shrugging it off as a non-obstacle to seduction.  Actually, maybe I should rearrange that idea:  Don't think of people's lives and choices as obstacles for you to overcome because you'd rather have them with you.

> **PIERSALL**: Have you finished loading the protomatter device into the shuttle-pod?

You might recall protomatter figuring into the plot of [**The Search for Spock**]({% post_url 2022-03-31-tsfs %}), which somehow doesn't sound like a red flag to them.  Actually, considering the prior mention of the ship's name and the fate of the mythical Prometheus, it seems strange that the plot ends so neatly.  Although in retrospect, I suppose that we never follow up on the story, so might well have an ending where a certain someone gets continuously revived on the star's surface to instantly burn to death.

See what I mean about Samuel Beckett?  A person can have fairly dark ideas with a whimsical song in their heart...

> **SEYETIK**: Are you familiar with the Klingon poet G'trok?
>
> **SISKO**: A little. *The Fall of Kang* is required reading at the Academy.
>
> **SEYETIK**: *So honor the valiant who die 'neath your sword.*
>
> **SISKO**: *But pity the warrior who slays all his foes.*

We get some actual not-from-1930s-Earth culture, here.

Also, the title seems like an interesting choice.  We met **a** Kang in [*The Day of the Dove*]({% post_url 2021-04-01-dove %}), and while he could plausibly have fallen in the intervening century, that doesn't quite fit with what we'll learn and who we'll meet on...oh, that post comes on (US) Thanksgiving.  And I suppose the fictional poet or real episode writer could've meant a different Kang, but how often do we have multiple characters with the same name in this franchise?

> **PIERSALL**: What's he talking about?
>
> **SISKO**: Klingon poetry.

Didn't Sisko describe the poem as "required reading at the Academy"?  Did this drone get the flu that week?  Riker and others certainly went to great lengths to tell us that they barely paid attention to their classes, so that would make some sense.

## Conclusions

Mostly, we got some actual alien culture.  Seyetik also suggests that some civilians wear what look to my eyes like batik-inspired tops.

### The Good

The episode has a surprising amount of people at least trying to support each other emotionally.

We see some recognition that sleep improves performance.

Federation culture, both in general and on an individual level, seems to have become more accepting of foreign cultures.  We see that mainly in Sisko learning Bajoran astronomy, rather than the dismissal of the local culture that we've seen through a lot of the series.  But we also see that Starfleet requires its cadets to have some background in the literature of at least one foreign power.

### The Bad

Despite awareness of the value of sleep, people still/again use caffeine to help them sleep.

Likewise, for all Sisko's emotional support, apparently nobody ever taught him to help clear the table when someone cooks dinner for you, especially if you want an excuse to talk to them afterward.

And people still apparently don't read the required reading at school, not even enough to recognize references to it.

### The Weird

At least some people (still? again?) see replicated food as low-quality.

Dax seems to imply that enough people have open marriages that a wedding shouldn't necessarily stop a person from pursuing someone romantically.

Apparently, Starfleet no longer considers protomatter a dangerous substance.
