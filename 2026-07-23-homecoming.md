## The Homecoming

For our usual context, this episode comes after [*Liaisons*]({% post_url 2025-06-05-liaisons %}), with no non-**DS9** episode, because [*Interface*]({% post_url 2025-06-12-interface %}) apparently aired a couple of days late, for reasons that I can't recall or find.

More directly, a lot of this episode and the next two act a bit like three of four parts following [*In the Hands of the Prophets*]({% post_url 2026-07-09-in-hands-prophets %}) and the religious/political conflict described there.  That also largely means that we might not have too much to do, this time out.

> **SISKO**: You passed your algebra test?

I realize that I probably beat on the educational side far more than necessary when talking about *In the Hands of the Prophets*, but it occurs to me that the episode would've probably gone down far better if we had gotten a bit of Keiko teaching algebra instead of going way out of her way to alienate the Bajorans.  I don't mean exclusively, but giving some indication that she teaches something other than the topic that she refuses to teach in an inclusive way.

And to reiterate, for those who don't recall the details of the episode or my comments, Keiko insisted on referring to "the wormhole" (apparently unnamed) and "entities" living within, only acknowledging under duress that the Bajorans named both---the Celestial Temple and the Prophets, respectively---centuries ago, and treating those names like a distasteful inconvenience.

> **SISKO**: You learned out how to hit Bob Gibson's fast ball?

{% wiki Bob_Gibson|Hoot|en %}, as they called him, played for the St. Louis Cardinals for his entire professional career, winning a pile of awards, including the informal claim to ushering in most of the so-called "Year of the Pitcher," passing away in 2020 to surprisingly little fanfare, though I suppose the news had other topics occupying it.

> **SISKO**: The main thing is you can't be afraid of rejection

Yes and no, though, right?  I mean, you don't want so much fear that you don't ask people out.  Figure, worst case, your question exposes the object of your affections as the agent of a hostile power, prompting them to detonate a weapon that triggers a global thermonuclear war that vaporizes everybody and everything.  In that case, you'll barely even notice the sting of the rejection, and the stakes only decrease from there, so you might as well ask them out if you want, and if they seem at all receptive.  You don't want to make the destruction of all life on Earth all about *your* anxiety, right?

However, fearing rejection for violating somebody's boundaries seems like a fairly healthy way to get a certain kind of person to actually respect those boundaries.  Don't confront them at their place of employment, for example, as we saw in [*The Forsaken*]({% post_url 2026-06-18-forsaken %}), or as a public spectacle or the like, because those will absolutely make **somebody** uncomfortable, no matter which answer.  Bashir has this kind of "no fear of rejection" as he repeatedly propositions Dax.  Feel free to fear getting rejected from that sort of request.

> **KIRA**: To Cardassia Four to rescue a Bajoran prisoner of war.
>
> **SISKO**: You're right. I wish you hadn't told me.

Really?  That sounds like something worth knowing, regardless of whether it impacts the immediate decision.  For example, you could definitely do a maybe-less-satisfying version of this episode without all the clunky cloak-and-dagger nonsense where Kira sets out and, as she crosses the border, Sisko notifies the Cardassians that he needs the illegal prisoner(s) on the planet released, in accordance with such-and-such bit of whatever treaty.

> **KIRA**: His body was never found. Look, Commander, I wouldn't be asking this if I thought there was another way to rescue Li. But Bajoran ships don't have the maneuverability or defensive capabilities to get me safely in and out of Cardassian territory.

Given the constant failures of the runabouts when faced with any stress, did the Bajorans build their ships out of those big cardboard boxes that they ship refrigerators in?

> **O'BRIEN**: The Circle, huh. What gives them the right to mess up our station?

We get plenty of worrying lines in this franchise, but this might beat at least the majority.  The Federation, recall, only manages the station on behalf of the Bajoran provisional government.  O'Brien, however, has gone full-on {% wiki Broken_windows_theory|broken windows|en %} colonialism, declaring it the *Federation's* station (or at least joint ownership) and has strong opinions about relatively minor graffiti.

Odo will (next) describe them as an extremist Bajoran-supremacist group, but O'Brien doesn't already know that, or some "hate crime"-adjacent concept would've come up earlier in the scene instead of focusing on the paint.

> **O'BRIEN**: If they think scrawling a few of signs is going to get rid of us, they've got another think coming.

Really?  Because rather than cleaning up relatively minor vandalism that plenty of people would rather [decriminalize](https://news.ucsc.edu/2021/09/graffiti-street-art/), he called the equivalent of the cops *and* the mayor to complain about *those people* messing up *his* station.  Again, if he had reason to already know about the racial bigotry bit, I could understand that, but he seems more than a bit concerned about what he also wants to play down as a trivial incident.

> **O'BRIEN**: They want to help us?

Right?  It seems so weird when peacetime foreigners want you to not randomly die when they have an opportunity to help.  {% emoji eyeroll %}  O'Brien's confusion raises all sorts of questions, like do Federation posts *not* offer help to ships in distress?  Or does he not believe that any Cardassian has the capacity to offer assistance?

> **O'BRIEN**: Then you know why the Prefect doesn't want to be kept waiting, huh?

He...dives into that comment a bit too comfortably.

Seriously, if you think that assessment seems too harsh, because of undercover shenanigans or whatever, think about it in context of his prior comments about "Bajoran women," how little his wife's time without him matters to him, and his "shopping for wares" in [*The Storyteller*]({% post_url 2026-05-28-storyteller %}).

> **OVERSEER**: What would it cost me for an appointment?

You might recognize sleazy-overseer as {% wiki John_Fleck_%28actor%29|John Fleck|en %}, who we last saw as one of the Romulans in [*The Mind's Eye*]({% post_url 2024-04-18-mind-s-eye %}).  He'll come back as other characters in later episodes of this show, plus a spot on **Voyager** and (not that we'll cover it) one character on **Enterprise**.  He also put in an appearance in the pilot film for **Babylon 5** mentioned a couple of months ago as context for [*The Passenger*]({% post_url 2026-04-23-passenger %}), and more recently on **The Orville**, rounding out the shows that people associate loosely with this franchise among a broader career.

> **LI**: Who are you?

Likewise, you might recognize Li as {% wiki Richard_Beymer|Richard Beymer|en %}, probably most recognized as Tony from the (original) film version of **West Side Story** or Ben Horne in **Twin Peaks**, but he has also done a lot of civil rights documentary film work and even worked with Barney Frank a bit, among a much wider-ranging career.

> **BORUM**: The earring. You got it.

And you might recognize "the other guy" as {% wiki Michael_Bell_%28actor%29|Michael Bell|en %}, primarily a voice actor, union activist, and co-creator of the gadget that lets off-the-grid folks recycle washing water elsewhere in the house.  He also played Zorn in [*Encounter at Farpoint*]({% post_url 2022-05-19-farpoint1 %}).

And believe it or not, we have an even bigger surprise coming after these three...

> **DUKAT**: Major, I have just informed Commander Sisko that the Cardassian High Command has issued a formal apology to the people of Bajor.

We last saw Dukat in [*Duet*]({% post_url 2026-07-02-duet %}).

> **DUKAT**: We had no idea that Bajoran prisoners were still being held on Cardassia Four. Such detentions are a direct violation of supreme directive twenty-six forty-five, and I assure you the camp Prefect will be chastised accordingly.

I don't know if this proves useful to the project here, but this sounds like a fairly open lie, given that they had this labor camp in the Cardassian system, presumably the seat of government, rather than some distant outpost.  Figure, with the possible population density of space (the few habitable planets in the void), this comes off like some current head of state claiming to not know about the secret prison camp in the next office over.  And Sisko and Kira nod along like this sort of thing understandably happens all the time...

> **JARO**: Major. Where is he?

Last guest, not even listed in the credits, you almost certainly recognize Jaro as {% wiki Frank_Langella|Frank Langella|en %}, known for...pretty much everything that he has ever touched, maybe?  I will point out that he seems to have started career with an I-believe-pretty-much-lost TV series called {% wiki The_Trials_of_O%27Brien|**The Trials of O'Brien**|en %} (no relation), starring young Peter Falk as a defense attorney.  He also had high-profile relationships with Diane Baker, Elizabeth Taylor, and (not long after this) Whoopi Goldberg.

Seriously, though, this feels like a shockingly weighty guest cast for a not-too-complex season premiere...

> **JAKE**: Because I'm not a Bajoran.
>
> **SISKO**: That's not much of a reason. Jake, there are some things happening on Bajor. Troubling things. I'm sorry you've become a victim of them. You don't deserve it. Nobody does. Time for bed, okay?

Do I really need to point out that a significant fraction of the first season had episodes where Sisko grumbled about not wanting his kid to spend time with "that Ferengi boy," his words in [*A Man Alone*]({% post_url 2026-03-19-man-alone %}).

> **DOEK**: Commander Sisko, I am Romah Doek, executive officer of the *Nanut*. We were preparing to depart the station for the Gamma Quadrant when we discovered a Bajoran citizen trying to stow away on ship. I was hoping you'd know what to do with him.

Under the prosthetics, you might recognize the officer as {% wiki Paul_Nakauchi|Paul Nakauchi|en %}, best known as a voice performer, but also a fair amount of musical theater, not super-well-known, but definitely continuing this episode's weird punching above its weight in casting.

> **SISKO**: No. It's based on a legend. And legends are as powerful as any truth. Bajor still needs that legend. It needs you.

This feels a lot like a jab at **The Next Generation**'s recurring idea that everybody should trust the truth in legends.  In fact, echoing a lot of what I've said about the other show, in [*Haven*]({% post_url 2022-07-28-haven %}), Picard said that legends "have a way of sometimes coming true."  And you might notice the almost obscenely passive note to this, that he values legends because something useful might drop out of the aether for him to exploit.  Exactly like his lectures on how humanity has already done all its evolving, he saw it as something provided to him, whereas Sisko doesn't mind engaging in a bit of myth-making, if it serves the common good...as long as the myth doesn't center on him, as we saw last time about his role as the Emissary.

## Conclusions

As mentioned, we don't get much new, here, and may not for a couple of weeks.

### The Good

Someone teaches the kids algebra.

Sisko has a fairly clear understanding of the political importance of symbols, and doesn't mind sharing that knowledge.

### The Bad

The Federation seems surprisingly disinterested in using political pressure to help free political prisoners.  Sisko also weirdly takes Dukat at his word that the Cardassian government had no idea about the forced labor camp of leftover political prisoners in the Cardassian home solar system.

O'Brien gives us our weekly dose of bigotry.  He seems to want to crack down on fairly minor vandalism, because he apparently sees Deep Space Nine as the Federation's property to defend and doesn't want to feel weak.  But he also acts completely baffled by the possibility that Cardassians might come to the aid of a civilian ship in potential distress.  More in the sexism department, he also seems to have no objection to treating a (foreign) woman like a sex slave.

### The Weird

Sisko doesn't think that anybody deserves social exclusion due to their ethnicity, at least when humans get excluded, unlike all the times that he complained about his kid spending time with Nog.
