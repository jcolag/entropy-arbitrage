## Lumpin

The creator describes the album as follows, with apologies to people for already getting into the coarse bits.

> released October 13, 2023
>
> Belladonna wrote these (except the Pat song); she plays banjolele and sings on all tracks  
> Her Ellie plays mandolin on 2, 3, 6, and 8 and also sings on 2 and 6  
> Her Rosemary takes really good care of them both and makes them so much fuckin soup  
> Ashe took the cover picture
>
> This album is for our lovers and homies and comrades and everyone who inspires us. It is also for the martyrs and the innocents and our own beloved dead.

In the tags, it tries on a lot of genres, but I think that I'd characterize it as emo, for the most part.

## What Works Well?

The political messaging seems the most cohesive, I think.  Situations may get bad, and we may never run out of issues to get angry about, but the glimpses of a better future make the discomfort worthwhile.

Likewise, the emotion behind the pieces largely comes through, even when the song itself seems far less clear about where it wants to go.

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

As I mentioned, at least to me, a few of the songs feel either totally opaque or hyper-specific in a way that we wouldn't understand without participating in the (real? fictional? metaphorical?) incidents.

## Opportunities

You can buy the album, but I don't see any other ways to support the artist.

## What's Adaptable?

I didn't catch anything, unless Seattle/Portland geography became fictional since it last came up in conversation.  Although some songs seem to imply some post-apocalyptic landscape off in the background.
