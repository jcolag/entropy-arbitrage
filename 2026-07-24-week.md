## Bonus

Because it accidentally became a tradition early on in the life of the blog, I drop any additional articles that didn't fit into the one-article-per-day week, but too weird or important to not mention, here.

{% embed https://wagingnonviolence.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Untitled-design-20.jpg|Working class tenants with the Committee Against Anti-Asian Violence and allied organizations rally in April 2025 to demand a rent freeze in New York City|false| %}

<i class="fas fa-square"></i> [Movements cannot shy away from conflict](https://wagingnonviolence.org/2026/07/movements-cannot-shy-away-from-conflict/) from Waging Nonviolence

> But perhaps unexpectedly, often enough, the conflict is inside us — between the parts of us that want to be big and powerful and effective, and the part of us that is ambivalent about power, the part of us that is afraid of the loss that comes with all real choice.

While I don't have much "movement" experience, from what I've seen in general, an organization needs everybody to feel safe before they start seeing healthy disagreements.  And they need a conflict resolution process that everybody understands.  They also need an understanding that, at a certain point, everybody gets behind the final decision or leaves.  It looks like a lot of the movement side gets every piece of that wrong, preferring to save arguments for after the final decisions...
