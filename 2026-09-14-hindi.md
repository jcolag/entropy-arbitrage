Today, India marks {% wiki Hindi Day %} (हिन्दी दिवस), celebrating the adoption of Hindi as written in {% wiki Devanagari %} as one of the country's now-twenty-two official languages in developing their constitution.  The multiple-language compromise tried to resolve the factional rivalries stirred up by British colonization, which frequently pit Hindi against {% wiki Urdu %}, a closely related language---and the other major Indo-European language in the region---written in an Arabic-derived script used by Muslims...not that it ended the rivalry, as evidenced by the {% wiki Hindutva|Hindu nationalist movement -%}'s resurgence over the past couple of decades.

![The art from a 1988 stamp celebrating Hindi Day, featuring a flower emerging from the approximate center of the country, against a red background](/blog/assets/Hindi-Day-stamp-1988.png "A smarter blogger would probably take pains to translate the fifteen words written in Hindi, but who has the time...?")
