## Whispers

For our usual context, this episode lands between [*Sub Rosa*]({% post_url 2025-08-28-sub-rosa %}) and [*Lower Decks*]({% post_url 2025-09-04-lower-decks %}).  Over at the competition, this episode sits between **Babylon 5**'s *Soul Hunter* and *Born to the Purple*.

Oh, and if you somehow didn't get enough of O'Brien's coffee-slurping and whinging last week, have I got a deal for you...

> **KEIKO**: I had a lot of work to catch up on at school. Thought I'd get an early start.
>
> **O'BRIEN**: At five-thirty in the morning? Coffee, Jamaican blend, double strong, double sweet.

Wait, don't ex-military people typically wake up upsettingly early?  I feel like, as someone who has generally woken up at around six, if I have the motivation to go for a walk first thing, I'll bump into all the former soldiers and Marines in particular on their way back from their workouts.  Given the big deal that O'Brien makes of loving military rations and (soon) his battle experience, it almost seems bizarre that Keiko could beat him out of bed at an almost-reasonable hour.

Granted, I haven't met *every* former soldier, and four hundred years can change a lot of socialization, but it still seems odd.

> **KEIKO**: There's something I want to show her. A Vulcan program that teaches basic problem-solving skills.
>
> **O'BRIEN**: At five-thirty-five in the morning?
>
> **KEIKO**: I want to do it while her mind is fresh.

I want to see *that* episode, a drowsy (and stressed) toddler staring at edutainment...

Also, though, note the othering, and try to find an analogous adjective that doesn't make you cringe.  For example, writing this from the United States as I do, we might think of "Vulcan" as referring to the place inside the Federation known for a stereotypical view, so something like "a {% wiki Nevada %}n program on libertarianism."  Or we might see "Vulcan" as referring to the ethnicity and its stereotypes, so "a Jewish program on accounting."  The first reads as complete gibberish, and the second repulsive.

We might *imagine* a third option, referring to a nationality where some institution has an important specialty, such as---again, assuming the United States as analogous---"a Filipino program on rice-related agricultural science," implying that it comes from the highly prestigious (not that you'd guess it from the name) {% wiki International Rice Research Institute %} in the Philippines.  However, that premise falls apart, because they share a government with Vulcan.  In the United States, we wouldn't refer to a "marine sciences program from Massachusetts" to refer to the {% wiki Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution %}'s work; we'd say that it came from Woods Hole.

> **DECURTIS**: Just getting a head start, Chief

If the Ensign looks familiar to you, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Todd Waring %}, with most of his career overshadowed by taking over two separate characters established by Tom Hanks.

> **O'BRIEN**: I mean, the way they were acting, they might've been trying to pull off one of those surprise parties that I can't stand, only my birthday's not until September, and believe me, as it turned out, I had nothing else to celebrate.

This feels like one of those situations where he loudly and repeatedly tells everyone about how much he hates surprise parties, then spends the week after his birthday griping that nobody threw him a surprise party...

> **SISKO**: Now, tell me something that's not in the report.
>
> **O'BRIEN**: Sir?
>
> **SISKO**: You know, the kind of things we don't include in reports but might help me in these talks.

How does he not understand this?  Surely, the job *explicitly* entails this sort of thing.  I mean, even today, any job where you stand on the "front lines" dealing with customers---let alone diplomatic and security work---you need to understand where they come from (especially internationally) and gather information that'll help the rest of the team keep the relationship comfortable.

> **O'BRIEN**: It can't be anything that serious. I rebuilt that entire subsystem myself. I'll get on it right away.

Oh, look, another case where O'Brien worked on something that immediately failed.  I still have to assume that they meant to expose him as a saboteur...

> **BASHIR**: Cough. How's the sex life?
>
> **O'BRIEN**: I don't have a sense of humor.
>
> **BASHIR**: Cough. Everything all right in that department?
>
> **O'BRIEN**: That department is none of Starfleet's business, or yours.

Low blow, but if he doesn't "*have* a (ahem) sense of humor," then I really don't see why Keiko stays with him, with all the other problems.

Seriously, though, even the adults with children can't talk frankly about sex?

> **O'BRIEN**: You don't remember me telling you when my father remarried last spring? Me blathering on about this woman I've never met before?

Wait a minute.  If even O'Brien knows that he acts like this, then why do we still need to endure it...?

> **JAKE**: I'm working on a subspace transceiver for my science project, but I can't seem to figure out how to get the emitter crystal to work.
>
> **O'BRIEN**: A subspace transceiver model. I used to build them when I was your age. I'll be glad to help.

For those of you too young to recognize it or otherwise haven't encountered the idea, they presumably want to make their transceiver analogous to a {% wiki crystal radio %}, which only require a handful of parts, most of which a builder can improvise.  I believe that the project fell out of fashion during this era in the United States, which---since those sets can only really receive AM stations unless you do some sophisticated engineering---I always (maybe incorrectly) tied to the FCC abolishing the Fairness Doctrine in 1987 and later market consolidation, since I don't think that even the staunchest conservatives thought that kids should have a project that "rewards" them with (at best) Rush Limbaugh...

That bit of quasi-history aside, I do need to ask what the heck kind of school Keiko runs, because when you live in a port and/or military installation, it seems highly irresponsible to force teenagers, especially those already prone to pranks, to acquire transceivers of the sort that would let them listen in on and send to nearby ships.  For a much lower-tech example, my college campus sat next to a tiny airport, and we had strict rules on lighting visible from above, because one bright blue light could convince a pilot that they miscalculated the position of their runway.  And then it took years to get Wi-Fi permission squared away...

> **JAKE**: You don't think it's a conflict of interest or anything, you being married to the teacher?

It almost surprises me that they think about this kind of corruption.

> **O'BRIEN**: I'd like the access codes to the Parada quarters.

Who makes a request like this?  Asking for access seems reasonable, but acting like somebody owes him the keys shouldn't work in any healthy work environment.

> **O'BRIEN**: I worked on the pylon controls the better part of the day. Needle in a haystack wouldn't do this job justice. When I finally found the crack in the RF power conduit, I couldn't imagine how it could've occurred so deep in the system, unless someone had broken it deliberately.

Right, because usually, broken components have the decency to not sit in difficult parts of the system to access.  {% emoji eyeroll %}  I realize that I keep coming back to his incompetence or malevolence, but I really don't see how an alleged engineer comes up with the idea that somebody disassembled the system to break a hard-to-reach component *before* the possibility that all parts eventually break down, and sometimes it doesn't happen in a convenient way.

For those who haven't needed to fix things, imagine a cab driver getting an out-of-town fare and assuming some conspiracy, because they can't understand how anybody would travel so far.

> **O'BRIEN**: But you don't like fricandeau stew.
>
> **KEIKO**: You do.
>
> **O'BRIEN**: Yeah, but---
>
> ...
>
> **O'BRIEN**: How thoughtful of you. You're not having any?

He complains because he gets something that he likes, and then complains that his wife, who doesn't like it, wants to eat something else, in a world where it costs *nothing* to have different food.  Has he considered that everybody has started snubbing him because he doesn't have anything positive to contribute to conversations?

Oh, and [fricandeau](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/fricandeau) refers to...think fricassee, but usually veal instead of usually poultry, either fried or stewed---stewed in this case, since they repeatedly indicate that---in its own gravy or some other sauce.

> **O'BRIEN**: Any unknown chemical agents?

How the heck do you search for unknown chemicals?

> **O'BRIEN**: Computer, display all arriving vessels from stardate 47550 through 47571.

You can go check the screen for yourself to see the popular culture and political references for yourselves...

> **O'BRIEN**: Coffee, Jamaican blend, double strong, double sweet. You're drinking too much of this stuff, O'Brien. Anything to stay alert, O'Brien. Okay, computer, play back station logs chronologically, starting with stardate 47550.

They make the logs available for anybody?  That seems pretty irresponsible.

> **COMPUTER**: Please enter security verification.

They still haven't improved their passwords[^J7YtpV], by the way.  He only innovated on the "Me ABC-123" formula by including his daughter's name.

[^J7YtpV]:  Granted, this goes out in a week when we found out that major global SSL certificate authority {% wiki EMudhra %} used completely insecure private keys including 4, 56, 66, 115, 155, 156, 230, and 237.  The attack found the first one, by which I mean that, if I asked you to guess a number between 0 and 2<sup>384</sup> - 1, and you started with zero and incremented with every guess, you'd get their key in well under a minute.

> **O'BRIEN**: What are you talking about, Quark?

Violence, again.

> **QUARK**: Racquetball. The rematch with Bashir next week. We never finished the first one, remember? My, we are jumpy, aren't we.

We went through that mess in [*Rivals*]({% post_url 2026-10-01-rivals %}), if you need a refresher.

> **ROLLMAN**: I know who you are.

We know Rollman---and {% wiki Susan Bay %} portraying her---too, from her appearance in [*Past Prologue*]({% post_url 2026-03-12-past-prologue %}).

> **O'BRIEN**: It seemed almost unbelievable to me. Had someone or something started to infiltrate all of Starfleet?

For reference---and this seems worth pointing out, especially since O'Brien did shoot a bunch of his colleagues while running from the security office---the last time that somebody asked this question, we got [*Conspiracy*]({% post_url 2022-11-03-conspiracy %}), where O'Brien's previous boss committed mass murder because he didn't agree with some patrol route changes.

> **O'BRIEN**: He's perfect. He looks just like me.

Does everybody from the *Enterprise*-D have a secret twin?

> **REPLICA**: Tell her I love---

A tiny point, here, and possibly nothing more than a flubbed take that nobody wanted to reshoot at the end of the week, *but* the cadence of this line suggests that he had some sort of zinger in mind, not "I love her," which would flow more smoothly.

On that note, I feel like I need to point out that, repeatedly in this post, I wanted to make the point that this *Pseudo'Brien* doesn't do or say anything that we haven't seen the real O'Brien do or say multiple times.  But thinking about it, we do have a fairly serious difference, in that we didn't spend any time in this episode on racism or sexism.  I can only distinguish between the two, in other words, because he acts like *slightly less* of a jackass.

## Conclusions

We probably shouldn't take it for granted that everything that we learn applies, since the central character technically doesn't hail from the Federation.  However, (got it) Furlongs Faux'Brien acts enough like his "donor" that we can probably get away with conflating them.

### The Good

They at least briefly acknowledge that a student getting help from the teacher's immediate family might constitute a problem in fairness.

### The Bad

We see fairly a fairly aggressive attempt to indicate that Vulcans sit outside mainstream culture and blend into a unified mass.

O'Brien, at least, doesn't seem to understand what officers need and expect from deep contact with a foreign culture.  We also see quite a bit more evidence that he does a terrible job in his engineering work.

We see the return of the inability to deal with sex in a mature way.  In fact, O'Brien shows fairly miserable socialization skills through the entire episode, down on everything, acting entitled to secure information, and has multiple violent outbursts.  He even thinks that people not wanting to spend time with him indicates a subversion of authority.

Education apparently involves having young people build and deploy dangerous items.  And Starfleet continues to have worthless security, with the usual passwords, and it seems like pretty much anybody can read critical management logs unless the owner specifically locked them.
