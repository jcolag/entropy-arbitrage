## Draketos Songbook

The creator describes the book as follows, admittedly not much to go on.

> My own songs as well as songs for which I have the permission to publish under cc by-sa (mainly public domain songs).

We might also look at the credits early in the book.

> ### Künstlerinnen und Künstler des Covers
>
> - Trudy Wenzel: Draketo-Dragon (Seite 1). Ihr findet sie auf <https://synticfaye.com>.
> - Arne Babenhauserheide: Tav, (Seite 1), Tuscheskizze.
>
> ### Impressum
>
> Copyright der Lieder steht bei den Liedern.  
> Editor: Arne Babenhauserheide  
> Lizenz: Creativecommons cc by-sa: <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/>
{: lang="de" }

Or, in English, we get this.

> ### Cover Artists
>
> - Trudy Wenzel: Draketo-Dragon (page 1). You can find her at <https://synticfaye.com>.
> - Arne Babenhauserheide: Tav (page 1), ink sketch.
>
> ### Imprint
>
> Copyright for the songs is held by the respective authors.  
> Editor: Arne Babenhauserheide  
> License: Creative Commons CC BY-SA: <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/>

I'll skip the traditional songs---leaving us with about sixteen songs---and the music notation and largely treat this as a book of poetry.

## What Works Well?

While none of the concepts need much more than a short sentence to describe them, somewhat reasonably given that almost none of the songs needs more than page, we get some interesting storytelling in the back half of the book.  And some songs even connect into a common narrative.

We also get some solid symbolic moments, such as using a famous anti-fascist anthem as the basis of a screed against the EU's {% wiki Chat Control %} bills.  And we also have this, from *Träume für die Welt*/*Dreams for the World*.

> Wenn Lieder Träume gebären,  
> Geschichten Welten sind,  
> Soll’n sie helfen, dass die Menschen sich wehren,  
> Sich heilen im Feenwind.
{: lang="de" }

In English, that becomes this.

> If songs can give birth to dreams,  
> if stories can become worlds,  
> may they help people to stand and resist,  
> to find healing in a fairy-breeze.

I can't really argue with that...

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

Despite promises of songs with either firm provenance---created by the author or granted permission to publish with the public license---or in the public domain, we get at least one song that I seriously doubt that they secured permission for.  A couple of borderline cases also might exist, especially if you care about the melodies, but you'll spot the big one.

I found a couple of the songs totally incomprehensible.  Some, I couldn't work out a translation, no matter what languages I tried and how much I tried to adjust the spelling.  One seems to have too many private references to guess at.

And *Infinite Hands* spends far too much time lionizing Richard Stallman, which...no, thank you.

## Opportunities

The book has a [Mercurial repository](https://hg.sr.ht/~arnebab/draketos-songbook), but I don't see much else.

## What's Adaptable?

We have a vague laundering of fantasy tropes across songs that arguably form parts of the same world, but mostly we have handy metaphors, such as the aforementioned "infinite hands" to describe the value and power of Free Software.
