## The Siege

For our usual context, this episode lands directly between [*Gambit, Part I*]({% post_url 2025-06-19-gambit-part-1 %}) and [*Gambit, Part I*]({% post_url 2025-06-26-gambit-part-2 %}), as well as continuing the plot of [*The Homecoming*]({% post_url 2026-07-23-homecoming %}) and [*The Circle*]({% post_url 2026-07-30-circle %}).

> **SISKO**: Governments can break off relations with an edict. It's not so easy when it comes down to our level, hm? Lieutenant Bilecki here is engaged to a young man from Bajor. Ensign Kelly. Ensign Kelly's daughter and a Bajoran girl worked together on a prize-winning science fair project. All of us who have served on the station for the past year have made Bajoran friends. We've come to care about what happens to these people. I know I do. So, I've decided to stay. Just to make sure the evacuation is conducted properly.

Apparently, things have changed since [*In the Hands of the Prophets*]({% post_url 2026-07-09-in-hands-prophets %}), when we got a nearly explicit statement that the to parts of the crew don't generally interact.

It also seems notable that, as the teaser runs, the entire Starfleet crew opts to put themselves in harm's way to buy time for colleagues to get the truth out.  I don't think that we've ever seen anything like that in the franchise.

> **NOG**: Has there ever been one of your kind and one of mine who were better friends?

No, usually the humans shoot first and invent the answers to questions later, as seen throughout **The Next Generation** starting with [*The Battle*]({% post_url 2022-07-14-battle %}).

> **O'BRIEN**: We can't just let the Cardies have the wormhole.

Sorry, *most* of the crew stays behind to buy time for their colleagues to get the truth out.  O'Brien abandons his family to stay due to his commitment to racism.

> **KEIKO**: They can have the whole Gamma Quadrant for all I care. Miles, come with us.

Apparently, Keiko has no problems with literal, overt fascists conquering and exploiting smaller cultures, as long as they don't personally bother her family.

> **QUARK**: I might have overbooked slightly.
>
> ...
>
> **QUARK**: It's an accepted Ferengi transit practice. I feel perfectly justified. I have complete faith in Commander Sisko's ability to straighten this out.

For the younger among you, when this aired, this all came off as a funny joke, because regulations meant that not even the most hyper-capitalist corporation would [strand people in an emergency {% cc %}](https://www.airhelp.com/en/flight-disruptions/caribbean-flight-cancellations-hurricane-melissa-01112025/).  And yet, somehow, someone used the Ferengi as a template instead of an exaggeration.

> **ODO**: I'll miss the aggravation, the petty theft, the bad manners.

Not relevant to us, but that doesn't change the admission.  Arguably, it comes off as one of the most tender moments of the franchise.  "I miss their faults" feels like the stuff of love songs, not jokes between bitter opponents.

> **SISKO**: A letter I wrote you.

No, you don't get to hand someone the equivalent of a floppy disk and call it "a letter."  I refuse to allow it...

> **DAY**: Clear. No resistance at all. Minister Jaro's prediction was correct. The Federation has tucked its tail between its legs.

If Day looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Steven_Weber|Steven Weber|en %}, then primarily known as the goofy brother on **Wings**, though he has had a few high-profile runs since then.

> **DAX**: I guess the Bajoran resistance must have had a lot of short pilots.

As it turns out, despite Kira's joke after this, we have a fair bit of evidence that [shorter people make better fighter pilots](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/2008/september/pulling-gs-pilots-body-sets-limit), because the heart doesn't need to pump blood as far to the brain, reducing blackouts in tight maneuvers.  It might make sense for Starfleet officers to not recognize that, though, because all their ships seem to have artificial gravity and various force dampeners that'd compensate.

> **BASHIR**: I wouldn't get too close to that. It's thorium isotope. Prolonged exposure could have very nasty consequences. Bashir to Sisko.

{% wiki Thorium|Thorium|en %} has a few industrial uses such as gas lamp mantles and some smaller-scale nuclear energy.  As Bashir says, unless it gets inside your vital organs, long-term exposure presents health dangers as any radioactive material would, but I gather that not enough to worry about walking past barrels, which presumably have some shielding.

> **KIRA**: You Starfleet types are too dependent on gadgets and gizmos. You lose your natural instincts for survival.

I guess that I didn't read as much into the short pilot thing as I thought, if Kira agrees that Starfleet technology insulates them to the point that they don't actually know how any of the work happens.

> **O'BRIEN**: It's a good idea. Kind of thing I might've come up with. We've got to get out of here, and soon.

Well, someone who "might've come up with" the idea *probably* would've considered the possibility and planned for it, no?  Maybe think about that before patting yourself on the back for your cleverness...

> **QUARK**: Fine. Fine. I'm coming.

In case anybody wants to tell you that, by time of this show, the franchise had given up on portraying the Ferengi via Antisemitic tropes, I present you a scene of Quark risking everybody's life because he refuses to part with his money for a while.

> **SISKO**: Major, there are heroes all over Bajor. I'm sitting with one.

I have to appreciate the rejection of {% wiki Great_man_theory|Great Man Theory|en %}, here.  Plenty of people do great things, and they all matter.

> **O'BRIEN**: Listening to Kira talk about all he was, all he did, all he was going to do, she makes him sound like he was larger than life...like he was some kind of military genius. But the Li Nalas I---

Notice that O'Brien only *barely* held in his disdain for a Bajoran folk legend until the only Bajoran left the conversation.  With any luck, I'll remember this when he starts reminiscing about his days fighting the Cardassians.

> **SISKO**: Chief, Li Nalas was the hero of the Bajoran resistance. He performed extraordinary acts of courage for his people and died for their freedom. That's how the history books on Bajor will be written, and that's how I'll remember him when anybody asks.

And true to form, Sisko patiently tells the bigot to shut up and respect the local culture instead of trying to cast himself as a gatekeeper of greatness.

## Conclusions

This episode focuses tightly on the plot, but we still get a bit out of it.

### The Good

Broadly, we see more respect for Bajorans.  We find out that, over the past few months, relationships have blossomed between them and their Starfleet counterparts.  The full Starfleet staff puts themselves in danger to protect them from another Cardassian invasion.  And Sisko makes the entire enterprise about holding the people and culture in high regard.

At least Sisko seems to reject the idea that history progresses due to the brilliance of powerful, famous individuals.

### The Bad

In contrast to the Bajorans, we get reminders that Federation citizens still hold extreme hatreds towards the Ferengi and Cardassians.  And even the respect for the Bajorans doesn't permeate, with Keiko willing to sell them out for her own convenience.  And O'Brien can't wait to tear down one of their idols.

At least Starfleet officers appear to rely so much on technology that they've lost a sense for how the technology actually works without all the conveniences.

O'Brien spends significant time stroking his own ego, treating himself like some military expert, even as he admits failure.

### The Weird

Letters apparently include e-mail.
