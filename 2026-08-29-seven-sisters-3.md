## What Works Well?

As always, this sort of thing comes down to taste and experience, but I'd say that the music has improved dramatically.  The voices still don't have much of a pattern to when we get screeching and when we get speaking or singing, but it sounds more cohesive.

I would actually say that the writing in general has improved.  This seems most evident in the big fight, which we see enough of to understand what happened, as contrasted with prior attempts where they tell us that the fight will start, then have someone comment on its resolution.

Also, speaking of the writing, I'll leave this here.

> **Synthia**: Althaea! Quit flirting with the Devil!
>
> **Althaea**: Ba-a-a-abe, come on, you heard her; she’s been trapped here for eons and has clearly put a lot of work into herself. No one’s been around to tell her how beautiful she is and, "the Devil" or not, every woman should hear it from to time.
>
> **Synthia**: I...sure, fine, and I guess you’re not wrong. She does look...really incredible, actually.

Perfect.  No notes, as they say.

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

The printed lyrics for the album---after the first few tracks---shift to the individual song pages, for some reason, making everything more difficult to follow.

We also have a somewhat critical part of the story---Ch. 7: *Through Flesh And Fire*---released under a non-commercial license, distinct from the rest.  I skipped it, as not Free Culture, even though it seems relevant to understanding what happens.  We haven't seen a move like that since [**Headshot**]({% post_url 2021-07-24-headshot %}).  On the other hand, I suppose that we can all guess what happened after this at the end of *Vengeance*...

> **Synthia**: Oh, I am, babe. We’re going to fight our way through all nine worlds in the terror dimension, collect the keys to the Gates of Ruin, and liberate my predecessor free from the fires of perdition. We’re going to avenge our sisters and the vampires, and we’ll send these goddamn Angels to a Hell of our own making.

...and before this, at the start of *Sophia*...

> **Althaea**: [expletives deleted], we’re walking inside a black hole right now. What are we even standing on? How are we breathing? Are you sure we’ll be able to get out?

I suppose that, as non-Free gaps go, we could miss far worse.  Though the book, which also doesn't seem to carry a public license, might include significantly more.

Actually, this brings up a small point, since I praised the writing earlier.  Overall, it does come out smoother and seems much clearer, but a lot of it also seems redundant, where they'll describe a plan, then we'll have a song about executing exactly that plan.

## What's Adaptable?

We get an entire planet of vampires, exiled from Earth after no longer useful for space travel and resettled when their colony's sun went nova.  And we get Sophia, the Mother of Knowledge, previously known as a certain Father of Lies cast out from among the Angels, imprisoned inside a black hole that (somehow) functions as a library.

Oh, and while the Free Culture parts never *see* it, we get the "nine worlds in the terror dimension" that passes for Hell and the keys to the Gates of Ruin, actually components of a wormhole generator.

Really, though, as I have alluded to for the past couple of weeks, the entire series (with some work replacing the non-commercial bits) feels like the most adaptable thing, here.  Hear me out on this:  Water down the profanity and make the "metal sound" more accessible than this cast does---all acceptable by the terms of the license, remember---and in the right community with the right director, a stage version of these episodes would absolutely *kill* as a high school musical or four, to say nothing of the Internet.  (Please send me a copy...)

## Coming Attractions

Next time, we'll test the waters of **Fantastique Unfettered**, a Free Culture literary magazine launched by (but distinct from) the same editorial team as [**The Aether Age:  Helios**]({% post_url 2024-07-20-helios-1 %}).  This comes with two catches, though.

First, the magazine ran for four issues with multiple stories in each.  Since I don't see myself getting through almost six hundred pages any time soon, we'll go with our usual "sampling" approach to larger projects.  Let's call it four posts, each highlighting what look like the most interesting or distinctive features (stories, poetry, or whatever else) for one issue.  No surprise here, we'll start with the first issue.

Second, the magazine only came out in print.  I contacted the creator when we talked about the other projects, and he didn't have any digital versions.  Because the license does technically permit it, I'll see if I can get at least a bare version of the stories that the posts discuss, *but* I wouldn't get my hopes up, considering that this post came down much closer to the wire than I'd like to admit.

As such, if anyone wants---fairly handsome, for literary magazines, though with low-quality paper---physical copies, you can do what I did and grab them at Bookshop.org.
