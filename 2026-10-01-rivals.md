## Rivals

Contrary to our usual context, this episode didn't get bracketed by episodes of **The Next Generation**.  We had [*Sanctuary*]({% post_url 2026-09-24-sanctuary %}) before and *The Alternate* after, while the *Enterprise* enjoys its winter hiatus, I guess.

> **ALSIA**: When my husband was alive, we ran the business together, and I managed to put away a bit of latinum every year just for myself. Now he's gone, well, I've got quite a little sum.

If Alsia looks familiar to you, then you might recognize her as {% wiki K Callan %}, probably now best known as Martha Kent on **Lois & Clark:  The New Adventures of Superman**---which started around the same time as this show---though with many other appearances.

> **MARTUS**: That's important.

And if Martus looks familiar to you, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Chris Sarandon %}, probably best known as eitherthe evil prince in **The Princess Bride** or marrying a then-Susan Tomalin, though he as done a *lot* more...if not nearly as much as his ex-wife.

> **O'BRIEN**: Yeah. Just finished. I missed playing, and I figured there'd be at least a couple of other players on board. I didn't think you'd be one of them.

They put a lot of effort into coding O'Brien as working class, but while I don't know the actual demographic breakdown[^EDYM3g], {% wiki racquetball %} feels coded as a rich-people thing to me, a lot like squash in [*Suddenly Human*]({% post_url 2023-11-30-suddenly-human %}).

[^EDYM3g]:  A web search turned up one document suggesting that the sport skews towards educated people with disposable income, which backs my position, but also didn't seem to have any research behind it.

> **BASHIR**: Formally I mean. Hey, whatever works, works. This exercise for example, I picked up at a tournament from a top player. It's a five-thousand-year-old battle warm-up. According to the legend, it makes the heart a friend to the hand. Medically, I don't really know what that means, but it seems to give me a lot of energy. Fancy a game?

Also note the comfort with cultural appropriation.  He doesn't have any interest in the culture that he took this ritual from, or the metaphysical principles behind it.

> **BASHIR**: I guess from the lines you prefer the old style rules.

And notice that the obsession with dominance can't even wait for the game...

> **COS**: If only I could be so fortunate.

If Cos looks familiar to you under the prosthetics, then you might recognize him as **Car 54, Where Are You**'s own {% wiki Albert_Henderson_(actor)|Albert Henderson %}.  He'd retire not long after this appearance.

> **O'BRIEN**: The hell he didn't. That smug look on his face. *I guess you prefer old style rules*, like I was some kind of a fossil. In my day, I could've wiped the court with him.

Other than the toxic masculinity that he needs to see himself as dominant, what does this matter?  I mean, if I told you that, if I liked the sport enough to have spent hours every day for decades practicing, I could totally beat Serena Williams at tennis, you might acknowledge that this has some slim chance of having some truth to it, but it also has absolutely no relevance, since that practice *never happened*.  In the same way, unless O'Brien plans to time travel back to "his day" so that he can beat the doctor decades(?) ago, he could claim whatever he wants.

> **BASHIR**: While he was gone, I called my assistant and told her. (May I?) Told her to call me back after five minutes and say there was an emergency at the Infirmary.

I know that the show will change direction, but I like that Bashir has about the same tolerance for O'Brien's macho posturing as I do...

> **DAX**: Honestly, Julian, you're exaggerating. O'Brien can't be a day over thirty-eight.

For reference, {% wiki Colm Meaney %} would have his forty-first birthday coming up a few months later.  As a scientist, Dax should know better than to offer unwarranted precision in your estimates...

> **QUARK**: No? The forty-seventh Rule of Acquisition says *Don't trust a man wearing a better suit than your own*. Either you're a con artist or you're covering up an empty coin purse. I happen to know it's both.

Quark's comments don't usually make it in here unless he specifically comments on the Federation, but do I smell meta-textual criticism of Picard's reflexive trust of every fascist in a blazer?  Wait, in [*The Battle*]({% post_url 2022-07-14-battle %}), when Picard keeps insisting that the Ferengi vessel attacked him, did their captain mention the rule, and Picard took it as a personal affront...?

> **ROANA**: Oh, it's not that. Business was good.

If Roana looks familiar, then you might recognize her as the late {% wiki Barbara Bosson %}, then still famous for her role on **Hill Street Blues**, though I can't not also call out her work on **Cop Rock**[^zqpT6x] and (briefly) the aforementioned **Lois & Clark** show in a couple of years.

[^zqpT6x]:  I need to point it out, not because she (or anybody else) did her best work there, but because how many *other* opportunities do you think that I'll ever get to bring up **Cop Rock**...?

> **O'BRIEN**: You think I'm stupid, too?
>
> **BASHIR**: I don't know what you're talking about.

Wait, when did I start siding with Bashir...?

> **QUARK**: I have a contract for which I paid considerably. All gambling on DS Nine happens at Quark's, or it doesn't happen.
>
> **SISKO**: A few bribes to the Cardassians when they ran this place doesn't constitute a contract, not in the eyes of the Federation.

That...feels pretty racist.  Specifically, change the ethnicities to anything else, and I can't imagine Sisko using the word *bribes* here.  Federation law almost certainly has good reasons to not respect a contract made with a rival government during a military occupation, but if he had formal permission to run the games, with other petitioners rejected on that basis, then he *did* have a legitimate contract, not (only) some quid pro quo for bribery.

> **SISKO**: I didn't beg, I blackmailed you. And don't pretend it hasn't paid off for you either.

Ha!  You might remember that from [*Emissary*]({% post_url 2026-02-26-emissary-1 %}).

> **QUARK**: And all For a worthy cause. Yes, these noble competitors have insisted that I donate half the house's winnings to the Bajoran Fund for Orphans.
>
> **O'BRIEN**: Orphans?

Does...wait, does O'Brien *object* to donating to help orphans?

> **BASHIR**: Actually, you'd be surprised just how often traditional medicine turns out to have genuine value. Did they say was in this stuff?

Notice the framing, here, it might "turn out to have genuine value," not that ancient people have figured things out through experimentation.  The writers of the episode wouldn't have known this at the time, but we now have evidence that [elephants self-medicate](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-53610-4.pdf) with medicinal plants, when they feel ill.  Ancient people got a lot *wrong*, but what they got right in medicine, they got right by checking the results, not because they guessed.

> **BASHIR**: Will be provided by you one way or another, and if the monks don't get their blankets, Chief O'Brien and I'll be very upset. Do I make myself clear?

Do they literally only need blankets?  Because I feel like the crew could handle this regardless of the match.

> **O'BRIEN**: The best I've played my entire life. I'm making shots I couldn't have made fifteen years ago when I was playing five hours a day, every day. I can't miss.

Assuming that Dax had it right, and so we assume O'Brien's age as thirty-eight, then fifteen years ago would've made him twenty-three.  Do twenty-three-year-olds do *anything* discretionary for five hours a day.  That sounds downright pathological to me...

I suppose that he could've put in this kind of practice for some intended athletic career---the only kind of person who I've ever known put that kind of time into a sport---but I feel like that would've come up in the seven years that we've known him.  Plus, it makes him look even more like an ivory tower elitist to have that kind of discretionary time *and* a likelihood of getting discovered.

By the way, when I analogized O'Brien's idea to me going back in time to retroactively become extremely good at a sport, I didn't expect O'Brien to actually mean that.  It feels even sillier, that I failed at parodying the thinking.

## Conclusions

The episode, focusing as it does on Quark's frenemy who has no other connection to the show, doesn't give us much.

### The Bad

We get a reminder that Starfleet largely attracts rich kids to its ranks.

Bashir also shows us that people largely accept cultural misappropriation, while also presuming that ancient cultures developed useful medicine accidentally.  And O'Brien seems to hate the idea of charitable donations to supporting orphans.

The Federation side of the episode largely centers on the obsession with status and dominance that we've seen throughout the franchise.  This stretches from who has the best opinions on how to play the game to insisting that losing could only have happened through aberrations.

Scientists continue to provide precision significantly beyond the margin for error.

They also manage to cram in some racism, characterizing what they'd call fees or rent in any other situation as bribery, because it involved Ferengi and Cardassians.

### The Weird

Young men apparently have enough free time to recreationally play sports for five hours per day.
