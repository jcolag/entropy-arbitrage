## CC25

Creative Commons has this to say on its anniversary page.

> To commemorate our anniversary, CC created a zine featuring submissions from the CC community! The theme is “Remix is resistance,” and features creative works that reflect how remix, reuse, adaptation, and sharing challenge power, build community, preserve culture, and imagine more just futures. Download the zine and assemble by simply folding the pages in half and stacking, or read the [digital version](https://creativecommons.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CC25-Zine-DIGITAL.pdf)!

I don't always have the kindest things to say about Creative Commons as an organization.

- At least some of their founders had deep ties to the growing right-wing movement and Jeffrey Epstein,
- They have often conflated the Free Culture movement with the anti-copyright movement.
- They have undermined the movement using their licenses by suggesting that photographers license degraded copies of their work as an advertisement to buy the "real" versions.
- The organization persists in treating the non-Free licenses as first-class options that probably won't cause problems.
- They keep reminding us of their conclusion that training an LLM probably qualifies as Fair Use, but remain quiet about selling access to those LLMs and using those LLMs to specifically replace the works in the training set.
- They believe that big corporations exploit people because we don't ask them nicely enough to act better towards us, pushing [Signals]({% post_url 2025-06-29-signal-change %}).

However, the fact that I disagree with them on almost every substantive issue doesn't make their well-tested licenses or their compilations of work less useful.  In that sense, I do wish them a happy twenty-fifth anniversary, and hope that they consider using the next twenty-five years to read the room and actually help the people who look to them for guidance.

I originally planned to talk about only the fiction, but it turns out that we only get the one story, so I'll focus on that, but also include the non-fiction in extremely broad strokes.

## What Works Well?

The story could expand in any number of directions, fairly impressive given its short length.  Most prominently, we get an implication that the stone changes something.  But we also get the idea that we create meaning, rather than discovering it.

On the non-fiction side, one essay---and sadly only one---considers an idea for the movement that I wish that I had considered, and will almost certainly borrow:  "The refusal to forget," or that copying and remixing "becomes an act of care only when it does not cut the thread."  Specifically, borrowing and adapting works from other cultures only makes sense when we honor the sources.  Otherwise, we misappropriate a culture that probably has less power than our own, giving us the harmful "erasure, exploitation, or denigration" model of cultural appropriation from Julia Serrano's work.

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

The story does have some odd inconsistencies, like a water well in an urban alley.  I suppose that such things must have existed at some point, but I can't recall ever seeing evidence of it in an actual city.

Beyond the story, the zine seems to have an unspoken theme of "remix as resistance."  And if you've read this blog for long, you can probably already guess what I have to say:  Since nobody seems to identify *what* it resists, all the essays feel juvenile, that cultural works exist as raw material to exploit, rather than people's traditions, one (by Kyriaki Papadopoulou) even calling out (unnamed) indigenous communities where it requires "permission from elders" to share certain information, lumping it in with non-commercial licenses.  Do we really *want* to resist indigenous identity and traditions (again)...?  Another (by Oby Ezeilo) positions the resistance against a history of colonization that "archived us incorrectly," but I don't know that *my* hypothetically taking Igbo culture for my own work at all resists colonization.

In fact, it seems odd---except for the prior failings at Creative Commons, I suppose---that none of this "resistance" involves people sharing modern works or spreading value and authority to disadvantaged communities.  From my perspective on things, we need to resist that attitude, that culture only matters as a resource to catalogue and exploit...

## Six Years Hence:  Drakes

I don't think that I have much to add to the [original post on **Drakes**]({% post_url 2020-10-24-drakes %}).  The author wrote me at some point to confirm that he did want to model PTSD in the protagonist's actions.  He also recommended, if/when these posts get around to revisiting creators, that we largely ignore the other books in this series as works with less skill put into them, in favor of [**The Necromancer's Apprentice**](https://github.com/shakna-israel/NecromancersApprentice).  And it appears that he "rebooted" the series with [**Suzie Drakes**](https://git.sr.ht/~shakna/suziedrakes).

Despite the author's clear distaste with his earlier writing, though, I did find the original well worth reading.  In that sense, I disagree with the assessment, but I hope that the reboot pans out.
