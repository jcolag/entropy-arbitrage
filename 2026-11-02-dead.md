* Ignore for ToC
{:toc}

In most of the Mexican-influenced world, today people celebrate (the latter half) of the {% wiki Day of the Dead %}, dedicated to remembering family and friends who have died.  While I strongly recommend against aping the aesthetic---that slides quickly into the most harmful kinds of misappropriation every single time---it does feel like one of the holidays that every culture should have[^xRthhs], but somehow doesn't.  We can all do far worse than taking a beat to privately pay tribute to the people who we've lost.

[^xRthhs]:  I say similar things about Iran's Yalda, the celebration of the winter solstice where people gather to ward off the longest night of the year.

![The Skull of the Female Dandy, a skeleton in a fancy hat](/blog/assets/skull-female-dandy.png "Wait until she meets Memento Maury...")

I would also like to draw attention to the {% wiki International Day to End Impunity for Crimes Against Journalists %}, a mouthful, but at the moment, the United States (among other countries) currently has political and corporate leadership more than happy to endanger journalists who threaten their corrupt practices.  At the moment, investigations of murders of journalists globally have a whopping *thirteen percent* resolution rate, meaning that the killers only risk about a one-in-eight chance of even getting caught.

And since I mentioned the decline in the United States, to those of my fellow Americans who haven't done so already, make sure to *vote* tomorrow.  We can't fix any of this without checks in place.

## Clearer Licensing, Part 2

To recap the point of this exercise, since it changes the format of these posts so much, the public licenses that I use all include an attribution requirement to some extent.  And while the AI industry---even in what look a lot like the first death throes---continues to use its software to launder copyright as "intelligence," it has started to make a bigger stink about that attribution.

However, I haven't always had attribution in mind when building things, so somebody who stumbles on a lot of my work and wants to redistribute or adapt it would then need to remember where they found it and then try to run down my information.  To make this as convenient as possible, I chose the [REUSE](https://reuse.software/) standard, since it ensures that every file has attribution and licensing information; going file by file also nicely resolves some common edge-cases, such as when I include a file from someone else's project or when worrying about whether anybody has the right under the AGPL to see the alterations to a configuration file, revealing API keys and passwords.

This time through, I got the following into shape.

- [Board Games](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/board-games), which I have put some behind-the-scenes work into to get that new book ready
- My [Periodic Scripts](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/periodic-scripts)
- [Comics](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/comics), actually only one, so far
- [Boring CSS](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/boring-css)
- [Socialite](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/socialite)
- [Iungimoji](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/iungimoji), though I temporarily claim credit for the JSON file of emoji out of expediency, because I couldn't track down where I got it from, probably legally acceptable, since you probably can't secure a copyright on such a mechanical file.
- [Neuron Drizzle](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/neuron-drizzle), a project that I should probably get going
- [SaLaVi](https://codeberg.org/jcolag/salavi)

I probably won't hit *every* project---because it takes quite a bit of work getting everything right and then fighting over obscure file-types---but I want to get "important" projects settled, so that nobody has the excuse that proper attribution takes too much effort.
