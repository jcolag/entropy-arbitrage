## Recipe

As suggested last week, I did try turning my [attempt at oat milk]({% post_url 2026-10-12-lovelace %}) into yogurt.  Combining the rice cooker yogurt instructions with the yogurt starter instructions, I heated the oat milk first, then added the starter and two tablespoons of sugar.  The "yogurt setting" runs for eight hours, following that by a two-hour rest, then chilling at least six hours, which for me meant overnight.

The starter instructions repeatedly emphasize that vegan yogurts other than soy don't thicken, so I didn't have any expectations on that front, beyond the thickness of the milk.  Weirdly, the I-don't-know-if-I-can-call-it-yogurt *did* thicken, but ended up with a fairly mild but decent flavor.

Meanwhile, I think that the sauerkraut flopped.  I get a distinctly briny flavor out of it, not sour.  If I had to guess, my innovation of using parchment paper filled with pie weights possibly choked off the air supply.  In addition, the time soaking weakened the parchment paper, so I needed to clean up that mess, and can't really recommend following my lead on that...

## Sewing, Part 2

I probably should've made this before [last week's cloth mask]({% post_url 2026-10-12-lovelace %}#sewing), but it also made some sense to practice first on something that I wouldn't care about.

![A long, narrow pillow in the now-obligatory painful orange](/blog/assets/neck-pillow.png "It also makes a satisfying thumping sound when dropped")

Regardless, I have had this thoroughly unsophisticated project in the back of my mind for at least a year.

Often, when I have some trouble falling asleep, I usually want a pillow that lets me lie flat *but* that also props up my neck.  The advent of memory foam seems to have divided pillows into enormous bricks (or wedges) designed for people who apparently want to sleep sitting up versus bags with filler that slip away from the head, creating a useless dumbbell shape where the head and neck have no support, both of which make things bad enough that I spend a lot of (especially) the summer tossing the pillows aside to feel slightly less uncomfortable.

This, a somewhat floppy cylinder roughly the diameter of my forearm (because I know that roughly fits that nape area), I filled pretty well with buckwheat hulls that I bought a long while ago for the purpose.  It'll go in a pillowcase---whether I make one, cut down and refinish an existing one, or lazily fold an existing one over---and runs slightly shorter than the width of my other pillows[^4kq248], so I don't much care about the color.

[^4kq248]:  The width comes less from technical reasons than from my getting tired of scooping buckwheat hulls.

Speaking of the color, the original version of my idea for this involved using a cheap "protecting" pillow cover as fabric, figuring that would save at least some of the measuring and cutting effort.  Once I acquired such a protector and started cutting, though, I realized that the cheap weave fell apart quickly without support *and* it didn't breathe, which would defeat part of the purpose of using the buckwheat hulls.  Say what you will about the safety-vest orange, but I did get cotton, and it does breathe fairly well...

As you can see on the one end, I didn't really plan a closure---surely, I knew how to do this, at some point---so it only folds over at the end with two seams run through it, and hadn't yet (when I took the picture) cut off the unused part.  If I ever need to go back in to adjust the fill, I can think about adding a zipper or buttons to give it a cleaner appearance, and look into how to keep the little hulls from working their way out through the gaps that any fasteners would create.  If anybody wants to use me as a guide to your own work, though, it took me about half an hour to get the right rectangle of fabric, double-stitch the seams, and shovel in the hulls.

And it does the job well, fitting to the curve of my neck, preventing my head from bending forward as I lie on my back.  Honestly, I should've done this years ago, especially considering the price.  Figure that a yard of this fabric (inexplicably shipping as a flattened tube) runs about five feet wide total, so assuming a twelve-by-eighteen inch rectangle---ignore the unused tail---comes to exactly a tenth of a yard.  At a dollar-fifty per yard, that comes to a negligible fifteen cents.  I got the buckwheat hulls (ten pounds, in case I wanted to make other things, and free shipping may have kicked in there) for twenty-seven bucks, and used less than a sixth of it[^NTmN96], so we can call the whole thing four-fifty total, including the thread though not including the half-hour of labor.  Though admittedly, that kind of price on *any* fabric admittedly doesn't come along often anymore, hence my stocking up.

[^NTmN96]:  Their bag that the company shipped had a small hole, so I split the ten pounds into three smaller bags, of which I used less than half of one bag for this, otherwise I'd have no idea what a sixth looked like unless I weighed the pillow.

Some buttons also got reattached to pants---bent a needle at the tail end, too, so that needed replacing---and a couple of older shirts got patched, but that probably doesn't make for interesting show-and-tell for these posts.
