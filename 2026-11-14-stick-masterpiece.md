## Stick's Masterpiece and Adventure

The creators describe the first book as follows.

> Cute, little Stick doesn’t know how to paint a masterpiece, but she can do small and simple things. These small and simple things, composed over a lifetime, become her masterpiece. And the result is more beautiful than she could have imagined.

Likewise, they describe the second book.

> Cute, little Stick sets off on a grand adventure pursuing big dreams and exciting goals. Along the way she learns that life’s sweetest moments happen along the way. It’s not the destination, but the journey that makes the adventure wonderful.

As I hinted last week in mentioning that we'd head for at least the first book of the two, it surprises me that we haven't talked about this before.  The book(s) didn't get a *lot* of attention when they came out, but they definitely entered the conversation by providing something *not* targeted at an audience that you can bet has a favorite public license.

## What Works Well?

The first book has a fairly solid message, presented in what I'd call an appealing way.  It has a peculiar flaw in introducing the idea that the world changes in response to the art, but then not only ignores it for the rest of the story, but tells us that Stick doesn't notice.

Actually, the first book has quite a few clever structural bits.  For one example, when the rhyme spans two pages, the text has a similar layout tying the two together.  The writing also feels fluid enough that I didn't even *notice* the rhymes until the second book beats you over the head with it.

And...look, I don't have much good to say about the second book, and this will sound like "damning with faint praise."  While I don't find the art in the second book at all to my tastes---a different design for Stick, set against photographic backgrounds, for some reason---I will say that it does a fairly good job integrating the drawn portions with the photographs.  The two-dimensional character actually looks positioned in the real space.

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

As mentioned, the first book has a moment where it introduces a clever idea that seems designed to draw the character's attention to the nature of the world.  It then informs us that Stick didn't notice, but somehow still ends up drawing exactly the right conclusion

By contrast, the second book strikes me as completely incomprehensible.  It seems to want to tell three unrelated stories, one a fantasy sequence, ignores the problem to solve that it introduces, seems to want to make the fantasy sequence real for the purposes of the story, then undoes the entire story and literally brings us back to the first page preparing to go for a walk.  It also has lower-quality writing, I think, drawing attention to clunkier rhyming...and a horrid attempt at a half-rhyme that it tries to force by mangling a word, then tries again with different match for the same word.
