## Profit and Loss

For our usual context, this episode lands between [*Genesis*]({% post_url 2025-10-02-genesis %}) and [*Journey's End*]({% post_url 2025-10-09-journey-s-end %}), the latter of which will become fairly important for us in a couple of weeks.  Over at the competition, **Babylon 5** had aired *And the Sky Full of Stars*, but had about a month off until the next episode.

We won't spend much relevant time with the Federation folks, this time, so we'll skip ahead.

> **NATIMA**: I think we're all fine, considering. I'm Professor Natima Lang. These are two of my students, Rekelen and Hogue.

If our primary guest looks familiar, then you might recognize her as {% wiki Mary Crosby %}, as in Bing's daughter and Denise's aunt.  She may not have gotten out from her father's shadow, but in a long career, she also had center stage on at least one of broadcast television's biggest moments, the {% wiki Who shot J.R.? %} arc on **Dallas**.  That *might* become relevant during the argument around the midpoint of this episode, though I couldn't tell you whether the writers intended it.

> **GARAK**: It all comes down to a question of loyalty. My dear Doctor, Yiri had to choose between protecting his brother and protecting the state. He chose the state, as would I, every time.

While he got a mention more recently, we last saw Garak in [*Cardassians*]({% post_url 2026-08-20-cardassians %}).

> **HOGUE**: Is this Ferengi bothering you?

If the creep looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Michael Reilly Burke %}, who we actually saw in [*Descent*]({% post_url 2025-05-29-descent-2 %}), and would go on to guest in a few other science fiction (and adjacent) shows, including a recurring role on **Enterprise**.

> **REKELEN**: Her teachings will change the future of Cardassia.

And if "the other one" looks familiar, then you might recognize her as {% wiki Heidi Swedberg %}, who has mostly left acting for music.

> **SISKO**: Being shot at by your own people goes a little beyond politics.

You might want to put that line in your back pocket for a couple of weeks...

> **QUARK**: I owe you. You saved my life. If you'd turned me over to the authorities, I would have been executed.
>
> **NATIMA**: I admired your courage. It was a brave thing you were doing selling food to the Bajorans. I thought you were a man of honor.
>
> ...
>
> **QUARK**: So I took a little money from the Cardassian Communication Service. They could afford it.

You might remember my raising the accusation of Quark's collaboration with the Cardassians in [*Shadowplay*]({% post_url 2026-11-05-shadowplay %}), set against the peculiar idea that Odo would never do such a thing.  Here, we find out that Quark's version of this involved him undermining and embezzling from the occupation, and risking a relationship that he valued to do it.  And I have to admit that doesn't sound much like collaborating, beyond keeping up appearances.

> **SISKO**: Then relay this. Tell the Central Command if anyone attempts to take Hogue and Rekelen by force, I will respond in kind. Am I clear, Mister Garak?
>
> ...
>
> **SISKO**: I'd like nothing better, but the provisional government has ordered you all be turned over. I'm fighting this every way I can. I'm not ready to give up yet. But in the end, this is a Bajoran station, and I have to abide by their decision no matter how much I disagree with it.

We don't see this often enough from Starfleet.  We have a firm moral stance, even in the face of two groups who arguably have more authority and one almost certainly has more firepower than he can access.  It only disappoints me that his sparring with Garak doesn't make the point that the Cardassians wouldn't risk another war with the Federation unless these three had a massive following.

> **QUARK**: Later will be too late. **I, the Jury**, by Mickey Spillane?
>
> **ODO**: Chief O'Brien loaned it to me. This better be important, Quark.

The first of the {% wiki Mike_Hammer_(character)|Mike Hammer %} novels came out in 1947, shockingly, not the 1930s like you'd expect from a former *Enterprise*-D officer.  It does track that O'Brien would recommend it, though, a detective known for his violence, machismo, and anti-communist flag-waving.  I feel like Odo would hate it, mind you, but O'Brien suggesting it definitely feels right.

As to Spillane himself, some might find it interesting that, in the years prior to Mike Hammer, he wrote for comics, including on Captain America, Captain Marvel, Batman, and Superman scripts, plus Harvey's (obscure) Mike Lancer, Hammer's prototype.  Oh, and one more thing:  Among other screen roles, he also played the victim on a **Columbo** episode.

## Quick Commentary

I...didn't expect to enjoy this episode this much, for some reason.  It doesn't show, because almost none of the story has anything to do with these posts, but it "understands the assignment," as they say, using the visiting Cardassians as an allegory for the often-unpopular resistance to the militarization of democratic societies, and the resulting crackdowns on dissent.

And it gives us one of the better scripts, certainly the best of the past few weeks---not that I'd call any of them *bad*---with a lot of humor.  And we even limit O'Brien to one grunt that only vaguely implies his racism...

## Conclusions

As mentioned, we don't get much out of this, unless you count the persistence of Mickey Spillane novels.

### The Good

Sisko fights (politically speaking) to prevent outsiders from becoming political prisoners, and persists in this when he loses.

### The Bad

The episode makes it fairly clear that the accusations that the local Ferengi population collaborated with the occupational government has more to do with their ethnicity than their actions, because they apparently robbed the Cardassians and sometimes helped the Bajorans.
