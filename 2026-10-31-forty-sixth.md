We'll ignore the cover song.

If the behind-the-scenes choices matter to anybody other than me, despite the short length, I chose this because it fit the usual criteria---lyrics and an appropriate license---but because it stood out with the tag *scary*, which lined up nicely with this going out at the end of October.  As I have said a few times over the years, I don't have much love for Halloween, but I know that many other people do love it, and I don't want to stomp on your fun.

Did I mention that they wrote/sang the lyrics in Japanese?  As usual, I read them in translation, since they didn't sing about cats existing here or there.

## What Works Well?

Mostly, I think that I like the music itself, or at least can appreciate the skill behind it.

And *256 theses* has at least the makings of an interesting story.  A sufficiently motivated listener might even hear some echoes of the ideas in [*Cult of the Nu Girl*]({% post_url 2025-09-20-cult-nu-girl %}) and similar works.

## What's Adaptable?

Nothing gets a name, but as mentioned, *256 theses* does present at least a coherent dystopian vision that someone might find useful.

## Six Years Hence:  MTF Sigma-5 Pumpkin Punchers

I can't think of anything to add to the [original post on [*MTF Sigma-5 "Pumpkin Punchers"*]({% post_url 2020-10-31-pumpkin %}), really, other than that the story's flaws (in my eyes, at least) led me to write [*The Gevkahahal*]({% post_url 2021-10-31-gevkahahal %}) the following Halloween, taking loose inspiration from the SCP Foundation story and even looser inspiration from Charles M. Schulz's Great Pumpkin.

The [SCP Foundation](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/) itself, meanwhile, has continued to roll along, crossing ten thousand of their "formal"---as in, not including side-chains and other projects---dossiers not too long ago, and currently in conversations with film studios (trying to chase the success of **The Backrooms**) about how to adapt their work commercially while complying with the CC BY-SA license.
