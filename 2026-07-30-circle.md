## The Circle

For our usual context, this episode comes immediately after [*Homecoming*]({% post_url 2026-07-23-homecoming %}) that we talked about last week, and of which this serves as part two of three, and [*Interface*]({% post_url 2025-06-12-interface %}).

> **JARO**: But I expected you to be celebrating. I was under the impression that Major Kira has been nothing but trouble to you since---

Notice, again, that we see hints that everybody else sees a far different relationship between Sisko and Kira than we do.

> **JARO**: Commander, Starfleet doesn't consult us when they wish to promote one of their officers.

While I suppose that this technically provides no actual information, it does strongly imply that at least one member of the station's Starfleet leadership got the job over the Bajoran government's objections.  Granted, that doesn't narrow it down.  Sisko's presence seems awkward as the reluctant Emissary of the Prophets.  Dax nearly got convicted of murder in, well, [*Dax*]({% post_url 2026-04-16-dax %}), with no indication that she wouldn't do it again on Bajoran soil.  O'Brien not only has his...entire personality, but also brought along his proud heretic wife who presumably refers to "the visibly ringed planet in Earth's Star System" to avoid referring to {% wiki Saturn_%28mythology%29|Saturn|en %} or {% wiki Sol_%28Roman_mythology%29|Sol|en %}.  And not wanting Bashir around probably doesn't even need an explanation...

> **SISKO**: Yes, of course I do. For a minute I was thinking to myself, there's a warm wind blowing in from Minicoy.

{% wiki Minicoy|Maliku|en %}, to use its native name, actually exists, an Indian island.  However, I can't find any evidence of the "old saying," so one imagines that the ambassador came on the scene in the future.  Or maybe they only come to prominence in the historical record in the future, since I could absolutely imagine this describing some colonial Brit.

> **JAKE**: Look.

I thought that we had finished with the overt authoritarianism, but notice how they frame this as a huge escalation, that the threatening symbol appearing around the station and all over Bajor has now appeared on *the boss's doorstep*.  And...OK?  We already knew that they hate humans, and other than the urgent need to recast the role, I don't see how endangering Sisko escalates anything, unless we believe that his rank means that he matters more...

> **BAREIL**: I hope I'm not interrupting.

We met Bareil during [*In the Hands of the Prophets*]({% post_url 2026-07-09-in-hands-prophets %}).

Not relevant to us, but I should also mention that I love that entire preceding sequence.  It gets us the emotional delivery of losing a valued colleague and friend, while also making providing plenty of fun.

> **SISKO**: I'm going to get you back, Major. I promise that to both of us.

If the scene of everybody wishing Kira well has a flaw, I'd say that it doesn't connect to this moment well enough.  They keep pitching the idea of asking Sisko to intervene, and it doesn't occur to any of them that he already has at least part of a plan, because *he supports people*.

> **WINN**: That's right. Major Kira. From the space station. What brings you here?

Heh.  "From *the space station*," as if it has no name.  Compare it to the Federation's refusal to use Bajoran terms for anything important...

And like Bareil, we met Winn during *In the Hands of the Prophets*, too.  She popped her head into the vision, too, but I didn't bother to mention her there.

> **KRIM**: Have them withdraw from the Northeast quadrant. Move them to join the fourteenth column south of the city.

If General Krim (not of **The Independent**) looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Stephen_Macht|Stephen Macht|en %}, who among other things including an appearance near the middle of **Babylon 5**, nearly landed the role of some obscure character named Jean-Luc Picard.

> **KRIM**: That's out of my purview, Commander. You might want to discuss it with Minister Jaro. Commander Sisko, you told me about the Kressari before you asked the favor regarding Kira. You could've tried to trade that information for the favor.
>
> **SISKO**: I wouldn't do that.

This feels like another fairly firm rejection of **The Next Generation**'s typical storytelling, where the crew frequently bartered critical information for some concession or other.

> **ZEF'NO**: Somebody had better be able to help me. I have been delayed for six hours. I can assure you the Kressarian government will lodge a formal protest

Less prominent than a lot of the guest cast that we have floating around, but you might recognize the captain as {% wiki Mike_Genovese|Mike Genovese|en %}, probably most prominent before this as antagonists in a couple of Richard Pryor films, though we also saw him (briefly) as the desk sergeant in [*The Big Goodbye*]({% post_url 2022-08-04-big-goodbye %}).

> **CARDASSIAN**: I think you'll find everything's in order.

This move irritates me, because it signals that we'll shrug off the problems as "outside agitators" instead of using the episode's metaphor to hold up a mirror to bigotry in our society.  Granted, we do that in our world, too, dismissing political polarization as something that happens because of Russian bots and not, say, centuries of ignoring misogyny, colonization, and slavery, or that we have so many organizations oriented towards exploiting us all because of a (conveniently dead) sex trafficker blackmailing them, rather than exploitation forming the backbone[^cibkxR] of capitalism.

[^cibkxR]:  You can like or dislike capitalism, but if employers paid a fair wage and sold their wares for a fair price, by definition, they'd never turn a profit, so they wouldn't go into business.  Therefore, if nobody got cheated, then you couldn't have capitalism.

Oh, and if no-name looks familiar, then you might recognize {% wiki Anthony_Guidera|Anthony Guidera|en %}, who apparently passed away only two months ago.

> **JARO**: Major, I don't have to tell you. I've heard your opinion of this government. Government. They can't even agree it is a government, so they call it provisional. It's just another word for powerless. I won't allow the Bajoran people to be powerless anymore. We have been doormats to the Cardassians and now the Federation. Any great power that happens by. We will not be that again. We are a people who brought art and architecture to countless planets. We don't deserve to be victims.

Most of his speech doesn't make any difference, but zero in on "a people who brought art and architecture to countless planets," for a moment.  Jumping around the timeline a bit, we'll get a fair amount of corroborating if circumstantial evidence of this throughout the series, such as indications of a pre-industrial space program.  Bajor doesn't have colony worlds, though, and no splinter ethnicities that we know of[^WUpqff], it makes sense to ask why this hasn't come up frequently, why it hasn't even come up before now.

[^WUpqff]:  You *could* make a decent case for the Cardassians as potential descendants of ancient Bajorans, but it requires cherry-picking evidence and making a lot of unfounded guesses.

The original series gave the impression of a prior flowering of civilization in the galaxy, mostly of cultures that fled underground and/or replaced themselves with androids, so the Federation does love this stuff.  Picard, meanwhile, claims to love archaeology, legends, and the Bajoran people.  And we've spent an entire year watching Bajorans.  And their lack of a colonial empire could mean that they strictly adhered to something like the Prime Directive, but actually took it seriously.  But the first time we hear about this, it comes from the bigoted confidence trickster---getting help from a rival nation, which doesn't sound familiar *at all* in 2026...---giving his scenery-chewing reveal-the-plan-to-the-protagonist cliché.  And that seems odd.

> **SISKO**: Doctor, Lieutenant, you're with me. Mister O'Brien, you'll be our pilot. Configure the runabout sensors for a full sweep of the peninsula. Once we find the base, I'll be depending a great deal on your famous transporter talents.

Heh.  As a chief engineer, he makes an *excellent* transporter technician...

> **CHEKOTE**: I think so, Commander. How's it look?

Home stretch of the episode, and we bring in {% wiki Bruce_Gray|Bruce Gray|en %}, who has...made the rounds for decades, until (a few months after) his 2017 death.  We saw him in [*Gambit*]({% post_url 2025-06-19-gambit-part-1 %}), which when this aired still sat about two weeks in the future.

> **CHEKOTE**: But internal to Bajor. The Cardassians might involve themselves in other people's civil wars, but we don't. The Prime Directive applies, Ben.

Does it, though?  I realize that the Prime Directive usually means whatever allows the crew to check out instead of helping people, but the *letter* of the law focuses on not letting on about alien life and space travel, right?  And they've allowed interference on far flimsier grounds than a rival power funding one faction.

## Conclusions

We mostly had the plot to deal with, this time, and *so much* Bajoran politics, but let's give it a try anyway...

### The Good

We continue to see Sisko working to treat everybody with respect, despite people consistently misreading him.

### The Bad

While not specific enough to identify what they have happening, the comments about the alleged fractious relationship between Sisko and Kira, the lack of communication with Bajor about DS9's management, the expectation that Sisko wouldn't provide help without a quid pro quo, Winn's disinterest in using the name "Deep Space Nine," and the indication that something has suppressed conversation about Bajor's effect on the ancient galaxy all strongly indicate that the Federation tends not to respect the culture.

We see a return to the idea that threats to people in general pale in comparison to threats to powerful individuals and their families.

The Prime Directive continues to mean whatever the powerful find expedient in a given situation, rather than a stand for principles.
