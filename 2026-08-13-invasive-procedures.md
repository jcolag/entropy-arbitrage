## Invasive Procedures

For our usual context, this episode lands between [*Gambit, Part II*]({% post_url 2025-06-26-gambit-part-2 %}) and [*Phantasms*]({% post_url 2025-07-03-phantasms %}).  And actually, speaking of *Gambit*, [*The Siege*]({% post_url 2026-08-06-siege %}) last week also wrapped up the show's tentative first steps into outright serialized storytelling.  If you think about it, the franchise hadn't really tried to push a story beyond two episodes, and usually with a summer or other hiatus between the episodes.

Even the films, even if you consider [**The Wrath of Khan**]({% post_url 2022-03-24-twok %}), [**The Search for Spock**]({% post_url 2022-03-31-tsfs %}), [**The Voyage Home**]({% post_url 2022-04-07-tvh %}), and [**The Final Frontier**]({% post_url 2022-04-14-tff %}) one continuous story, those four films hit theaters spaced across seven years (and five days), or an average of two-and-a-third years between "episodes."  Seeing those three episodes air alongside *Gambit*---which similarly aired two weeks in a row instead of wrapping a long break---definitely gave a feel that the franchise wanted to explore something new...though we don't get that this week.  This looks more like "throw in a standalone episode to give the audience a chance to reorient," especially with the (putting it as charitably as possible) tone poem in *Phantasms* the following week.

> Station log, stardate 47182.1. DS-Nine has been temporarily evacuated due to a violent plasma disruption. Until the disturbance ends, we'll be forced to maintain the station with a skeleton crew.

After my introduction, it almost seems funny to open this way, since they absolutely could've blamed the skeleton crew on needing to pick up the evacuees from *The Siege*.  But I guess that they wanted to emphasize the standalone nature, here.

> **ODO**: The only reason you volunteered is because there wasn't any room on the evacuation shuttles for six hundred bars of gold-pressed latinum. Which still doesn't explain what you're doing out here in the docking ring.

Again, didn't we do exactly this joke last week, suggesting that they could've made this some consequence of the previous three episodes?  And I have to say, the Antisemitic canard disguised as a joke didn't become *less* offensive in the intervening week.

> **QUARK**: That doesn't mean I don't love him. Chief, do you have any brothers?
>
> **O'BRIEN**: As a matter of fact, I have two.

I know that they don't provide an order, but (extremely debunked) {% wiki Middle_child_syndrome|middle kid syndrome %} would explain a *lot* about O'Brien.

> **VERAD**: This is the cargo ship *Ekina*. We've been caught by the storm. Our engines have been severely damaged. We need emergency assistance. Can you read me? Repeat, this is the cargo ship *Ekina*...

If Verad sounds (and soon looks) familiar to you, then you may recognize him as {% wiki John_Glover_(actor)|John Glover %}, probably best known for his run as Lex Luthor's father on **Smallville** and the Trump proxy antagonist in **Gremlins 2**, but you also don't need to search far to find him appearing elsewhere...

> **T'KAR**: You see? The ever-helpful Federation. So predictable.

It looks like the people who don't see the Federation as conniving and warmongering see them as incompetent and gullible.  That seems especially funny, considering that Picard fairly directly interfered in Klingon affairs in [*Redemption*]({% post_url 2024-05-02-redemption %}) **and** threatened to do so again in [*Unification*]({% post_url 2024-06-27-unification-part-1 %}) in order to secure Klingon help in a war, living up to the *usual* reputation, but not this one, given how often they've tried to use the Prime Directive to justify letting people die.

And if T'Kar looks familiar, then you might recognize him as {% wiki Tim_Russ|Tim Russ %}, who showed up in [*Starship Mine*]({% post_url 2025-03-20-starship-mine %}) and [**Generations**]({% post_url 2022-04-28-generations %}), and would soon---as in, opposite to the middle of **DS9**'s next season---land a regular role on **Voyager**.  He also lends his voice to the **Khan** podcast, and has a brief scene in the final season of **Picard**, among his voice work and career that has nothing to do with *Star Trek* at all...

> **O'BRIEN**: Listen, friend, in case you haven't heard the Federation and the Klingon Empire are allies now.

The alliance still exists?  I got the impression that they broke up a while back for no clear reason.

> **MAREEL**: This one must be the shape-shifter.

And if Mareel looks familiar, then you might recognize her as {% wiki Megan_Gallagher|Megan Gallagher %}, mostly known for her stage work, but we'll see her again before this show ends, as well as on **Voyager**.

> **VERAD**: I don't have any choice. I spent my whole life trying to qualify for the joining. I studied constantly every day, every waking hour. I sacrificed everything, and then I went before the Symbiosis Evaluation Board, and they reduced my entire life to one word. Unsuitable.

I suppose that we don't know whether the Trill actually live as part of the Federation, but if so, this gives some indication of the pressure that people feel to excel, much like we've seen with children since (I believe) [*Coming of Age*]({% post_url 2022-09-22-coming-age %}).

> **VERAD**: No, not just Verad. I'm Verad Dax.

Do the Trill worm-people not have any morality of their own?  Surely, if it brings along its own personality and those of prior hosts, then one would expect Dax to have some objection to doing this in its name.  Instead, it seems fairly content to ride this out and see where it all goes.  In that sense, it seems odd that such an amoral nature didn't come up in [*Dax*]({% post_url 2026-04-16-dax %}).

> **BASHIR**: Everything went perfectly. The operation was a complete success.

Yeah, that doesn't sound nearly as comforting for the person who lost a vital organ in the deal.

> **MAREEL**: Senarian egg broth. It'll keep your strength up. All right, don't drink it. It makes no difference to me.
>
> **O'BRIEN**: Hold on. Thank you.

I know, shocking that O'Brien would treat an alien woman from the hospitality industry poorly.  Racism, sexism, or classism, take your pick...

## Conclusions

This feels like one of the least-insightful episodes that we've had since at least **The Animated Series**, but still...

### The Bad

Among some Klingons, the Federation has a reputation for relaxing security to help people.

If the Trill have Federation citizenship, Verad's angst expands the story that we've seen of children feeling intense pressure to succeed at something to adults.

And O'Brien mostly contributes to the episode by grumping at a foreign woman trying to ease his pain, then angrily deigning to accept her help.

### The Weird

The Federation apparently still or again has some sort of formal alliance with the Klingon Empire.
