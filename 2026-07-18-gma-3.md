## Galactic Mutant Animals

The creator describes the as follows.

> Galactic Mutant Animals is a comic I drew when I was twelve (~1993). Probably not the best comic you'll read today, but if somewhere a kid sees it & wants to create its own comic I'll be happy. Sorry you have to speak French to understand all the hilarious jokes.

The creator also provides this biography.

> I draw on my spare time, and love sharing my art under 'libre'/free license ; all my works use CC-BY-SA.
>
> I love monsters.

Occasionally, I see the creator posting new art on Mastodon, which led me here.

## What Works Well?

Maybe this hits because I grew up around the referenced comics (even if I didn't regularly read them), but I found the background pages on the X-nimals surprisingly funny.  You could almost imagine Chris Claremont adding a wolf with mechanical tentacles or a trident-wielding octopus, for example.  Although the writing may have improved over the forty-or-so pages, because we also have giddy moments the pages sneak in a clever and well-structured jab at the excitement of the ship having a *high-definition television* in 2894, something in the middle of standardization around the time the creator wrote this.

We only get a few---five, for the pedants---pages into the new story, it has a lot more promise, setting up a series of conventional problems, rather than yet another not-really-motivated fight.  It actually strikes me as insightful to lead with the violence to encourage reading without much emotional investment before trying to sell us on the interpersonal angst; the big franchises still haven't figured that one out.

## What Works...Less Well?

{% include lesswell.md %}

I mentioned that we have only started the third story, and that it seems like a fun change in direction, but I also feel like I need to point out how disjointed it feels.  Every half-page to page, it feels like it wants to start over.  We get the mutant vegetables, then the mutant armadillos and their slice-of-life bits.  From there, we go to play the same game as before, but against the Battle-Frogs.  Then we shift to a biology lab, they meet the vegetables, then meet the Rodent-Rangers and go watch movies.  None of it seems to connect or suggest a next step, so fingers crossed that it pulls together next week.
