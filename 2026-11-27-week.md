[As Americans gather for Thanksgiving, a centuries-old Sikh tradition offers an expansive vision of hospitality](https://theconversation.com/as-americans-gather-for-thanksgiving-a-centuries-old-sikh-tradition-offers-an-expansive-vision-of-hospitality-296562) from The Conversation @TheConversationUS@newsie.social

> Preparing and serving langar is a form of “seva,” often translated as selfless service. In his chapter on Sikh economic ethics, religious studies scholar Pashaura Singh explains that Guru Nanak taught people to earn a living through honest work and share what they earned with those in need. The free communal kitchen puts that teaching into practice.

Hashtags:  #Thanksgiving #Sikh

While I haven't *seriously* hosted a holiday in years, I used to have a tradition---starting shortly after college, really, when I largely took over the kitchen---of making sure that people in my family's circle who didn't have family nearby (or a willingness to spend the day with them) had someplace to go.  I can't recommend that enough, so I can understand the value in the more structured service described.

{% embed https://images.theconversation.com/files/771740/original/file-20261112-64-y5yp1y.jpg|A concerned person looking at a computer screen|false| %}

<i class="fas fa-square"></i> [Catching AI’s mistakes is a mental burden – and a drag on productivity](https://theconversation.com/catching-ais-mistakes-is-a-mental-burden-and-a-drag-on-productivity-291748) from The Conversation @TheConversationUS@newsie.social

> People feel a cognitive burden because verification is fundamentally different from creation. You generally know when something you are creating is “done,” or is at least sufficient for the task. When to stop verification is less obvious. A worker must decide what to check and how; they do not know whether an error exists, where it might be or what form it might take.

The last couple of weeks have shown that we plan to "solve" that problem by pushing the burden of failures onto the victims, so that the companies can stop caring about fussy things like quality and reputation.

{% embed https://knowablemagazine.org/docserver/fulltext/tunneling-nanotubes-1600x600.jpg|A thread spanning between two cells|false| %}

<i class="fas fa-square"></i> [Tiny, threadlike connections act as ‘skyways’ between cells](https://knowablemagazine.org/content/article/health-disease/2026/role-of-tunneling-nanotubes-in-health-and-disease) from Knowable Magazine @KnowableMag@mstdn.science

> The results can be transformative. An exhausted cell can get mitochondria from a healthy cell, restoring its energy. Cells can transmit informational molecules like RNA that change how the receiving cells behave. Tunneling nanotubes essentially act as long-distance conduits that directly pass important resources from one cell to another. “I liken them to skyways that connect buildings in cities,” says Emil Lou, an oncologist at the University of Minnesota.

I don't see it in the article, but if we find these tubes in common across organisms, then it might answer one of the bigger unsolved questions in early biology, of how collections of unicellular organisms could have become multicellular organisms.
